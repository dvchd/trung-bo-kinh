- **EN**: Then Mahānāma the Sakyan went up to the Buddha, bowed, sat down to one side, and said to him,
- **NOTE**: Mahānāma was the brother of Anuruddha and Ānanda (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd17/en/sujato#1.1.3'>Kd 17:1.1.3</a>) which, according to the commentary, makes the Buddha his cousin. However, the commentarial claim that he was a once-returner is supported by passages such as <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn55.22/en/sujato#2.3'>SN 55.22:2.3</a> and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn55.21/en/sujato#3.6'>SN 55.21:3.6</a> that suggest he was indeed a noble disciple.

## mn14:4.1  `cck12.168, csp1ed9.127, csp2ed9.127, dr12.162, ms9M_570, msdiv177, ndp9.127, nya4, sya12.180, vri12.127`
- **PLI**: ‘Appassādā kāmā bahudukkhā bahupāyāsā, ādīnavo ettha bhiyyo’ti—
- **EN**: Sensual pleasures give little gratification and much suffering and distress, and they are all the more full of drawbacks.

## mn14:4.2
- **PLI**: iti cepi, mahānāma, ariyasāvakassa yathābhūtaṁ sammappaññāya sudiṭṭhaṁ hoti, so ca aññatreva kāmehi aññatra akusalehi dhammehi pītisukhaṁ nādhigacchati, aññaṁ vā tato santataraṁ;
- **EN**: So, Mahānāma, even though a noble disciple has clearly seen this with right wisdom, as long as they do not achieve the rapture and bliss that are apart from sensual pleasures and unskillful qualities, or something even more peaceful than that,
- **NOTE**: The “rapture and bliss that are apart from sensual pleasures and unskillful qualities” includes the first and second <i lang='pi' translate='no'>jhānas</i>. The text is in present tense: they <em>do not attain</em> <i lang='pi' translate='no'>jhāna</i>, not <em>they have never attained</em> <i lang='pi' translate='no'>jhāna</i>. In order to realize the noble truths, Mahānāma must, of course, have practiced the noble eightfold path, which includes <i lang='pi' translate='no'>jhāna</i>. Indeed, at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an3.73/en/sujato#1.6'>AN 3.73:1.6</a> Mahānāma said he understood that in the Buddha’s teaching, “Knowledge is for those with immersion, not those without immersion.” However, a stream-enterer and a once-returner have not fully given up the underlying attachment to sensual pleasures, so unless they are dedicated to regular meditation following that realization, it is possible for their mental clarity to deteriorate and sensual desires to return. It seems that the temptations and business of the lay life had distracted Mahānāma from his meditation.
