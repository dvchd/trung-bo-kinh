## mn128:15.1  `dr13.275, ms11M_653, msdiv241, nya15, sya14.302`
- **PLI**: “Sādhu sādhu, anuruddhā.

## mn128:15.2
- **PLI**: Atthi pana vo, anuruddhā, evaṁ appamattānaṁ ātāpīnaṁ pahitattānaṁ viharataṁ uttari manussadhammā alamariyañāṇadassanaviseso adhigato phāsuvihāro”ti?
- **EN**: But as you live diligently like this, have you achieved any superhuman distinction in knowledge and vision worthy of the noble ones, a comfortable meditation?”
- **NOTE**: As at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn31/en/sujato#10.3'>MN 31:10.3</a>ff., Anuruddha must be prodded to reveal the full extent of his meditation prowess. The form of both suttas parallels Yājñavalkya’s reticence to answer Janaka. In addition, the theme also echoes the Upaniṣad, for Janaka asks what is a person’s light (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>kiṁjyotirayaṁ puruṣa</i>, Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 4.3.2). Yājñavalkya answers that it is simply the sun. When pressed further, he admits it might be the moon, then fire, or even speech, for you can make it home in the dark when someone is calling. All these things—sun, moon, fire, speech—are worshiped in the Vedic tradition as external manifestations of divinity. He only reluctantly admits that a person’s true light is their Self, made of consciousness, the light in the heart. This is the same Self referred to above in the phrase, “whose nature is to desire only the self”. | The Vedantic commentator Śaṅkara, on a verse of the same chapter (4.3.7), discusses at length Buddhist objections to his doctrine. Thus the Buddhist text looks back to the Upaniṣad, while the commentary to that same Upaniṣad looks to Buddhism.

## mn128:15.3
- **PLI**: “Idha mayaṁ, bhante, appamattā ātāpino pahitattā viharantā obhāsañceva sañjānāma dassanañca rūpānaṁ.
- **EN**: “Well, sir, while meditating diligent, keen, and resolute, we perceive both light and vision of forms.
- **NOTE**: The key terms here are “perceive” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>sañjānāma</i>), “light” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>obhāsa</i>), and “vision of forms” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>dassanañca rūpānaṁ</i>). Variations of these are common terms for the visions seen in meditation (eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn9/en/sujato#14.2'>DN 9:14.2</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn77/en/sujato#22.2'>MN 77:22.2</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn121/en/sujato#5.4'>MN 121:5.4</a>). The “light” is the radiance that indicates freedom from defilements, while the “forms” may be the various phenomena that one in such a meditation might see. In particular this refers to the power of clairvoyance (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>dibbacakkhu</i>), in which Anuruddha was later declared foremost (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/an1.192/en/sujato#1.1'>AN 1.192:1.1</a>). See the discussion in the previous sutta (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn127/en/sujato#10.1'>MN 127:10.1</a>) and also eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn32/en/sujato#6.6'>MN 32:6.6</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn9.6/en/sujato#1.2'>SN 9.6:1.2</a> <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an3.130/en/sujato#1.3'>AN 3.130:1.3</a>, and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an8.46/en/sujato#1.3'>AN 8.46:1.3</a>.

## mn128:15.4
- **PLI**: So kho pana no obhāso nacirasseva antaradhāyati dassanañca rūpānaṁ;
- **EN**: But before long the light and the vision of forms vanish.
