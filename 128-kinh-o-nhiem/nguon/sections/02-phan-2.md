## mn128:7.1  `bj12.348, cck14.278, dr13.271, ms11M_648, msdiv238, nya7, pts-vp-pli3.155, sya14.298`
- **PLI**: Atha kho bhagavā ṭhitakova imā gāthā bhāsitvā yena bālakaloṇakāragāmo tenupasaṅkami.
- **EN**: After speaking these verses while standing, the Buddha went to the village of the child salt-miners,
- **NOTE**: “Child salt-miners” is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>bālakaloṇakāra</i> (cf. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>loṇakāradārako</i> at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an4.188/en/sujato#5.3'>AN 4.188:5.3</a>). Children are used to mine salt in India to this day.

## mn128:7.2
- **PLI**: Tena kho pana samayena āyasmā bhagu bālakaloṇakāragāme viharati.
- **EN**: where Venerable Bhagu was staying at the time.
- **NOTE**: Bhagu was a leading Sakyan who went forth along with Bhaddiya, Anuruddha, Ānanda, Kimbila, Devadatta, and Upāli (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd17/en/sujato#1.4.1'>Kd 17:1.4.1</a>). He is known from his ardent striving described in his verses at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/thag4.2/en/sujato'>Thag 4.2</a>, and is listed with other renowned mendicants at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn68/en/sujato#2.2'>MN 68:2.2</a>. | After the conflict at Kosambī, the Buddha sought the company of his fellow Sakyans; first Bhagu, then the three Sakyans below. | Another Bhagu was one of the ancient Vedic seers, while yet another was a contemporary of Sāṇavāsī around the time of the Second Council (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd8/en/sujato#24.6.6'>Kd 8:24.6.6</a>).

- **EN**: Then the Buddha educated, encouraged, fired up, and inspired Bhagu with a Dhamma talk, after which he rose from his seat and set out for the Park of the East-Raftered Hall.

## mn128:8.1  `ms11M_649, ndp11.224, nya8, vri14.195`
- **PLI**: Tena kho pana samayena āyasmā ca anuruddho āyasmā ca nandiyo āyasmā ca kimilo pācīnavaṁsadāye viharanti.
- **EN**: Now at that time the venerables Anuruddha, Nandiya, and Kimbila were staying in the Park of the East-Raftered Hall.
- **NOTE**: This park was in Ceti, not far from Kosambī. A Prācīnavaṁśa was a hall or shed built for the ritual, with bamboo (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>vaṁśa</i>) rafters running easterly (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>prācīna</i>; Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 3.1.1.6 and Eggeling’s note). | Apart from the setting, the following passage is identical with <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn31/en/sujato#2.1'>MN 31:2.1</a>ff., down as far as the discussion on meditation; see that sutta for notes. The current sutta is the original narrative context (see note on <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn31/en/sujato#6.1'>MN 31:6.1</a>).

## mn128:8.2
- **PLI**: Addasā kho dāyapālo bhagavantaṁ dūratova āgacchantaṁ.
- **EN**: The park keeper saw the Buddha coming off in the distance
- **NOTE**: The park keeper was evidently not Buddhist, as he was responsible for a Vedic ritual site.

## mn128:8.3
- **PLI**: Disvāna bhagavantaṁ etadavoca:
- **EN**: and said to the Buddha,

## mn128:8.4
- **PLI**: “mā, mahāsamaṇa, etaṁ dāyaṁ pāvisi.
- **EN**: “Don’t come into this park, ascetic.
- **NOTE**: MS and BJT read <i lang='pi' translate='no'>mahāsamaṇa</i> here, whereas PTS and parallel passages at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn31/en/sujato#3.4'>MN 31:3.4</a> and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd10/en/sujato#4.2.3'>Kd 10:4.2.3</a> have simply <i lang='pi' translate='no'>samaṇa</i>. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Mahāsamaṇa</i> (“great ascetic”) is a rare epithet of the Buddha found mainly in the Vinaya. It is unlikely the park keeper would have kept him out knowing who he really was.

## mn128:8.5
- **PLI**: Santettha tayo kulaputtā attakāmarūpā viharanti.
- **EN**: There are three gentlemen staying here whose nature is to desire self-knowledge.

## mn128:8.6
- **PLI**: Mā tesaṁ aphāsumakāsī”ti.
- **EN**: Do not disturb them.”
