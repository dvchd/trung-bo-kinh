## mn128:31.4
- **PLI**: So kho ahaṁ, anuruddhā, savitakkampi savicāraṁ samādhiṁ bhāvesiṁ, avitakkampi vicāramattaṁ samādhiṁ bhāvesiṁ, avitakkampi avicāraṁ samādhiṁ bhāvesiṁ, sappītikampi samādhiṁ bhāvesiṁ, nippītikampi samādhiṁ bhāvesiṁ, sātasahagatampi samādhiṁ bhāvesiṁ, upekkhāsahagatampi samādhiṁ bhāvesiṁ.
- **EN**: I developed immersion while placing the mind and keeping it connected; without placing the mind, merely keeping it connected; without placing the mind or keeping it connected; with rapture; without rapture; with pleasure; with equanimity.
- **NOTE**: This threefold presentation of the process of absorption focuses on <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vitakka</i> (“placing the mind”) and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vicāra</i> (“keeping it connected”), looking closely at how they cease (see also <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn33/en/sujato#1.10.121'>DN 33:1.10.121</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn34/en/sujato#1.4.7'>DN 34:1.4.7</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn43.3/en/sujato#1.2'>SN 43.3:1.2</a>, and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an8.63/en/sujato#3.1'>AN 8.63:3.1</a>). The standard <i lang='pi' translate='no'>jhāna</i> formula focuses more on the refinement of feelings, from which perspective the first two stages of immersion here fall under the “rapture and bliss born of seclusion”, while only the third qualifies as “rapture and bliss born of immersion”. Thus Analayo describes the two descriptions as “complementary perspectives on the same process of deepening concentration” (<em>Comparative Study</em>, vol. ii, pg. 739, note 263).
