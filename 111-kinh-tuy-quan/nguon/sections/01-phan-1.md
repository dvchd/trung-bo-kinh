## mn111:1.1  `bj12.134, csp1ed11.75, csp2ed11.75, dr13.106, ms11M_243, msdiv93, ndp11.88, nya1, pts-vp-pli3.25, sya14.116, vri14.74`
- **PLI**: Evaṁ me sutaṁ—
- **EN**: So I have heard.
- **NOTE**: This discourse describes Sāriputta’s practice of discernment leading to awakening. It fills in the blanks between realizing stream entry and arahantship, reminding us that when a narrative depicts a person realizing the truth while listening to a teaching—as Sāriputta does—there is usually an extensive background of practice behind that. A number of indications suggest that this is among the later discourses: there are no parallels; the exposition is unusually elaborate; there is editorial clumsiness; and a number of terms are unique or characteristic of later texts.

## mn111:4.1  `nya4`
- **PLI**: Ye ca paṭhame jhāne dhammā vitakko ca vicāro ca pīti ca sukhañca cittekaggatā ca, phasso vedanā saññā cetanā cittaṁ chando adhimokkho vīriyaṁ sati upekkhā manasikāro—tyāssa dhammā anupadavavatthitā honti.
- **EN**: And he distinguished the phenomena of the first absorption one by one: placing and keeping and rapture and bliss and unification of mind; contact, feeling, perception, intention, mind, enthusiasm, decision, energy, mindfulness, equanimity, and application of mind.
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Vavatthita</i> (“distinguished”) occurs elsewhere in the suttas in only one late verse (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn20/en/sujato#6.10'>DN 20:6.10</a>). The Vinaya gives it as an antonym to <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sambhinna</i>, “mixed” (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd13/en/sujato#33.1.7'>Kd 13:33.1.7</a>). | The first five items here are the five so-called “jhāna factors”, which in the suttas appear here and at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn43/en/sujato#19.3'>MN 43:19.3</a>. The list summarizes the normal depiction of the first absorption, as the first four factors are all part of the standard first absorption formula, and all absorption or <i lang='pi' translate='no'>samādhi</i> is characterized by unification of mind (eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn20/en/sujato#3.3'>MN 20:3.3</a>). | There is a striking syntactic break between the first five factors and the remainder of the list starting with “contact”. The five factors are articulated in the colloquial Pali fashion connected with <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ca</i>, while the remainder are simply listed without <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ca</i>. This, together with the fact that the first five appear elsewhere as a set and the remainder do not, as well as the fact that the second list is partly redundant (eg. “feeling” is already covered in the five factors), makes it certain that the latter items were appended to the first five. Indeed, the appended items appear elaborated even compared with similar lists in the Abhidhamma (eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dt1.2/en/sujato#16.1'>Dt 1.2:16.1</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/kv15.10/en/sujato#2.3'>Kv 15.10:2.3</a>; see too <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mil3.3.6/en/sujato#7.2'>Mil 3.3.6:7.2</a>).

## mn111:4.2
- **PLI**: Tyāssa dhammā viditā uppajjanti, viditā upaṭṭhahanti, viditā abbhatthaṁ gacchanti.
- **EN**: He knew those phenomena as they arose, as they remained, and as they went away.
- **NOTE**: He was mindfully aware as the attainment characterized by these qualities arose, persisted, and fell away. As Anālayo points out, this cannot refer to observing the cessation of phenomena <em>inside</em> the absorption, as the absorption itself is defined by their persistence (<em>Early Buddhist Meditation Studies</em>, p. 117ff.). In other words, he was aware of the impermanence <em>of</em> absorption, not of impermanence <em>in</em> absorption. | Compare the “development of immersion” that contemplates feelings, perceptions, and thoughts in the same way (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/an4.41/en/sujato#4.3'>AN 4.41:4.3</a>). According to the suttas, for all conditioned phenomena, “arising is evident, vanishing is evident, and change while persisting is evident” (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/an3.47/en/sujato#1.3'>AN 3.47:1.3</a>).

## mn111:4.3
- **PLI**: So evaṁ pajānāti:
- **EN**: He understood:

## mn111:4.4
- **PLI**: ‘evaṁ kirame dhammā ahutvā sambhonti, hutvā paṭiventī’ti.
- **EN**: ‘So it seems that these phenomena, not having been, come to be; and having come to be, they flit away.’
- **NOTE**: In the previous passage he <em>observed</em> them, whereas now he <em>reflects</em> on what he has seen. This course of practice anticipates the Niddesa’s notion of three kinds of full knowledge (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/cnd11/en/sujato#51.4'>Cnd 11:51.4</a>). First comes “full knowledge of the known” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>ñātapariññā</i>), where the meditator clearly observes each phenomena in experience. Then they reflect and scrutinize the “known” in light of the three characteristics of impermanence, suffering, and not-self, as Sāriputta is doing here (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>tīraṇapariññā</i>). Finally they let go all attachments (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>pahānapariññā</i>), as Sāriputta does below. | “Flit away” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>paṭiventi</i>) is unique.

## mn111:4.5
- **PLI**: So tesu dhammesu anupāyo anapāyo anissito appaṭibaddho vippamutto visaṁyutto vimariyādīkatena cetasā viharati.
- **EN**: Regarding those phenomena, he meditated without getting involved or shying away, independent, untied, liberated, detached, with mind free of boundaries.
- **NOTE**: “Without getting involved or shying away” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>anupāyo anapāyo</i>) is explained by the commentary as not being swayed by the power of lust or aversion. It means having a poised and unreactive attitude. It is a rare phrase that also occurs, with the same syntactic context, in the next sutta, creating a link between the two (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn112/en/sujato#4.2'>MN 112:4.2</a>).

## mn111:4.6
- **PLI**: So ‘atthi uttari nissaraṇan’ti pajānāti.
- **EN**: He understood: ‘There is an escape beyond.’
- **NOTE**: The “escape beyond” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>uttari nissaraṇaṁ</i>) normally refers to full awakening (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn7/en/sujato#17.1'>MN 7:17.1</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn49/en/sujato#2.3'>MN 49:2.3</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an3.66/en/sujato#46.6'>AN 3.66:46.6</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an7.56/en/sujato#6.1'>AN 7.56:6.1</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an10.93/en/sujato#14.4'>AN 10.93:14.4</a>), but the commentary says that here it refers to the next higher attainment.

## mn111:4.7
- **PLI**: Tabbahulīkārā atthitvevassa hoti.
- **EN**: Repeated practice of that confirmed this for him.
- **NOTE**: The compound <i lang='pi' translate='no'>atthitvevassa</i> resolves to: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>atthi iti</i> (“that it is real”) <i lang='pi' translate='no'>eva</i> (“indeed”) <i lang='pi' translate='no'>assa</i> (“for him”). The commentary glosses, “That it is real is reinforced for that monk.”
