## mn70:2.1  `nya2`
- **PLI**: “ahaṁ kho, bhikkhave, aññatreva rattibhojanā bhuñjāmi.
- **EN**: “Mendicants, I abstain from eating at night.
- **NOTE**: While I have not been able to trace a Jain rule regarding eating in the afternoon, not eating at night (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>rattibhojanā</i>) was a standard practice of Jain ascetics (Uttarādhyayana 19.30, 13.2; Dasaveyāliya 4.6, 6.26; Sūyagaḍa 1.2.3.3). It was adopted early into the Gradual Training (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn27/en/sujato#13.9'>MN 27:13.9</a>). The failure of mendicants to comply led to the laying down of a formal Vinaya rule (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-bu-vb-pc37/en/sujato'>Bu Pc 37</a>). But some came to regret their objections, recognizing that the Buddha had acted for their welfare (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn66/en/sujato#6.4'>MN 66:6.4</a>). | The related practice of eating in one sitting is, by contrast, not required in the Vinaya, but was encouraged (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn21/en/sujato#7.4'>MN 21:7.4</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn65/en/sujato#2.1'>MN 65:2.1</a>).

- **EN**: Why should we give up what is apparent in the present to chase after what takes effect over time?
- **NOTE**: They are twisting the meaning of well-known properties of the Dhamma, which is “apparent in the present” and “immediately effective” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>sandiṭṭhiko akāliko</i>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn7/en/sujato#6.2'>MN 7:6.2</a>). The fallacy is that it is sensual pleasures that take time—for they bind you to rebirth—whereas the Dhamma may be realized in this life.

- **EN**: We shall eat at dusk, at daybreak, and at the wrong time of day.”
