## mn22:31.1  `bj10.348, dr12.248, ms9M_821, nya31`
- **PLI**: Kathañca, bhikkhave, bhikkhu ukkhittapaligho hoti?
- **EN**: And how has a mendicant raised the cross-bar?
- **NOTE**: Ignorance is a “hindrance” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>nīvaraṇa</i>), an image that ultimately draws on the Vedic myth of Vṛtra, a giant “constrictor” who trapped the waters (and sometimes cattle and sun) in darkness until it was slain by Indra and the waters released. Removing the bar permits escape from entrapment. At Chāndogya Upaniṣad 2.24.6, it is said that a devoted sacrificer may, at the time of death, pray that the cross-bar blocking entry to the next world be removed.

- **EN**: It’s when a mendicant has given up ignorance, cut it off at the root, made it like a palm stump, obliterated it, so it’s unable to arise in the future.

- **NOTE**: This expression conveys a deep sense of wonder when faced with a state of meditative consciousness so profound that its “basis” is unknown (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/an11.9/en/sujato#3.15'>AN 11.9:3.15</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn22.79/en/sujato#14.26'>SN 22.79:14.26</a>). All such dependencies have been uprooted. | For the phrase <i lang='pi' translate='no'>saindā devā sabrahmakā sapajāpatikā</i> compare <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sa indraḥ sa prajāpatis tad brahma</i> (Kauṣītaki Brāhmaṇa 8.4.15) or <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sa indralokam sa prajāpatilokam sa brahmalokam</i> (Śāṅkhāyana Āraṇyaka 3.3).
