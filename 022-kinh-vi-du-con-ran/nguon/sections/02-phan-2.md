## mn22:7.3
- **PLI**: api nāyaṁ ariṭṭho bhikkhu gaddhabādhipubbo usmīkatopi imasmiṁ dhammavinaye”ti?
- **EN**: Has this mendicant Ariṭṭha kindled even a spark of ardor in this teaching and training?”
- **NOTE**: “Kindled even a spark of ardor” renders <i lang='pi' translate='no'>usmīkatopi</i>, found in a similar context at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn38/en/sujato#6.3'>MN 38:6.3</a>. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Usmā</i> elsewhere appears as bodily warmth (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn43/en/sujato#22.9'>MN 43:22.9</a>, cf. Brahmasūtra 4.2.11), or the initial heating of fire-sticks when rubbed together (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn140/en/sujato#19.13'>MN 140:19.13</a>). But the most pertinent context is <a href='https://suttacentral.net/ja526/en/sujato#55.4'>Ja 526:55.4</a>, where a young ascetic will swiftly lose their <i lang='pi' translate='no'>usmāgataṁ</i>—explained by the commentary as “the fire of an ascetic” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>samaṇatejaṁ</i>)—should they fall prey to sensual temptation. Thus, drawing on the traditional imagery of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>tapas</i> as heat and fervor, it refers to the kindling of ascetic ardor. It never became a technical term in Pali, but in Sanskrit Abhidharma, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>uṣmagata</i> refers to the conjunction of radiant <i lang='pi' translate='no'>samādhi</i> with wisdom in the initial realization of the truths, which “burns up” the defilements (Abhidharmakoṣabhāṣya 6.17, Abhidharmasamuccaya 2.4).

## mn22:10.1  `bj10.334, ms9M_786, msdiv238, nya10, sya12.268, vri12.187`
- **PLI**: Idha, bhikkhave, ekacce moghapurisā dhammaṁ pariyāpuṇanti—
- **EN**: Take a futile person who memorizes the teaching—

## mn22:10.2
- **PLI**: suttaṁ, geyyaṁ, veyyākaraṇaṁ, gāthaṁ, udānaṁ, itivuttakaṁ, jātakaṁ, abbhutadhammaṁ, vedallaṁ.
- **EN**: statements, mixed prose & verse, discussions, verses, inspired exclamations, legends, stories of past lives, amazing stories, and elaborations.
- **NOTE**: These nine categories (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>aṅga</i>) of the teaching were an early organization of the Dhamma before the system of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nikāyas</i> (or <i lang='pi' translate='no'>āgamas</i>) was introduced at the First Council. While their exact specification is uncertain, in my view they are most likely as follows (with an example of each). <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Sutta</i> is short doctrinal statements (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn12.1/en/sujato'>SN 12.1</a>). <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Geyya</i> is mixed prose and verse (“prosimetra”, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn1.1/en/sujato'>SN 1.1</a>). <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Veyyākaraṇa</i> is questions and answers (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn22/en/sujato'>MN 22</a>). <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Gāthā</i> is pure verse (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/thig1.1/en/sujato'>Thig 1.1</a>). <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Udāna</i> is the inspired statements identified as such in the early texts (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn75/en/sujato#19.1'>MN 75:19.1</a>). <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Itivuttaka</i> perhaps means “legends of the past” (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn27/en/sujato'>DN 27</a>) rather than the book of that name (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/iti1/en/sujato'>Iti 1</a>). <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Jātaka</i> are the past life stories of the Buddha found in the early texts (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn81/en/sujato'>MN 81</a>). <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Abbhutadhamma</i> are stories of the amazing qualities of the Buddha or disciples (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn123/en/sujato'>MN 123</a>). <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Vedalla</i> are detailed analytical elaborations (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn43/en/sujato'>MN 43</a>). Once the system of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>aṅgas</i> fell into disuse, some names were repurposed as specific books (Udāna, Itivuttaka, Jātaka). Northern traditions, including the parallels to this passage, usually extend the list to twelve with the addition of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nidāna</i> (background stories), <i lang='pi' translate='no'>apadāna</i> (past lives of disciples), and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>upadesa</i> (explanatory treatises).
