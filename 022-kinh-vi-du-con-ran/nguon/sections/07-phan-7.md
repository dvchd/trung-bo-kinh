## mn22:37.5
- **PLI**: Pubbe cāhaṁ, bhikkhave, etarahi ca dukkhañceva paññāpemi, dukkhassa ca nirodhaṁ.
- **EN**: In the past, as today, what I describe is suffering and the cessation of suffering.
- **NOTE**: The particle <i lang='pi' translate='no'>c’eva</i> here has its normal role of slight emphasis in conjunctions, and so this phrase does not mean, “I <em>only</em> teach suffering and the end of suffering”. The point, rather, is that when he speaks of cessation, it is the cessation of suffering, not the cessation of an “existing being”. This saying is also at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn22.86/en/sujato#13.2'>SN 22.86:13.2</a> and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn44.2/en/sujato#17.13'>SN 44.2:17.13</a>.

## mn22:38.3
- **PLI**: ‘yaṁ kho idaṁ pubbe pariññātaṁ tattha me evarūpā kārā karīyantī’ti.
- **EN**: ‘They do such things for me regarding what in the past was completely understood.’
- **NOTE**: Read <i lang='pi' translate='no'>tattha me</i> “regarding that for me” rather than commentary’s <i lang='pi' translate='no'>tattha (i)me</i> “regarding that for these [aggregates]”. The latter reading is supported by BJT’s <i lang='pi' translate='no'>tatrime</i>, but I think this is probably a backreading from the commentary. In the parallel phrase for the monks below, we would expect <i lang='pi' translate='no'>tattha no</i>, which is in fact attested in the Thai and PTS editions, so I translate “us” accordingly.

## mn22:39.4
- **PLI**: ‘yaṁ kho idaṁ pubbe pariññātaṁ, tattha me evarūpā kārā karīyantī’ti.
- **EN**: ‘They do such things for us regarding what in the past was completely understood.’
- **NOTE**: This seems out of place here, for “completely understood” refers to arahantship, yet as a general teaching this should include everyone. In the Chinese parallel at MA 200, after being abused, one reflects that it is due to past deeds, and after being praised, one reflects that it is due to present knowledge and elimination of defilements. Anālayo (<em>Comparative Study</em>, pp. 56–7) suggests that the Pali phrase <i lang='pi' translate='no'>pubbe pariññātaṁ</i> might be a conflation of these two ideas due to a textual confusion which also left the Pali without a reflection on being abused. While this must remain conjectural, it is a neat explanation for the several doctrinal and textual problems of this passage.
