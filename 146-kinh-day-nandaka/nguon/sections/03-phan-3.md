- **EN**: That’s how it is for a noble disciple who truly sees with right wisdom.

## mn146:9.1  `cck14.445, csp1ed11.317, csp2ed11.317, dr13.447, ms11M_1167, msdiv402, ndp11.365, nya9, pts-vp-pli3.274, sya14.491, vri14.327`
- **PLI**: Seyyathāpi, bhaginiyo, telappadīpassa jhāyato telampi aniccaṁ vipariṇāmadhammaṁ, vaṭṭipi aniccā vipariṇāmadhammā, accipi aniccā vipariṇāmadhammā, ābhāpi aniccā vipariṇāmadhammā.
- **EN**: Suppose there was an oil lamp burning. The oil, wick, flame, and light were all impermanent and perishable.
- **NOTE**: While the imagery of the dependence of the flame on its fuel is common in the suttas, this exact metaphor is not found elsewhere. Normally the Buddha employs metaphors in an ABA structure: he explains the doctrine, illustrates it, and repeats the explanation. Here, however, the metaphor acts as a springboard for the next stage of the argument, the impermanence of feelings.

- **EN**: Because that oil lamp’s oil, wick, and flame are all impermanent and perishable,
