## mn136:2.6

## mn136:2.7

- **EN**: “Don’t say that, Reverend Potaliputta, don’t say that! Don’t misrepresent the Buddha, for misrepresentation of the Buddha is not good. And the Buddha would not say this:

## mn136:2.8
- **PLI**: ‘moghaṁ kāyakammaṁ moghaṁ vacīkammaṁ, manokammameva saccan’ti.
- **EN**: ‘Deeds by way of body and speech are done in vain. Only mental deeds are real.’
- **NOTE**: There is indeed no such statement in the suttas.

## mn136:2.9

- **EN**: But, reverend, there is such an attainment where the one who enters it does not feel anything at all.”
- **NOTE**: This is the cessation of perception and feelings.

## mn136:2.10

## mn136:2.11

- **EN**: “Not long, reverend: three years.”

## mn136:2.12

- **EN**: “Well now, what are we to say to the senior mendicants, when even such a junior mendicant imagines their Teacher needs defending?

## mn136:2.13
- **PLI**: Sañcetanikaṁ, āvuso samiddhi, kammaṁ katvā kāyena vācāya manasā kiṁ so vediyatī”ti?
- **EN**: After doing an intentional deed by way of body, speech, or mind, reverend, what does one feel?”

## mn136:2.14

- **EN**: “After doing an intentional deed by way of body, speech, or mind, reverend, one feels suffering.”
- **NOTE**: It is this answer of Samiddhi’s that is criticized below.

## mn136:2.15

## mn136:2.16

- **EN**: Still, who are those foolish and incompetent wanderers of other religions to understand the Realized One’s great analysis of deeds?

## mn136:6.15
- **PLI**: Sace tumhe, ānanda, suṇeyyātha tathāgatassa mahākammavibhaṅgaṁ vibhajantassā”ti.
- **EN**: If you would all listen, Ānanda, I will explain the Realized One’s great analysis of deeds.”
- **NOTE**: Text here reads <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vibhajantassa</i>, which appears ungrammatical. This phrase is shared with <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an6.62/en/sujato#5.8'>AN 6.62:5.8</a>, which is also a case where a junior monk has made a silly statement. There the Pali has <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vibhajissāmi</i> (“I will explain”). The commentary does not explicitly comment on this phrase, but it does say that the Buddha thought, “I will make it clear for the mendicant Saṅgha” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>bhikkhusaṅghassa pākaṭaṁ karissāmīti</i>), which implies that it was reading the future tense. Compare the use of future tense in the stock phrase, “Listen and apply your mind well, I will speak” (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn138/en/sujato#2.2'>MN 138:2.2</a>).
