## mn115:2.1  `csp1ed11.107, csp2ed11.107, ms11M_349, nya2`
- **PLI**: “Yāni kānici, bhikkhave, bhayāni uppajjanti sabbāni tāni bālato uppajjanti, no paṇḍitato;
- **EN**: “Whatever dangers there are, all come from the foolish, not from the astute.
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Paṇḍita</i> is normally used for a learned scholar, rather than a contemplative “sage”. Pali has many more terms for “a wise person” than does English, and it is not easy to distinguish the senses. “Astute” is meant to convey the sense of one who is observant and of sound judgment.

## mn115:2.2
- **PLI**: ye keci upaddavā uppajjanti sabbe te bālato uppajjanti, no paṇḍitato;
- **EN**: Whatever perils there are, all come from the foolish, not from the astute.

## mn115:2.3
- **PLI**: ye keci upasaggā uppajjanti sabbe te bālato uppajjanti, no paṇḍitato.
- **EN**: Whatever hazards there are, all come from the foolish, not from the astute.

## mn115:2.4
- **PLI**: Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, naḷāgārā vā tiṇāgārā vā aggi mutto kūṭāgārānipi dahati ullittāvalittāni nivātāni phusitaggaḷāni pihitavātapānāni;
- **EN**: It’s like a fire that spreads from a hut made of reeds or grass, and burns down even a bungalow, plastered inside and out, draft-free, with doors fastened and windows shuttered.

## mn115:2.5
- **PLI**: evameva kho, bhikkhave, yāni kānici bhayāni uppajjanti sabbāni tāni bālato uppajjanti, no paṇḍitato;
- **EN**: In the same way, whatever dangers there are, all come from the foolish, not from the astute.

## mn115:2.6
- **PLI**: ye keci upaddavā uppajjanti sabbe te bālato uppajjanti, no paṇḍitato;
- **EN**: Whatever perils there are, all come from the foolish, not from the astute.

## mn115:2.7
- **PLI**: ye keci upasaggā uppajjanti sabbe te bālato uppajjanti, no paṇḍitato.
- **EN**: Whatever hazards there are, all come from the foolish, not from the astute.

## mn115:2.8
- **PLI**: Iti kho, bhikkhave, sappaṭibhayo bālo, appaṭibhayo paṇḍito;
- **EN**: So, the fool is dangerous, but the astute person is safe.

## mn115:2.9
- **PLI**: saupaddavo bālo, anupaddavo paṇḍito;
- **EN**: The fool is perilous, but the astute person is not.

## mn115:2.10
- **PLI**: saupasaggo bālo, anupasaggo paṇḍito.
- **EN**: The fool is hazardous, but the astute person is not.

## mn115:2.11
- **PLI**: Natthi, bhikkhave, paṇḍitato bhayaṁ, natthi paṇḍitato upaddavo, natthi paṇḍitato upasaggo.
- **EN**: There’s no danger, peril, or hazard that comes from the astute.

## mn115:2.12
- **PLI**: Tasmātiha, bhikkhave, ‘paṇḍitā bhavissāma vīmaṁsakā’ti—
- **EN**: So you should train like this: ‘We shall be astute, we shall be inquirers.’”

## mn115:3.1  `cck14.158, dr13.152, ms11M_350, ndp11.125, nya3, pts-vp-pli3.62, sya14.167`
- **PLI**: Evaṁ vutte, āyasmā ānando bhagavantaṁ etadavoca:
- **EN**: When he said this, Venerable Ānanda said to the Buddha,

## mn115:3.2
- **PLI**: “kittāvatā nu kho, bhante, paṇḍito bhikkhu ‘vīmaṁsako’ti alaṁvacanāyā”ti?
- **EN**: “Sir, how is a mendicant qualified to be called ‘astute, an inquirer’?”
- **NOTE**: Notice the different approaches used by Ānanda and Sāriputta in the previous discourse. Sāriputta took up the Buddha’s teaching and elaborated it, whereas Ānanda uses inquiry to prompt the Buddha himself to speak further.

## mn115:3.3
- **PLI**: “Yato kho, ānanda, bhikkhu dhātukusalo ca hoti, āyatanakusalo ca hoti, paṭiccasamuppādakusalo ca hoti, ṭhānāṭhānakusalo ca hoti—
- **EN**: “Ānanda, it’s when a mendicant is skilled in the elements, in the sense fields, in dependent origination, and in the possible and the impossible.

## mn115:3.4
- **PLI**: ettāvatā kho, ānanda, paṇḍito bhikkhu ‘vīmaṁsako’ti alaṁvacanāyā”ti.
- **EN**: That’s how a mendicant is qualified to be called ‘astute, an inquirer’.”

## mn115:4.1  `ms11M_351, msdiv125, nya4, vri14.109`
- **PLI**: “Kittāvatā pana, bhante, ‘dhātukusalo bhikkhū’ti alaṁvacanāyā”ti?
- **EN**: “But sir, how is a mendicant qualified to be called ‘skilled in the elements’?”
- **NOTE**: As emphasized in this sutta, the notion of an “element” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>dhātu</i>) is a flexible one, including a range of different properties, qualities, or aspects of existence.

## mn115:4.2
- **PLI**: “Aṭṭhārasa kho imā, ānanda, dhātuyo—
- **EN**: “There are, Ānanda, these eighteen elements.
- **NOTE**: These eighteen elements are mentioned only twice elsewhere in the Suttas (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn14.1/en/sujato#2.2'>SN 14.1:2.2</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn41.2/en/sujato#3.10'>SN 41.2:3.10</a>), and hundreds of times in the Abhidhamma, where they are classified specifically as an Abhidhamma teaching (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/vb3/en/sujato#32.1'>Vb 3:32.1</a>).

## mn115:4.3
- **PLI**: cakkhudhātu, rūpadhātu, cakkhuviññāṇadhātu;
- **EN**: The eye element, sight element, and eye consciousness element.

## mn115:4.4
- **PLI**: sotadhātu, saddadhātu, sotaviññāṇadhātu;
- **EN**: The ear element, sound element, and ear consciousness element.

## mn115:4.5
- **PLI**: ghānadhātu, gandhadhātu, ghānaviññāṇadhātu;
- **EN**: The nose element, smell element, and nose consciousness element.

## mn115:4.6
- **PLI**: jivhādhātu, rasadhātu, jivhāviññāṇadhātu;
- **EN**: The tongue element, taste element, and tongue consciousness element.

## mn115:4.7
- **PLI**: kāyadhātu, phoṭṭhabbadhātu, kāyaviññāṇadhātu;
- **EN**: The body element, touch element, and body consciousness element.

## mn115:4.8
- **PLI**: manodhātu, dhammadhātu, manoviññāṇadhātu.
- **EN**: The mind element, ideas element, and mind consciousness element.
- **NOTE**: The “mind element” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>manodhātu</i>) is not defined in the Suttas. See <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn28/en/sujato#37.1'>MN 28:37.1</a> and note there. The Abhidhamma Vibhaṅga says that the mind element arises immediately after a sense impression or engagement with mental phenomena or “ideas” (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/vb3/en/sujato#48.2'>Vb 3:48.2</a>), and the mind consciousness element arises immediately after the corresponding mind element (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/vb3/en/sujato#55.2'>Vb 3:55.2</a>).

## mn115:4.9
- **PLI**: Imā kho, ānanda, aṭṭhārasa dhātuyo yato jānāti passati—
- **EN**: When a mendicant knows and sees these eighteen elements,

## mn115:4.10
- **PLI**: ettāvatāpi kho, ānanda, ‘dhātukusalo bhikkhū’ti alaṁvacanāyā”ti.
- **EN**: they’re qualified to be called ‘skilled in the elements’.”
