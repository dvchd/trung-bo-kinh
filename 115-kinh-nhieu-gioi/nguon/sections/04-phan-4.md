# Phần 4  (6 đoạn)

## mn115:20.2

## mn115:20.3
- **PLI**: Konāmo ayaṁ, bhante, dhammapariyāyo”ti?
- **EN**: What is the name of this exposition of the teaching?”

## mn115:20.4
- **PLI**: “Tasmātiha tvaṁ, ānanda, imaṁ dhammapariyāyaṁ ‘bahudhātuko’tipi naṁ dhārehi, ‘catuparivaṭṭo’tipi naṁ dhārehi, ‘dhammādāso’tipi naṁ dhārehi, ‘amatadundubhī’tipi naṁ dhārehi, ‘anuttaro saṅgāmavijayo’tipi naṁ dhārehī”ti.
- **EN**: “Well then, Ānanda, you may remember this exposition of the teaching as ‘The Many Elements’, or else ‘The Four Rounds’, or else ‘The Mirror of the Teaching’, or else ‘The Drum of Freedom From Death’, or else ‘The Supreme Victory in Battle’.”
- **NOTE**: The “four rounds” are the four main topics (elements, sense fields, dependent origination, the possible and the impossible); elsewhere it is said to be the four noble truths (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn22.56/en/sujato#2.1'>SN 22.56:2.1</a>). | The “mirror of the teaching” is the ability of a noble one to see the truth of the Dhamma in themselves (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn55.8/en/sujato#5.1'>SN 55.8:5.1</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn16/en/sujato#2.8.3'>DN 16:2.8.3</a>). | The “drum of freedom from death” was sounded by the Buddha after his awakening (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn26/en/sujato#25.21'>MN 26:25.21</a>). | The “supreme victory in battle” was also suggested as a title for the Brahmajālasutta (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn1/en/sujato#3.74.3'>DN 1:3.74.3</a>), while elsewhere it is said to be a name for the noble eightfold path (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn45.4/en/sujato#4.3'>SN 45.4:4.3</a>).
