## mn115:10.1  `ms11M_357, msdiv126, nya10, sya14.169`
- **PLI**: “Kittāvatā pana, bhante, ‘āyatanakusalo bhikkhū’ti alaṁvacanāyā”ti?
- **EN**: “But sir, how is a mendicant qualified to be called ‘skilled in the sense fields’?”
- **NOTE**: Ānanda moves on to the next topic, the six senses. Perhaps this move was prompted by the mention of the “unconditioned element” of Nibbāna, signifying that that method of analysis had reached its culmination.

## mn115:10.2

- **EN**: the eye and sights, the ear and sounds, the nose and smells, the tongue and tastes, the body and touches, and the mind and ideas.

## mn115:10.4

## mn115:10.5

## mn115:11.1  `bj12.198, cck14.160, csp1ed11.109, csp2ed11.109, dr13.154, ms11M_358, ndp11.127, nya11, pts-vp-pli3.64, sya14.170`

- **EN**: “But sir, how is a mendicant qualified to be called ‘skilled in dependent origination’?”

## mn115:11.2
- **PLI**: “Idhānanda, bhikkhu evaṁ pajānāti:
- **EN**: “It’s when a mendicant understands:

## mn115:11.3
- **PLI**: ‘imasmiṁ sati idaṁ hoti, imassuppādā idaṁ uppajjati,
- **EN**: ‘When this exists, this comes to be; due to the arising of this, this arises.

## mn115:11.4
- **PLI**: imasmiṁ asati idaṁ na hoti, imassa nirodhā idaṁ nirujjhati, yadidaṁ—
- **EN**: When this doesn’t exist, this doesn’t come to be; due to the cessation of this, this ceases. That is:

## mn115:11.5
- **PLI**: avijjāpaccayā saṅkhārā,
- **EN**: ignorance is a requirement for choices.

## mn115:11.6
- **PLI**: saṅkhārapaccayā viññāṇaṁ,
- **EN**: Choices are a condition for consciousness.

## mn115:11.7
- **PLI**: viññāṇapaccayā nāmarūpaṁ,
- **EN**: Consciousness is a requirement for name and form.

## mn115:11.8
- **PLI**: nāmarūpapaccayā saḷāyatanaṁ,
- **EN**: Name and form are requirements for the six sense fields.

## mn115:11.9
- **PLI**: saḷāyatanapaccayā phasso,
- **EN**: The six sense fields are requirements for contact.

## mn115:11.10
- **PLI**: phassapaccayā vedanā,
- **EN**: Contact is a requirement for feeling.

## mn115:11.11
- **PLI**: vedanāpaccayā taṇhā,
- **EN**: Feeling is a requirement for craving.

## mn115:11.12
- **PLI**: taṇhāpaccayā upādānaṁ,
- **EN**: Craving is a requirement for grasping.

## mn115:11.13
- **PLI**: upādānapaccayā bhavo,
- **EN**: Grasping is a requirement for continued existence.

## mn115:11.14
- **PLI**: bhavapaccayā jāti,
- **EN**: Continued existence is a requirement for rebirth.

## mn115:11.15
- **PLI**: jātipaccayā jarāmaraṇaṁ sokaparidevadukkhadomanassūpāyāsā sambhavanti.

## mn115:11.17

- **EN**: When ignorance fades away and ceases no residue left behind, choices cease.

- **EN**: When choices cease, consciousness ceases.

## mn115:11.19

- **EN**: When consciousness ceases, name and form cease.

## mn115:11.20

- **EN**: When name and form cease, the six sense fields cease.

## mn115:11.21

## mn115:11.22
- **PLI**: phassanirodhā vedanānirodho,
- **EN**: When contact ceases, feeling ceases.

## mn115:11.23

- **EN**: When feeling ceases, craving ceases.

## mn115:11.24

## mn115:11.26

## mn115:11.27

Briefer examples of this trope are found at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn122/en/sujato#3.2'>MN 122:3.2</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn12.30/en/sujato'>SN 12.30</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an6.70/en/sujato'>AN 6.70</a>, and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an6.93/en/sujato'>AN 6.93</a>. ## mn115:12.2
- **PLI**: “Idhānanda, bhikkhu ‘aṭṭhānametaṁ anavakāso yaṁ diṭṭhisampanno puggalo kañci saṅkhāraṁ niccato upagaccheyya, netaṁ ṭhānaṁ vijjatī’ti pajānāti;
- **EN**: “It’s when a mendicant understands: ‘It’s impossible for an individual accomplished in view to take any condition as permanent. That is not possible.
- **NOTE**: A stream-enterer (or above) might unthinkingly act as if something were permanent (etc.), but on reflection they would know right away that this is not the case.

## mn115:12.3
- **PLI**: ‘ṭhānañca kho etaṁ vijjati yaṁ puthujjano kañci saṅkhāraṁ niccato upagaccheyya, ṭhānametaṁ vijjatī’ti pajānāti;
- **EN**: But it’s possible for an ordinary person to take some condition as permanent. That is possible.’
