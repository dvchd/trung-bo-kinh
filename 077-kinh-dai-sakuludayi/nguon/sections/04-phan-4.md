## mn77:15.1  `bj11.348, cck13.325, dr13.299, ms10M_691, msdiv247, nya15`
- **PLI**: Puna caparaṁ, udāyi, akkhātā mayā sāvakānaṁ paṭipadā, yathāpaṭipannā me sāvakā cattāro satipaṭṭhāne bhāventi.
- **EN**: Furthermore, I have explained to my disciples a practice that they use to develop the four kinds of mindfulness meditation.
- **NOTE**: The fifth quality as presented here constitutes one of the most comprehensive presentations of meditation in any early Buddhist text. The Chinese parallel (MA 207 at T i 783b16) simply says that the Buddha taught disciples to transcend doubt and reach the far shore, which makes the same point far more economically. It seems likely that the Pali text has been expanded. | From here through to <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn77/en/sujato#21.3'>MN 77:21.3</a> the Buddha presents the seven sets of practices later renowned as the 37 “qualities on the side of awakening” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>bodhipakkhiyadhammā</i>).

## mn77:15.6
- **PLI**: Tatra ca pana me sāvakā bahū abhiññāvosānapāramippattā viharanti.
- **EN**: And many of my disciples meditate on that having attained perfection and consummation of insight.
- **NOTE**: “Perfection” is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>pāramī</i>, here a term for arahantship. The concept of “perfections” as a set of qualities developed by a Bodhisattva over many lifetimes is not found in any early text. It was introduced several centuries after the Buddha in the Cariyāpiṭaka (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/cp1/en/sujato'>Cp 1</a>) and throughout the latest texts of the Pali canon (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/bv1/en/sujato#78.1'>Bv 1:78.1</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/thi-ap28/en/sujato#64.2'>Thi Ap 28:64.2</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/kp8/en/sujato#15.2'>Kp 8:15.2</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-pvr3/en/sujato#3.2'>Pvr 3:3.2</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mil5.1.4/en/sujato#13.1'>Mil 5.1.4:13.1</a>). Similar conceptions were meanwhile developing in other schools, eventually becoming a key doctrine of Mahāyāna.
