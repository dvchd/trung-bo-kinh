## mn77:2.1  `nya2`

- **EN**: Now at that time several very well-known wanderers were residing in their monastery in the peacocks’ feeding ground.

## mn77:2.2
- **PLI**: annabhāro varadharo sakuludāyī ca paribbājako aññe ca abhiññātā abhiññātā paribbājakā.
- **EN**: They included Annabhāra, Varadhara, Sakuludāyī, and other very well-known wanderers.
- **NOTE**: The Buddha addresses these wanderers at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an4.30/en/sujato'>AN 4.30</a>, where he teaches four fundamental principles of any holy life, and at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an4.185/en/sujato'>AN 4.185</a>, where he teaches four “truths of the brahmins”. These are all respectful engagements that highlight common ground. | Annabhāra means “food-carrier” and indicates a humble origin (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/thag16.9/en/sujato#19.1'>Thag 16.9:19.1</a>). | Varadhara means “bearer of good fortune”; it is probably a name for the earth. | Sakuludāyī also appears in <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn79/en/sujato'>MN 79</a>. His name (“one who rises up with his own family”) is ironic, given that his attempt to go forth is frustrated by his followers at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn79/en/sujato#46.2'>MN 79:46.2</a>.
