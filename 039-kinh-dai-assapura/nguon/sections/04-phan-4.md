## mn39:28.1  `ms9M_1424, nya28, sya12.511`
- **PLI**: Kathañca, bhikkhave, bhikkhu ariyo hoti?
- **EN**: And how is a mendicant a noble one?
- **NOTE**: Both <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ariya</i> and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>arahant</i> are explained with the same word <i lang='pi' translate='no'>āraka</i>, meaning “far from (defilements)”. The commentary gives <i lang='pi' translate='no'>āraka</i> in both cases, but explains <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ariya</i> as “slayer” and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>arahant</i> as “far from”. At <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an7.91/en/sujato'>AN 7.91</a>, PTS has <i lang='pi' translate='no'>arīhatattā</i>, “foe-slayer” for <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ariya</i> (BJT omits, while at least one more variant is attested). This is a slightly better pun for <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ariya</i> than <i lang='pi' translate='no'>āraka</i>, although at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/snp3.6/en/sujato#38.1'>Snp 3.6:38.1</a> <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ariya</i> is punned with <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ālaya</i> (“clinging”), which is better than both. Where both MA 182 and the parallel to <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn40/en/sujato'>MN 40</a> at MA 183 at T i 726c13 mention the <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ariya</i>, the Indic pun underlying EA 49.8 seems to be that a <i lang='pi' translate='no'>kṣatriya</i> is so called because they “end” (Sanskit <i lang='pi' translate='no'>kṣaya</i>) the defilements. Neither of the Chinese parallels (MA 182 at T i 725c4, EA 49.8 at T ii 802b4) mentions the <i lang='pi' translate='no'>arahant</i>, suggesting it was added later, which would explain the duplication of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>āraka</i>. However, given that “far from” is consistently attested for the <i lang='pi' translate='no'>arahant</i> in all editions and commentary; that “foe-slayer” is attested for <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ariya</i> in some texts and commentaries; and that <i lang='pi' translate='no'>kṣatriya</i> in the sense of “warrior” is loosely in the same semantic realm as “foe-slayer”, I translate accordingly.
