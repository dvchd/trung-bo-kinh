## mn39:1.1  `ms9M_1393, msdiv415, ndp9.333, nya1, sya12.496, vri12.343`
- **PLI**: Evaṁ me sutaṁ—
- **EN**: So I have heard.

## mn39:1.2
- **PLI**: ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā aṅgesu viharati assapuraṁ nāma aṅgānaṁ nigamo.
- **EN**: At one time the Buddha was staying in the land of the Aṅgas, near the Aṅgan town named Assapura.
- **NOTE**: Assapura, the “horse fort”, appears to be unknown apart from its mention here and the next sutta. Contra <em>Dictionary of Pali Proper Names</em>, this is not the same Assapura referred to in <a href='https://suttacentral.net/ja422/en/sujato'>Ja 422</a>, as that lay south of Ceti, far to the west. An Aśvapura is mentioned in Jain legends, but there is nothing to link this with the Aṅgan town. | Aṅga lay to the east of Magadha on the Ganges, toward modern Bengal.

- **EN**: “Mendicants!”

## mn39:1.5

## mn39:2.1  `cck12.474, dr12.437, ms9M_1394, nya2`
- **PLI**: “Samaṇā samaṇāti vo, bhikkhave, jano sañjānāti.
- **EN**: “Mendicants, people label you as ascetics.

## mn39:2.2
- **PLI**: Tumhe ca pana ‘ke tumhe’ti puṭṭhā samānā ‘samaṇāmhā’ti paṭijānātha;
- **EN**: And when they ask you what you are, you claim to be ascetics.
- **NOTE**: This sutta deals with the difference between outer perception and inner reality, addressing what we call “imposter syndrome”. The Buddha inspires students to aspire to the highest ideal through the practice of the Gradual Training so that they live up to their labels and claims.

## mn39:2.3
- **PLI**: tesaṁ vo, bhikkhave, evaṁsamaññānaṁ sataṁ evaṁpaṭiññānaṁ sataṁ ‘ye dhammā samaṇakaraṇā ca brāhmaṇakaraṇā ca te dhamme samādāya vattissāma, evaṁ no ayaṁ amhākaṁ samaññā ca saccā bhavissati paṭiññā ca bhūtā.
- **EN**: Given this label and this claim, you should train like this: ‘We will proceed having undertaken the things that make one an ascetic and a brahmin. That way our label will be accurate and our claim correct.

## mn39:2.4
- **PLI**: Yesañca mayaṁ cīvarapiṇḍapātasenāsanagilānappaccayabhesajjaparikkhāraṁ paribhuñjāma, tesaṁ te kārā amhesu mahapphalā bhavissanti mahānisaṁsā, amhākañcevāyaṁ pabbajjā avañjhā bhavissati saphalā saudrayā’ti.
- **EN**: The services of those whose robes, almsfood, lodgings, and medicines and supplies for the sick we enjoy shall be very fruitful and beneficial for them. And our going forth will not be barren, but will be fruitful and fertile.’

## mn39:2.5
- **PLI**: Evañhi vo, bhikkhave, sikkhitabbaṁ.
- **EN**: (Sujato gộp đoạn này vào câu trước — dịch trực tiếp từ PLI)

- **EN**: And what are the things that make one an ascetic and a brahmin?

## mn39:3.2
- **PLI**: ‘Hirottappena samannāgatā bhavissāmā’ti evañhi vo, bhikkhave, sikkhitabbaṁ.
- **EN**: You should train like this: ‘We will have conscience and prudence.’

## mn39:3.3
- **PLI**: Siyā kho pana, bhikkhave, tumhākaṁ evamassa:
- **EN**: Now, mendicants, you might think,

## mn39:3.4
- **PLI**: ‘hirottappenamha samannāgatā, alamettāvatā katamettāvatā, anuppatto no sāmaññattho, natthi no kiñci uttariṁ karaṇīyan’ti tāvatakeneva tuṭṭhiṁ āpajjeyyātha.
- **EN**: ‘We have conscience and prudence. This is sufficient; enough has been done. We have achieved the goal of life as an ascetic. There is nothing more to do.’ And you might rest content with just that much.

## mn39:3.5
- **PLI**: Ārocayāmi vo, bhikkhave, paṭivedayāmi vo, bhikkhave:
- **EN**: I declare this to you, mendicants, I announce this to you:

- **EN**: ‘You who seek to be true ascetics, do not lose sight of the goal of the ascetic life while there is still more to do.’

## mn39:4.2
- **PLI**: ‘Parisuddho no kāyasamācāro bhavissati uttāno vivaṭo na ca chiddavā saṁvuto ca.
- **EN**: You should train like this: ‘Our bodily behavior will be pure, clear, open, neither inconsistent nor secretive.
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Chiddavā</i> (“inconsistent”) is more literally “full of holes”. Its opposite (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>acchidda</i>) is one of the standard descriptors of “impeccable” precepts (eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn48/en/sujato#6.12'>MN 48:6.12</a>). | <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Saṁvuta</i> (past participle of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>saṁvara</i>) can mean either “restrained” or “hidden, secretive”. The sense depends on the syntax; the two instances of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ca</i> split the listed items into three plus two, with the negative distributed over the last two items. This agrees with the commentary, which explains “secretive” as “concealing one’s faults”. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Saṁvara</i> appears in the sense of sense restraint below (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn39/en/sujato#8.4'>MN 39:8.4</a>).
