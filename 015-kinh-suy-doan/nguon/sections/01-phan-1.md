## mn15:1.1  `bj10.228, dr12.170, ms9M_590, msdiv181, ndp9.132, nya1, sya12.189, vri12.133`
- **PLI**: Evaṁ me sutaṁ—
- **EN**: So I have heard.

## mn15:1.2
- **PLI**: ekaṁ samayaṁ āyasmā mahāmoggallāno bhaggesu viharati susumāragire bhesakaḷāvane migadāye.
- **EN**: At one time Venerable Mahāmoggallāna was staying in the land of the Bhaggas at Crocodile’s Bellow, in the deer park at Bhesakaḷā’s Wood.
- **NOTE**: The Bhaggas were an isolated republican clan, sandwiched between the Vacchas south of the Yamuna and the Kosalans north of the Ganges. By the time of the Buddha they were evidently subject to the Vacchas. | “Crocodile’s Bellow” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>susumāragira</i>) was their capital. The commentary says it was so-named because a crocodile bellowed in a nearby lake during the founding of the city.

## mn15:1.3
- **PLI**: Tatra kho āyasmā mahāmoggallāno bhikkhū āmantesi:
- **EN**: There Venerable Mahāmoggallāna addressed the mendicants:
- **NOTE**: While Moggallāna is known for his mastery of advanced psychic abilities, suttas such as this and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn5/en/sujato'>MN 5</a> show his concern for even the smallest defects in ethical conduct.

## mn15:1.4
- **PLI**: “āvuso bhikkhavo”ti.
- **EN**: “Reverends, mendicants!”

## mn15:1.5
- **PLI**: “Āvuso”ti kho te bhikkhū āyasmato mahāmoggallānassa paccassosuṁ.
- **EN**: “Reverend,” they replied.

## mn15:1.6
- **PLI**: Āyasmā mahāmoggallāno etadavoca:
- **EN**: Venerable Mahāmoggallāna said this:

## mn15:2.1  `cck12.177, csp1ed9.133, csp2ed9.133, ms9M_591, nya2`
- **PLI**: “Pavāreti cepi, āvuso, bhikkhu:
- **EN**: “Suppose a mendicant invites
- **NOTE**: The term “invites” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>pavāreti</i>) suggests a connection with the “Invitation” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>pavāraṇā</i>) ceremony at the end of each rains retreat, where the monastics invite each other for admonition (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd4/en/sujato'>Kd 4</a>). The Chinese parallels confirm this connection, saying the discourse was spoken on the Pavāraṇā.

## mn15:2.2
- **PLI**: ‘vadantu maṁ āyasmanto, vacanīyomhi āyasmantehī’ti, so ca hoti dubbaco, dovacassakaraṇehi dhammehi samannāgato, akkhamo appadakkhiṇaggāhī anusāsaniṁ, atha kho naṁ sabrahmacārī na ceva vattabbaṁ maññanti, na ca anusāsitabbaṁ maññanti, na ca tasmiṁ puggale vissāsaṁ āpajjitabbaṁ maññanti.
- **EN**: other mendicants to admonish them. But they’re hard to admonish, having qualities that make them hard to admonish. They’re impatient, and don’t take instruction respectfully. So their spiritual companions don’t think it’s worth advising and instructing them, and that individual doesn’t gain their trust.
- **NOTE**: This discourse acts as a sutta counterpart to the Vinaya rule <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-bu-vb-ss12/en/sujato'>Bu Ss 12</a>, which lays down a procedure to deal with a mendicant who makes themselves incorrigible. The Pali origin story for that rule lays the blame on the monk Channa, the Buddha’s former charioteer, who boasts “mine is the Buddha, mine the Dhamma!” It was evidently unsuccessful, as the Buddha on his deathbed had to further lay down the “divine punishment” for him (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn16/en/sujato#6.4.1'>DN 16:6.4.1</a>).
