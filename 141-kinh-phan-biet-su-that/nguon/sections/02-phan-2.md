## mn141:24.1  `ms11M_1064, ndp11.337, nya24`
- **PLI**: Katamā cāvuso, sammādiṭṭhi?
- **EN**: And what is right view?
- **NOTE**: “View” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>diṭṭhi</i>, Sanskrit <i lang='pi' translate='no'>dṛṣṭi</i>) is “seeing” and hence a term for “understanding” (Aitareya Upaniṣad 3.2). In Buddhism, it means a particular way of seeing or perspective, a theory or view through which one makes sense of the world. By itself it is neutral, although “views” in plural sometimes stands for the thicket of wrong views.

- **EN**: Knowing about suffering, the origin of suffering, the cessation of suffering, and the practice that leads to the cessation of suffering.
- **NOTE**: The fourth noble truth is the path, while the first path factor is the noble truths. These two teachings are different perspectives on the same <i lang='pi' translate='no'>dhamma</i>. | Ignorance is described in the opposite way (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn9/en/sujato#64.1'>MN 9:64.1</a>–66.7, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn12.2/en/sujato#14.2'>SN 12.2:14.2</a>).

## mn141:25.1  `ms11M_1065, nya25, sya14.454`
- **PLI**: Katamo cāvuso, sammāsaṅkappo?
- **EN**: And what is right purpose?
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Saṅkappa</i> is normally a synonym of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vitakka</i> in the suttas, hence it may be rendered “thought” (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn14.8/en/sujato#3.3'>SN 14.8:3.3</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn60/en/sujato#24.2'>MN 60:24.2</a>). It is, however, not just verbalized thought, but the direction in which one applies the mind, hence “intention” (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn41/en/sujato#14.3'>MN 41:14.3</a>), “resolve”, or “purpose”. It has a directionality to it: in Rig Veda 10.164.5 it is the “evil intent” wished upon those who we hate, whereas in Atharva Veda 3.25.2 it is a quality of the arrow of love. For this reason, I usually render it as “purpose”. This factor is the emotional counterpart of right view, ensuring that the path is motivated by love and compassion.

## mn141:25.2
- **PLI**: Nekkhammasaṅkappo, abyāpādasaṅkappo, avihiṁsāsaṅkappo,
- **EN**: Purposes of renunciation, good will, and harmlessness.
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Abyāpāda</i> is “non-ill-will” or simply “good will”.

## mn141:25.3
- **PLI**: ayaṁ vuccatāvuso: ‘sammāsaṅkappo’.
- **EN**: This is called right purpose.
