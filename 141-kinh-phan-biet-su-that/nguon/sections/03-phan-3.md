## mn141:31.1  `ms11M_1071, ndp11.338, nya31, vri14.302`
- **PLI**: Katamo cāvuso, sammāsamādhi?
- **EN**: And what is right immersion?
- **NOTE**: “Immersion” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>samādhi</i>) is treated in <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn53/en/sujato'>SN 53</a> and throughout the Suttas, eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn12.23/en/sujato#2.15'>SN 12.23:2.15</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn44/en/sujato#12.2'>MN 44:12.2</a> and notes. The primary sense is the mind “collected” or “immersed” inside itself, with a secondary connotation of radiant illumination. | Before the Buddha, we find <i lang='pi' translate='no'>samādhi</i> and related terms in the sense of gathering kindling for the fire. The past participle <i lang='pi' translate='no'>samāhita</i> occurs in the sense “collected, contained”, as the essence of waters are “contained” in the Sun (Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 5.1.2.7). Certain Upaniṣadic usages presage the Buddhist sense, though expressed in a mystical form designed to be as obscure as possible. A realized sage, being “collected”, sees only the self in the self and is free from evil, having become one with Divinity (Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 4.4.24). For such a one, the whole world is “contained” in the space within the heart—earth and sky, fire and wind, sun and moon, all beings and all desires (Chāndogya Upaniṣad 8.1.3–5). The hidden Self, finer than the finest, larger than the largest, may only be discerned by one who is peaceful and “collected” (Kaṭha Upaniṣad 2.24).

## mn141:31.2
- **PLI**: Idhāvuso, bhikkhu vivicceva kāmehi vivicca akusalehi dhammehi savitakkaṁ savicāraṁ vivekajaṁ pītisukhaṁ paṭhamaṁ jhānaṁ upasampajja viharati,
- **EN**: It’s when a mendicant, quite secluded from sensual pleasures, secluded from unskillful qualities, enters and remains in the first absorption, which has the rapture and bliss born of seclusion, while placing the mind and keeping it connected.
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Jhāna</i> is a state of “elevated consciousness” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>adhicitta</i>), so all the terms have an elevated sense. | The plural form indicates that “sensual pleasures” includes sense experience, which the meditator can turn away from since they no longer have any desire for it. | The “unskillful qualities” are the five hindrances. | The “rapture and bliss born of seclusion” is the happiness of abandoning the hindrances and freedom from sense impingement. | “Placing the mind and keeping it connected” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>vitakka</i>, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vicāra</i>) uses terms that mean “thought” in coarse consciousness, but which in “elevated consciousness” refer to the subtle function of applying the mind to the meditation.

## mn141:31.3
- **PLI**: vitakkavicārānaṁ vūpasamā ajjhattaṁ sampasādanaṁ cetaso ekodibhāvaṁ avitakkaṁ avicāraṁ samādhijaṁ pītisukhaṁ dutiyaṁ jhānaṁ upasampajja viharati,
- **EN**: As the placing of the mind and keeping it connected are stilled, they enter and remain in the second absorption, which has the rapture and bliss born of immersion, with internal clarity and mind at one, without placing the mind and keeping it connected.
- **NOTE**: Each <i lang='pi' translate='no'>jhāna</i> begins as the least refined aspect of the previous <i lang='pi' translate='no'>jhāna</i> ends. This is not consciously directed, but describes the natural process of settling. The meditator is now fully confident and no longer needs to apply their mind: it is simply still and fully unified.

## mn141:31.4
- **PLI**: pītiyā ca virāgā upekkhako ca viharati …pe… tatiyaṁ jhānaṁ …pe…
- **EN**: And with the fading away of rapture, they enter and remain in the third absorption, where they meditate with equanimity, mindful and aware, experiencing for themselves the bliss of which the noble ones declare, ‘Equanimous and mindful, one meditates in bliss.’
- **NOTE**: The emotional response to bliss matures from the subtle thrill of rapture to the poise of equanimity. Mindfulness is present in all states of deep meditation, but with equanimity it becomes prominent.

## mn141:31.5
- **PLI**: catutthaṁ jhānaṁ upasampajja viharati,
- **EN**: With the giving up of pleasure and pain and the disappearance of former happiness and sadness, they enter and remain in the fourth absorption, without pleasure or pain, with pure equanimity and mindfulness.
- **NOTE**: The emotional poise of equanimity leads to the feeling of pleasure settling into the more subtle neutral feeling. Pain and sadness have been abandoned long before, but are emphasized here as they are subtle counterpart of pleasure.

## mn141:31.6
- **PLI**: ayaṁ vuccatāvuso: ‘sammāsamādhi’.
- **EN**: This is called right immersion.
