## mn12:32.1  `bj10.178, ms9M_506, msdiv152, ndp9.104, nya32`
- **PLI**: Catasso kho imā, sāriputta, yoniyo.
- **EN**: Sāriputta, there are these four kinds of reproduction.
- **NOTE**: These four, which are absent from the Chinese parallel, describe the births of different kinds of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nāga</i> (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn29.1/en/sujato'>SN 29.1</a>) and phoenix (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn30.1/en/sujato'>SN 30.1</a>).

## mn12:32.2
- **PLI**: Katamā catasso?
- **EN**: What four?

## mn12:32.3
- **PLI**: Aṇḍajā yoni, jalābujā yoni, saṁsedajā yoni, opapātikā yoni.
- **EN**: Reproduction for creatures born from an egg, from a womb, from moisture, or spontaneously.
- **NOTE**: Compare the three “seeds” of Chāndogya Upaniṣad 6.3.1: beings are born from an egg (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>āṇḍaja</i>), from a living individual (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>jīvaja</i>, i.e. “viviparous”), from a sprout (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>udbhijja</i>, i.e. plants). Expanded to four at Aitareya Upaniṣad 3.3: from an egg, from a membrane (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>jāruja</i> = <i lang='pi' translate='no'>jarāyuja</i>, cf. Pali <i lang='pi' translate='no'>jalābuja</i>), from moisture/sweat (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>svedaja</i>, cf. Pali <i lang='pi' translate='no'>saṁsedaja</i>), from a sprout. These are defined at Manusmṛti 1.43–6. The Jain Tattvārtha Sūtra 2.31–5 presents three kinds of birth: congealing (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>sammūrcchana</i>), uterine (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>garbha</i>; includes egg and womb), and spontaneous (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>upapāda</i>, cf. Pali <i lang='pi' translate='no'>opapātika</i> in the same sense). “Congealing” birth refers to all those not otherwise covered, and thus includes both “mosisture-born” and plants (cf. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn15/en/sujato#21.2'>DN 15:21.2</a>). | The Buddhist list is the only one that does not include plants, as Buddhists believe that consciousness does not take rebirth in plants. The Vedic position is that the soul (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>ātman</i>) can be reborn in plants (Chāndogya Upaniṣad 6.3.2), while the Jains believed that all things living and not living possess souls (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>jīva</i>).
