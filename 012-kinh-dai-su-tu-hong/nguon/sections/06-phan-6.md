# Phần 6  (50 đoạn)

## mn12:47.1  `ms9M_520, ndp9.111, nya47`
- **PLI**: Tatrāssu me idaṁ, sāriputta, jegucchismiṁ hoti—
- **EN**: And this is what my living in disgust of sin was like.

## mn12:47.2
- **PLI**: so kho ahaṁ, sāriputta, satova abhikkamāmi, satova paṭikkamāmi, yāva udakabindumhipi me dayā paccupaṭṭhitā hoti:
- **EN**: I’d step forward or back ever so mindfully, so I was full of pity regarding even a drop of water, thinking:
- **NOTE**: The Bodhisatta learned mindfulness while doing Jain-like practices and later incorporated it into the eightfold path (see also <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn36/en/sujato#20.7'>MN 36:20.7</a>). He also learned mindfulness under Brahmanical teachers (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn26/en/sujato#15.17'>MN 26:15.17</a>). | “Pity” is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>dayā</i>, which is similar to the more commonly found terms <i lang='pi' translate='no'>karuṇā</i> or <i lang='pi' translate='no'>anukampa</i>.

## mn12:47.3
- **PLI**: ‘māhaṁ khuddake pāṇe visamagate saṅghātaṁ āpādesin’ti.
- **EN**: ‘May I not injure any little creatures on unclear ground.’
- **NOTE**: To this day, Jain ascetics carry a soft broom to gently sweep away any insects in their path. | “Unclear ground” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>visamagata</i>) is explained by the commentary as ground that is uneven or covered in grass, branches, etc. A lion is said to reflect in the same way when setting out on a hunt (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/an10.21/en/sujato#1.7'>AN 10.21:1.7</a>). | Compare Bhāgavata Purāṇa 5.10.2, where a man carrying a palanquin is so scrupulous about avoiding treading on ants that he stops and starts, making the progress of the palanquin unsteady (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>viṣama-gatāṁ</i>).

## mn12:47.4
- **PLI**: Idaṁsu me, sāriputta, jegucchismiṁ hoti.
- **EN**: Such was my living in disgust of sin.

## mn12:48.1  `cck12.147, dr12.143, ms9M_521, nya48, pts-vp-pli1.79, vri12.113`
- **PLI**: Tatrāssu me idaṁ, sāriputta, pavivittasmiṁ hoti—

- **NOTE**: “Seclusion” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>viviktaśayyāsana</i>) is classified by the Jains as an “external austerity” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>bāhyatapas</i>) at Tattvārthasūtra 9.19.

## mn12:48.2
- **PLI**: so kho ahaṁ, sāriputta, aññataraṁ araññāyatanaṁ ajjhogāhetvā viharāmi.
- **EN**: I would plunge deep into a wilderness region and stay there.

## mn12:48.3
- **PLI**: Yadā passāmi gopālakaṁ vā pasupālakaṁ vā tiṇahārakaṁ vā kaṭṭhahārakaṁ vā vanakammikaṁ vā, vanena vanaṁ gahanena gahanaṁ ninnena ninnaṁ thalena thalaṁ sampatāmi.
- **EN**: When I saw a cowherd or a shepherd, or someone gathering grass or sticks, or a lumberjack, I’d flee from forest to forest, from thicket to thicket, from valley to valley, from uplands to uplands.

## mn12:48.4
- **PLI**: Taṁ kissa hetu?
- **EN**: Why is that?

## mn12:48.5
- **PLI**: Mā maṁ te addasaṁsu ahañca mā te addasanti.
- **EN**: So that I wouldn’t see them, nor they me.

## mn12:48.6
- **PLI**: Seyyathāpi, sāriputta, āraññako mago manusse disvā vanena vanaṁ gahanena gahanaṁ ninnena ninnaṁ thalena thalaṁ sampatati;
- **EN**: I fled like a wild deer seeing a human being.

## mn12:48.7
- **PLI**: evameva kho ahaṁ, sāriputta, yadā passāmi gopālakaṁ vā pasupālakaṁ vā tiṇahārakaṁ vā kaṭṭhahārakaṁ vā vanakammikaṁ vā vanena vanaṁ gahanena gahanaṁ ninnena ninnaṁ thalena thalaṁ sampatāmi.
- **EN**: (Sujato gộp đoạn này vào câu trước — dịch trực tiếp từ PLI)

## mn12:48.8
- **PLI**: Taṁ kissa hetu?
- **EN**: Why is that?  (= mn12:48.4)

## mn12:48.9
- **PLI**: Mā maṁ te addasaṁsu ahañca mā te addasanti.

## mn12:49.1  `csp1ed9.113, csp2ed9.113, ms9M_522, nya49, sya12.159`

- **EN**: I would go on all fours into the cow-pens after the cattle had left and eat the dung of the young suckling calves.

## mn12:49.2

- **EN**: Such was my eating of most unnatural things.

## mn12:50.1  `bj10.192, cck12.148, ms9M_523, msdiv157, nya50`
- **PLI**: So kho ahaṁ, sāriputta, aññataraṁ bhiṁsanakaṁ vanasaṇḍaṁ ajjhogāhetvā viharāmi.
- **EN**: I would plunge deep into an awe-inspiring forest grove and stay there.

## mn12:50.2
- **PLI**: Tatrāssudaṁ, sāriputta, bhiṁsanakassa vanasaṇḍassa bhiṁsanakatasmiṁ hoti—
- **EN**: It was so awe-inspiring that

## mn12:50.3
- **PLI**: yo koci avītarāgo taṁ vanasaṇḍaṁ pavisati, yebhuyyena lomāni haṁsanti.
- **EN**: normally it would make your hair stand on end if you weren’t free of greed.

## mn12:50.4
- **PLI**: So kho ahaṁ, sāriputta, yā tā rattiyo sītā hemantikā antaraṭṭhakā himapātasamayā tathārūpāsu rattīsu rattiṁ abbhokāse viharāmi, divā vanasaṇḍe;
- **EN**: And on days such as the cold spell when the snow falls in the January winter, I stayed in the open by night and in the forest by day.
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Antaraṭṭhakā</i> (Buddhist Hybrid Sanskrit <i lang='pi' translate='no'>aṣṭaka-rātri</i>) is the coldest part of Indian winter between the festival days that fall on the eighth day after the fullmoon in the two winter months of Māgha and Phagguna. This roughly corresponds with January, the coldest month in northern India.

## mn12:50.5
- **PLI**: gimhānaṁ pacchime māse divā abbhokāse viharāmi, rattiṁ vanasaṇḍe.
- **EN**: But in the last month of summer I’d stay in the open by day and in the forest by night.

## mn12:50.6
- **PLI**: Apissu maṁ, sāriputta, ayaṁ anacchariyagāthā paṭibhāsi pubbe assutapubbā:
- **EN**: And then these verses, which were neither supernaturally inspired, nor learned before in the past, occurred to me:
- **NOTE**: “Not supernaturally inspired” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>anacchariyā</i>) rejects the Vedic “channeling” of scripture from the Divinity, while “not learned before in the past” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>pubbe assutapubbā</i>), echoing the Dhammacakkappavattanasutta (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn56.11/en/sujato#5.1'>SN 56.11:5.1</a>), rejects the oral tradition.

- **EN**: Due to eating so little, the skin of my belly stuck to my backbone, so that when I tried to rub the skin of my belly I grabbed my backbone, and when I tried to rub my backbone I rubbed the skin of my belly.
