## mn16:2.1  `ms9M_664, nya2`
- **PLI**: “Yassa kassaci, bhikkhave, bhikkhuno pañca cetokhilā appahīnā, pañca cetasovinibandhā asamucchinnā, so vatimasmiṁ dhammavinaye vuddhiṁ virūḷhiṁ vepullaṁ āpajjissatīti—netaṁ ṭhānaṁ vijjati.
- **EN**: “Mendicants, when a mendicant has not given up five kinds of hard-heartedness and severed five shackles of the heart, it’s not possible for them to achieve growth, improvement, or maturity in this teaching and training.
- **NOTE**: The two sets of five are also found at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an10.14/en/sujato'>AN 10.14</a>. The five kinds of “hard-heartedness” are also at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an5.205/en/sujato'>AN 5.205</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an9.71/en/sujato'>AN 9.71</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn33/en/sujato#2.1.76'>DN 33:2.1.76</a>, and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn34/en/sujato#1.6.24'>DN 34:1.6.24</a>, and the five “shackles of the heart” are at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an5.206/en/sujato'>AN 5.206</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an9.72/en/sujato'>AN 9.72</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an9.82/en/sujato'>AN 9.82</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an9.92/en/sujato'>AN 9.92</a>, and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn33/en/sujato#2.1.84'>DN 33:2.1.84</a>. | While this sutta is addressed as normal to the <i lang='pi' translate='no'>bhikkhus</i>, the parallels at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an10.14/en/sujato'>AN 10.14</a>, MA 206, and EA 51.4 are all addressed to both monks and nuns. This is one of the instances showing that the term <i lang='pi' translate='no'>bhikkhu</i> is meant to include both monks and nuns, a common feature of Pali where the default gender is masculine. Hence I translate <i lang='pi' translate='no'>bhikkhu</i> with “mendicant”, which is both literal and gender-neutral, unless it is clear that males are meant, in which case I use “monk”.

## mn16:3.1  `ms9M_665, nya3`
- **PLI**: Katamāssa pañca cetokhilā appahīnā honti?
- **EN**: What are the five kinds of hard-heartedness they haven’t given up?
- **NOTE**: “Hard-heartedness” or “emotional barrenness” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>cetokhila</i>): <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ceto</i> is one of several words for “mind, heart” and is functionally equivalent to <i lang='pi' translate='no'>citta</i>, yet it tends to be used in contexts that emphasize the emotional dimensions of the mind such as here. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Khila</i> is said, in the commentary to <a href='https://suttacentral.net/snp1.2/en/sujato'>Snp 1.2</a>, to be land so barren that nothing grows even if it rains for four months.

## mn16:3.2
- **PLI**: Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu satthari kaṅkhati vicikicchati nādhimuccati na sampasīdati.
- **EN**: Firstly, a mendicant has doubts about the Teacher. They’re uncertain, undecided, and lacking confidence.

## mn16:3.3
- **PLI**: Yo so, bhikkhave, bhikkhu satthari kaṅkhati vicikicchati nādhimuccati na sampasīdati tassa cittaṁ na namati ātappāya anuyogāya sātaccāya padhānāya.
- **EN**: This being so, their mind doesn’t incline toward keenness, commitment, persistence, and striving.

## mn16:3.4
- **PLI**: Yassa cittaṁ na namati ātappāya anuyogāya sātaccāya padhānāya, evamassāyaṁ paṭhamo cetokhilo appahīno hoti.
- **EN**: This is the first kind of hard-heartedness they haven’t given up.
