# Phần 1  (50 đoạn)

## mn94:1.1  `bj11.628, cck13.555, ms10M_1268, msdiv412, ndp10.414, nya1, pts-vp-pli2.158, sya13.573, vri13.372`
- **PLI**: Evaṁ me sutaṁ—
- **EN**: So I have heard.
- **NOTE**: As usual, the lack of mention of the Buddha at the start suggests that this sutta is set after his passing away, a detail confirmed at the end. This sutta attests to the continued influence of Buddhism among the wealthy and influential as they adapted to the new realities after the Buddha’s passing.

## mn94:1.2
- **PLI**: ekaṁ samayaṁ āyasmā udeno bārāṇasiyaṁ viharati khemiyambavane.
- **EN**: At one time Venerable Udena was staying near Varanasi in the Khemiya Mango Grove.
- **NOTE**: Neither monk nor monastery are mentioned elsewhere in early texts.

## mn94:2.1  `nya2`
- **PLI**: Tena kho pana samayena ghoṭamukho brāhmaṇo bārāṇasiṁ anuppatto hoti kenacideva karaṇīyena.
- **EN**: Now at that time the brahmin Ghoṭamukha had arrived at Varanasi on some business.
- **NOTE**: This may be the Ghoṭamukha (“Beard-face”) of Kauṭilya’s Arthaśāstra 5.5.11. On the question of when a courtier should know that his king is displeased with him, a series of expert opinions are cited, each one bewilderingly cryptic. Ghoṭamukha says such a king is like a “wet cloth”. The key to these clues is provided in a particular story, which is deliberately unmentioned, presumably because of the delicacy of the subject.

## mn94:2.2
- **PLI**: Atha kho ghoṭamukho brāhmaṇo jaṅghāvihāraṁ anucaṅkamamāno anuvicaramāno yena khemiyambavanaṁ tenupasaṅkami.
- **EN**: Then as he was going for a walk he went to the Khemiya Mango Grove.

## mn94:2.3
- **PLI**: Tena kho pana samayena āyasmā udeno abbhokāse caṅkamati.
- **EN**: At that time Venerable Udena was walking mindfully in the open air.

## mn94:2.4
- **PLI**: Atha kho ghoṭamukho brāhmaṇo yenāyasmā udeno tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā āyasmatā udenena saddhiṁ sammodi.
- **EN**: Ghoṭamukha approached and exchanged greetings with him.

## mn94:2.5
- **PLI**: Sammodanīyaṁ kathaṁ sāraṇīyaṁ vītisāretvā āyasmantaṁ udenaṁ caṅkamantaṁ anucaṅkamamāno evamāha:
- **EN**: Walking alongside Udena, he said,

## mn94:2.6
- **PLI**: “ambho samaṇa, ‘natthi dhammiko paribbajo’—
- **EN**: “My ascetic, there is no such thing as a principled renunciate life;
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Ambho samaṇa</i> (“my ascetic”) is a unique form of address.

## mn94:2.7
- **PLI**: evaṁ me ettha hoti.
- **EN**: that’s what I think.

## mn94:2.8
- **PLI**: Tañca kho bhavantarūpānaṁ vā adassanā, yo vā panettha dhammo”ti.
- **EN**: Yet I have not seen honorable ones such as yourself, or a relevant teaching.”

## mn94:4.6

- **EN**: “Brahmin, we can discuss this. But only if you grant what should be granted, and reject what should be rejected. And if you ask me the meaning of anything you don’t understand, saying:
- **NOTE**: Ghoṭamukha was unfamiliar with the forms of address, admits he has never seen a mendicant, talks to Udena while he is walking meditation, and does not know when he should sit down. Udena, noticing his awkwardness, makes sure that Ghoṭamukha understands that he can engage in a conversation without impropriety.

## mn94:4.7

## mn94:4.8

- **EN**: “Let us discuss this. I will do as you say.”

## mn94:5.1  `bj11.630, dr13.525, ms10M_1270, msdiv413, nya5, sya13.575`
