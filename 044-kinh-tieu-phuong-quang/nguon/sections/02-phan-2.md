## mn44:12.1  `ms9M_1593, nya12`
- **PLI**: “Katamo panāyye, samādhi, katame dhammā samādhinimittā, katame dhammā samādhiparikkhārā, katamā samādhibhāvanā”ti?
- **EN**: “But ma’am, what is immersion? What things are the bases of immersion? What things are the prerequisites for immersion? What is the development of immersion?”

## mn44:12.2  `ms9M_1594`
- **PLI**: “Yā kho, āvuso visākha, cittassa ekaggatā ayaṁ samādhi;
- **EN**: “Unification of the mind is immersion.
- **NOTE**: “Unification” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>ekaggatā</i>), “immersion” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>samādhi</i>), and “absorption” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>jhāna</i>) are synonyms, although they may have specialized nuances. This understanding was common to Indic traditions, possibly under Buddhist influence. As just a few examples, Patañjali’s Yogasūtra 3.11 defines <i lang='pi' translate='no'>samādhi</i> as unification, as does Śaṅkāra on Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 4.4.3 (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>aikāgryarūpeṇa samāhito</i>), and the commentary to Bhagavad Gīta 2.42–44 (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>samādhiś cittaikāgryam</i>) and 17.11 (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>samādhāyaikāgraṁ</i>). The Jain Tattvārthasūtra 9.27 similarly defines <i lang='pi' translate='no'>dhyāna</i>, as does the commentary to Bhagavad Gīta 4.27 (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>dhyānaikāgryam</i>) and 13.24 (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>ekāgratayā yac cintanaṁ tad dhyānam</i>).

## mn44:12.3
- **PLI**: cattāro satipaṭṭhānā samādhinimittā;
- **EN**: The four kinds of mindfulness meditation are the bases for immersion.
- **NOTE**: The “foundations of immersion” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>samādhinimittā</i>) are the practices that lead to <i lang='pi' translate='no'>jhāna</i>. Here <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nimitta</i> means “cause” (Commentary: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>paccaya</i>).

## mn44:12.4
- **PLI**: cattāro sammappadhānā samādhiparikkhārā.
- **EN**: The four right efforts are the prerequisites for immersion.
- **NOTE**: Success in mindfulness meditation is the immediate cause of immersion, but to achieve both mindfulness and immersion it is a prerequisite (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>parikkhāra</i>) to make an effort.

## mn44:12.5
- **PLI**: Yā tesaṁyeva dhammānaṁ āsevanā bhāvanā bahulīkammaṁ, ayaṁ ettha samādhibhāvanā”ti.
- **EN**: The cultivation, development, and making much of these very same things is the development of immersion.”
- **NOTE**: “Development” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>bhāvanā</i>) means “growing”, “amplifying”.

## mn44:13.1  `ms9M_1595, msdiv463, nya13`

- **EN**: “How many processes are there?”

## mn44:13.2  `ms9M_1596`
- **PLI**: “Tayome, āvuso visākha, saṅkhārā—
- **EN**: “There are these three processes.

## mn44:13.3
- **PLI**: kāyasaṅkhāro, vacīsaṅkhāro, cittasaṅkhāro”ti.
- **EN**: The physical process, the verbal process, and the mental process.”
- **NOTE**: These three “processes” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>saṅkhāra</i>) apply, as we shall see, in the development of meditation (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn41.6/en/sujato#1.5'>SN 41.6:1.5</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn18/en/sujato#24.1'>DN 18:24.1</a>). They must be distinguished from the similarly named three kinds of “choices” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>saṅkhāra</i>) that define volitional activity determining rebirth (i.e. karma, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn57/en/sujato#8.2'>MN 57:8.2</a>). In the three “processes”, the final item is always <i lang='pi' translate='no'>cittasaṅkhāra</i> (“mental process”), whereas in the three “choices” the final item is usually <i lang='pi' translate='no'>manosaṅkhāra</i>. While <i lang='pi' translate='no'>mano</i> and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>citta</i> both mean “mind”, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>mano</i> leans to the sense of “volition”, while <i lang='pi' translate='no'>citta</i> leans to the sense of “awareness”. It is a quirk of the Pali texts that they usually have <i lang='pi' translate='no'>cittasaṅkhāra</i> in the context of dependent origination (but see <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn12.25/en/sujato#13.1'>SN 12.25:13.1</a>), where the Sanskrit has the expected <i lang='pi' translate='no'>manaḥsaṁskāraḥ</i> (SF 165 Ādisūtra, Arv 5.2 Arthaviniścayasūtra, SF 238 Pratītyasamutpādādivibhaṅganirdeśa).

## mn44:14.2  `ms9M_1598, ndp9.372`

- **EN**: “Breathing is a physical process. Placing the mind and keeping it connected are a verbal process. Perception and feeling are a mental process.”

## mn44:15.1  `bj10.708, dr12.485, ms9M_1599, nya15`
- **PLI**: “Kasmā panāyye, assāsapassāsā kāyasaṅkhāro, kasmā vitakkavicārā vacīsaṅkhāro, kasmā saññā ca vedanā ca cittasaṅkhāro”ti?
- **EN**: “But ma’am, why is breathing a physical process? Why are placing the mind and keeping it connected a verbal process? Why are perception and feeling a mental process?”

## mn44:15.2  `csp1ed9.376, csp2ed9.376, ms9M_1600, sya12.551`
- **PLI**: “Assāsapassāsā kho, āvuso visākha, kāyikā ete dhammā kāyappaṭibaddhā, tasmā assāsapassāsā kāyasaṅkhāro.
- **EN**: “Breathing is physical. It’s tied up with the body, that’s why breathing is a physical process.
- **NOTE**: Breathing is referred to as a “physical process” in the context of breath meditation to emphasize the natural stilling that occurs as the meditation settles. | As in <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn43/en/sujato'>MN 43</a>, the questions are laying the groundwork for a discussion of deep meditation. Hence in these definitions what is being discussed is not “processes” in general, but the very last residue of activity or disturbance whose cessation marks the transition to a deeper level of consciousness.

## mn44:15.3
- **PLI**: Pubbe kho, āvuso visākha, vitakketvā vicāretvā pacchā vācaṁ bhindati, tasmā vitakkavicārā vacīsaṅkhāro.
- **EN**: First you place the mind and keep it connected, then you break into speech. That’s why placing the mind and keeping it connected are a verbal process.
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Vitakka</i> and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vicāra</i> have a more basic sense in ordinary states of mind (“thought” and “exploring”) and a more refined sense in the elevated consciousness of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>jhāna</i> (“placing the mind” and “keeping it connected”). They act as a condition for breaking into speech, so any hint of such movement, no matter how subtle, must be abandoned.

## mn44:15.4
- **PLI**: Saññā ca vedanā ca cetasikā ete dhammā cittappaṭibaddhā, tasmā saññā ca vedanā ca cittasaṅkhāro”ti.
- **EN**: Perception and feeling are mental. They’re tied up with the mind, that’s why perception and feeling are a mental process.”
- **NOTE**: As with the verbal process, it is the two taken together that constitute a “process”.
