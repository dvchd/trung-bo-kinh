## mn44:2.1  `nya2`
- **PLI**: “‘sakkāyo sakkāyo’ti, ayye, vuccati.
- **EN**: “Ma’am, they speak of this thing called ‘substantial reality’.
- **NOTE**: The normal form of address for nuns is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ayyā</i> (“ma’am”), from the root <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ariya</i> (“noble”); the masculine form <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ayya</i> is occasionally used for monks as well. | “Substantial reality” is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sakkāya</i>, from <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sat</i> (“real”) and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>kāya</i> (“substance”). The Jain form is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>astikāya</i>, which refers to the five fundamental substances (or ontological categories) comprised of the medium of motion, the medium of rest, space, soul, and matter (Bhagavatīsūtra 1.10). Note that the suttas use <i lang='pi' translate='no'>kāya</i>, in the sense of “substance” or “mass”, as a key doctrinal term for ascetic movements associated with Jainism (eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn2/en/sujato#23.3'>DN 2:23.3</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn2/en/sujato#26.2'>DN 2:26.2</a>), whereas <i lang='pi' translate='no'>kāya</i> appears to have had no philosophical importance in pre-Buddhist Vedic texts. The commentaries define <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sakkāya</i> as “the three planes of cyclic existence”, i.e. all that exists. Thus <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sakkāya</i> is the “substantial reality” that is mistakenly assumed to be a “self”.

- **EN**: “But ma’am, is that grasping the exact same thing as the five grasping aggregates? Or is grasping one thing and the five grasping aggregates another?”
- **NOTE**: See also <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn109/en/sujato#6.1'>MN 109:6.1</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn22.82/en/sujato#4.4'>SN 22.82:4.4</a>.

## mn44:6.2
- **PLI**: “Na kho, āvuso visākha, taññeva upādānaṁ te pañcupādānakkhandhā, nāpi aññatra pañcahupādānakkhandhehi upādānaṁ.
- **EN**: “That grasping is not the exact same thing as the five grasping aggregates. Nor is grasping one thing and the five grasping aggregates another.

## mn44:6.3
- **PLI**: Yo kho, āvuso visākha, pañcasu upādānakkhandhesu chandarāgo taṁ tattha upādānan”ti.
- **EN**: The desire and greed for the five grasping aggregates is the grasping there.”
- **NOTE**: The five aggregates are not presented as a catch-all category that encompasses all of reality, but rather five types of phenomena that provoke attachment, forming the basis of what we take to be “self”. Desire is what drives the formation of attachment, but it requires all the aggregates to function. Thus the “grasping” aggregates are numbered “five” after the hand that grasps.
