## mn89:12.1  `cck13.497, dr13.466, ms10M_1094, msdiv369, ndp10.369, nya12, sya13.511, vri13.331`

- **EN**: Furthermore, I have walked and wandered from monastery to monastery and from park to park.

## mn89:12.2
- **PLI**: Sohaṁ tattha passāmi eke samaṇabrāhmaṇe kise lūkhe dubbaṇṇe uppaṇḍuppaṇḍukajāte dhamanisanthatagatte, na viya maññe cakkhuṁ bandhante janassa dassanāya.
- **EN**: There I’ve seen some ascetics and brahmins who are thin, haggard, pale, and veiny—hardly a captivating sight for people, you’d think.
- **NOTE**: This is a standard description of monastics who are unhappy and dissatisfied for whatever reason, eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-bu-vb-ss1/en/sujato#1.1.3'>Bu Ss 1:1.1.3</a>.

## mn89:12.3

## mn89:12.4

- **EN**: ‘Clearly these venerables lead the spiritual life dissatisfied, or they’re hiding some bad deed they’ve done.

## mn89:12.5
- **PLI**: tathā hi ime āyasmanto kisā lūkhā dubbaṇṇā uppaṇḍuppaṇḍukajātā dhamanisanthatagattā, na viya maññe cakkhuṁ bandhanti janassa dassanāyā’ti.
- **EN**: That’s why they’re thin, haggard, pale, and veiny—hardly a captivating sight for people, you’d think.’

## mn89:12.7

- **EN**: ‘Venerables, why are you so thin, haggard, pale, and veiny—hardly a captivating sight for people, you’d think?’

## mn89:12.9
- **PLI**: ‘bandhukarogo no, mahārājā’ti.
- **EN**: ‘We have jaundice, Great King.’
- **NOTE**: The commentary glosses <i lang='pi' translate='no'>kularogo</i> (“family sickness”), showing it must accept the reading <i lang='pi' translate='no'>bandhu</i> (“relative”). However, the parallel at MA 213 has <span lang='lzh'>白病</span>, which supports the variant <i lang='pi' translate='no'>paṇḍukarogino</i> (“jaundice”).

## mn89:12.10

- **EN**: But here I see mendicants always smiling and joyful, obviously happy, with cheerful faces, living relaxed, unruffled, surviving on charity, their hearts free as a wild deer.

## mn89:13.3
- **PLI**: Tassa mayhaṁ, bhante, aḍḍakaraṇe nisinnassa antarantarā kathaṁ opātenti.
- **EN**: Yet when I’m sitting in court they interrupt me.
- **NOTE**: Read <i lang='pi' translate='no'>aḍḍa</i> (variants <i lang='pi' translate='no'>attha</i>, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>aṭṭa</i>) which in Sanskrit has the senses “solve, infer, argue, discern”. The commentary interprets <i lang='pi' translate='no'>aḍḍakaraṇa</i> as the “place of judgment” i.e. the court, which is clearly the case at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn17/en/sujato#1.11.1'>DN 17:1.11.1</a>.

- **NOTE**: Pasenadi speaks of his frustrations in judging at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn3.7/en/sujato'>SN 3.7</a>. He was getting old and passing his duties to his son.
