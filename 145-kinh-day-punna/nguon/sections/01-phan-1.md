## mn145:1.1  `bj12.546, cck14.437, dr13.438, ms11M_1145, msdiv395, ndp11.357, nya1, pts-vp-pli3.267, sya14.481, vri14.320`
- **PLI**: Evaṁ me sutaṁ—
- **EN**: So I have heard.
- **NOTE**: This sutta recurs at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn35.88/en/sujato'>SN 35.88</a>, with slight differences in the opening and closing. In addition to two discourse parallels in Chinese, the popularity of this narrative is attested by its appearance in later texts such as the Mūlasarvāstivāda Vinaya and the Divyāvadāna, as well as in artworks (see Anālayo, <em>Comparative Study</em>, vol. ii, p. 828).

## mn145:1.2
- **PLI**: ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṁ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme.
- **EN**: At one time the Buddha was staying near Sāvatthī in Jeta’s Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika’s monastery.

## mn145:1.3
- **PLI**: Atha kho āyasmā puṇṇo sāyanhasamayaṁ paṭisallānā vuṭṭhito yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṁ abhivādetvā ekamantaṁ nisīdi. Ekamantaṁ nisinno kho āyasmā puṇṇo bhagavantaṁ etadavoca:
- **EN**: Then in the late afternoon, Venerable Puṇṇa came out of retreat and went to the Buddha. He bowed, sat down to one side, and said to the Buddha,
- **NOTE**: This Puṇṇa is to be distinguished from the Koliyan ascetic of <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn57/en/sujato'>MN 57</a> and the son of Mantāṇī of <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn23/en/sujato'>MN 23</a>. His only other appearance is his Theragāthā verse at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/thag1.70/en/sujato'>Thag 1.70</a>.

## mn145:2.1  `nya2`
- **PLI**: “sādhu maṁ, bhante, bhagavā saṅkhittena ovādena ovadatu, yamahaṁ bhagavato dhammaṁ sutvā eko vūpakaṭṭho appamatto ātāpī pahitatto vihareyyan”ti.
- **EN**: “Sir, may the Buddha please teach me Dhamma in brief. When I’ve heard it, I’ll live alone, withdrawn, diligent, keen, and resolute.”
- **NOTE**: The idea of a “retreat” as understood today was popularized in the 20th century. In early times, it seems, when an individual monastic felt they were ready, they would approach the teacher seeking advice for their retreat.

## mn145:2.2
- **PLI**: “Tena hi, puṇṇa, suṇāhi, sādhukaṁ manasi karohi; bhāsissāmī”ti.
- **EN**: “Well then, Puṇṇa, listen and apply your mind well, I will speak.”

## mn145:2.3
- **PLI**: “Evaṁ, bhante”ti kho āyasmā puṇṇo bhagavato paccassosi.
- **EN**: “Yes, sir,” replied Puṇṇa.

## mn145:2.4
- **PLI**: Bhagavā etadavoca:
- **EN**: The Buddha said this:

## mn145:5.1  `bj12.548, ms11M_1150, nya5`
- **PLI**: Iminā ca tvaṁ, puṇṇa, mayā saṅkhittena ovādena ovadito katarasmiṁ janapade viharissasī”ti?
- **EN**: Puṇṇa, now that I’ve given you this brief advice, what country will you live in?”

## mn145:5.2
- **PLI**: “Imināhaṁ, bhante, bhagavatā saṅkhittena ovādena ovadito, atthi sunāparanto nāma janapado, tatthāhaṁ viharissāmī”ti.
- **EN**: “Sir, there’s a country named Sunāparanta. I shall live there.”
- **NOTE**: The commentary says that Puṇṇa was in fact born in Sunāparanta, specifically the trading port of Suppāraka, which is modern Nallasopara north of Mumbai on India’s west coast. Sunāparanta is otherwise known as Aparanta (“Far West”, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/bv29/en/sujato#17.2'>Bv 29:17.2</a>). Puṇṇa’s choice was far-sighted, as from the time of Ashoka, Suppāraka became a major trading center with ports in India and lands west as far as Mesopotamia, Egypt, Eastern Africa, and Rome. A fabulous tale of such trading voyages is told in <a href='https://suttacentral.net/ja463/en/sujato'>Ja 463</a>.
