## mn38:16.1  `ms9M_1323, nya16`
- **PLI**: Ime ca, bhikkhave, cattāro āhārā kiṁnidānā kiṁsamudayā kiṁjātikā kiṁpabhavā?
- **EN**: What is the source, origin, birthplace, and inception of these four fuels?
- **NOTE**: The word <i lang='pi' translate='no'>āhāra</i> (“fuel”, “food”, “nutriment”) means literally “intake”, and is etymologically parallel to <i lang='pi' translate='no'>upādāna</i>, “grasping”, “uptake”. Both terms have dual senses, on the one hand denoting fuel or sustenance, and on the other grasping and attachment. That is why here (as at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn9/en/sujato#11.5'>MN 9:11.5</a>), <i lang='pi' translate='no'>āhāra</i> is created by craving, just like <i lang='pi' translate='no'>upādāna</i> in the standard sequence (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn38/en/sujato#17.8'>MN 38:17.8</a>).

## mn38:17.1  `ms9M_1331, nya17`
- **PLI**: Iti kho, bhikkhave, avijjāpaccayā saṅkhārā,
- **EN**: So, ignorance is a requirement for choices.
- **NOTE**: Here begins the full presentation of the standard sequence of dependent origination in forward order. Formal definitions are found at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn12.2/en/sujato'>SN 12.2</a>. Here I briefly indicate the nature of the conditioned links. | Because we are ignorant of the four noble truths, we make morally potent choices by body, speech, and mind.

## mn38:17.2
- **PLI**: saṅkhārapaccayā viññāṇaṁ,
- **EN**: Choices are a requirement for consciousness.
- **NOTE**: These choices are creative forces or energies in the mind that sustain the ongoing stream of sense consciousness from one life to the next.

## mn38:17.3
- **PLI**: viññāṇapaccayā nāmarūpaṁ,
- **EN**: Consciousness is a requirement for name and form.
- **NOTE**: Consciousness functions in relation to a cluster of phenomena both mental—feeling, perception, intention, contact, and application of mind—and physical—the four elements. These form an organism that grows and evolves.

## mn38:17.4
- **PLI**: nāmarūpapaccayā saḷāyatanaṁ,
- **EN**: Name and form are requirements for the six sense fields.
- **NOTE**: The sentient organism of the body requires senses to feed it stimuli.

## mn38:17.5
- **PLI**: saḷāyatanapaccayā phasso,
- **EN**: The six sense fields are requirements for contact.
- **NOTE**: Through these the sentient organism encounters the world outside and learns to make sense of it.

## mn38:17.6
- **PLI**: phassapaccayā vedanā,
- **EN**: Contact is a requirement for feeling.
- **NOTE**: It distinguishes experiences that are pleasant, unpleasant, and neutral.

## mn38:17.7
- **PLI**: vedanāpaccayā taṇhā,
- **EN**: Feeling is a requirement for craving.
- **NOTE**: It reacts by wanting to have more pleasure and to escape pain.

## mn38:17.8
- **PLI**: taṇhāpaccayā upādānaṁ,
- **EN**: Craving is a requirement for grasping.
- **NOTE**: Grasping at pleasures, view, observances, and theories of self, one makes sense of the world so as to optimize the capacity of oneself to experience pleasure.

## mn38:17.9
- **PLI**: upādānapaccayā bhavo,
- **EN**: Grasping is a requirement for continued existence.
- **NOTE**: This grasping binds one to time, to a continuity of existence in the realms of the senses or those of refined consciousness.

## mn38:17.10
- **PLI**: bhavapaccayā jāti,
- **EN**: Continued existence is a requirement for rebirth.
- **NOTE**: Shedding the body one takes up a new one in one of the realms of existence, perpetuating the cycle.

## mn38:17.11
- **PLI**: jātipaccayā jarāmaraṇaṁ sokaparidevadukkhadomanassupāyāsā sambhavanti.
- **EN**: Rebirth is a requirement for old age and death, sorrow, lamentation, pain, sadness, and distress to come to be.
- **NOTE**: Being born, it is inevitable that one will experience the pains of broken teeth, wrinkled skin, crooked back, and ultimately the failure of the body that we call death.
