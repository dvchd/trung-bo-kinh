## mn38:40.1  `bj10.632, cck12.473, csp1ed9.338, csp2ed9.338, dr12.436, ms9M_1390, msdiv414, ndp9.332, nya40, pts-vp-pli1.271, sya12.495, vri12.342`
- **PLI**: So cakkhunā rūpaṁ disvā piyarūpe rūpe na sārajjati, appiyarūpe rūpe na byāpajjati, upaṭṭhitakāyasati ca viharati appamāṇacetaso.
- **EN**: When they see a sight with their eyes, if it’s pleasant they don’t desire it, and if it’s unpleasant they don’t dislike it. They live with mindfulness of the body established and a limitless heart.
- **NOTE**: This resumes the teaching on attachment to the senses (from <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn38/en/sujato#30.1'>MN 38:30.1</a>), having shown what is required to let go such attachment. Here, one experiences the feelings through the senses, but without any attachment.

## mn38:40.2
- **PLI**: Tañca cetovimuttiṁ paññāvimuttiṁ yathābhūtaṁ pajānāti yatthassa te pāpakā akusalā dhammā aparisesā nirujjhanti.
- **EN**: And they truly understand the freedom of heart and freedom by wisdom where those arisen bad, unskillful qualities cease without remainder.

## mn38:40.3
- **PLI**: So evaṁ anurodhavirodhavippahīno yaṁ kiñci vedanaṁ vedeti, sukhaṁ vā dukkhaṁ vā adukkhamasukhaṁ vā, so taṁ vedanaṁ nābhinandati nābhivadati nājjhosāya tiṭṭhati.
- **EN**: Having given up favoring and opposing, when they experience any kind of feeling—pleasant, unpleasant, or neutral—they don’t approve, welcome, or keep clinging to it.

## mn38:40.4
- **PLI**: Tassa taṁ vedanaṁ anabhinandato anabhivadato anajjhosāya tiṭṭhato yā vedanāsu nandī sā nirujjhati.
- **EN**: As a result, relishing of feelings ceases.

## mn38:40.5
- **PLI**: Tassa nandīnirodhā upādānanirodho, upādānanirodhā bhavanirodho, bhavanirodhā jātinirodho, jātinirodhā jarāmaraṇaṁ sokaparidevadukkhadomanassupāyāsā nirujjhanti.
- **EN**: When their relishing ceases, grasping ceases. When grasping ceases, continued existence ceases. When continued existence ceases, rebirth ceases. When rebirth ceases, old age and death, sorrow, lamentation, pain, sadness, and distress cease.

## mn38:40.6
- **PLI**: Evametassa kevalassa dukkhakkhandhassa nirodho hoti.
- **EN**: That is how this entire mass of suffering ceases.

## mn38:41.11
- **PLI**: Imaṁ kho me tumhe, bhikkhave, saṅkhittena taṇhāsaṅkhayavimuttiṁ dhāretha, sātiṁ pana bhikkhuṁ kevaṭṭaputtaṁ mahātaṇhājālataṇhāsaṅghāṭappaṭimukkan”ti.
- **EN**: Mendicants, you should memorize this brief statement on freedom through the ending of craving. But the mendicant Sāti, the fisherman’s son, is caught in a vast net of craving, a tangle of craving.”
- **NOTE**: The mention of the “brief statement” at the end of a long discourse is puzzling. A similar exhortation to “memorize” a “brief” passage is found in only one other passage, where it is in reference to the short summary passage around which the sutta is based (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn140/en/sujato#32.3'>MN 140:32.3</a>). Compare the preceding sutta, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn37/en/sujato'>MN 37</a>, which revolves around a short passage for memorization that is fittingly described as “brief” throughout. That “brief” passage opens by saying “nothing is worth insisting on”, advice that is disregarded by Sāti who “insists” on his own view (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn38/en/sujato#3.11'>MN 38:3.11</a>). No “brief statement” is mentioned in the Chinese parallel (MA 201 at T i 769c28), which speaks instead of the shaking of the three-thousand-fold world system.
