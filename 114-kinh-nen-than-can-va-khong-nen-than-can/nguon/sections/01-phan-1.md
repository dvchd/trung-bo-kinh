- **NOTE**: “Incarnation” is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>attabhāva</i>, literally the “state of the self”, where the “self” comes close in meaning to “body”—whether material or immaterial—which is a secondary sense of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ātman</i> in the Upaniṣads. Accordingly, the commentary to <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an5.100/en/sujato'>AN 5.100</a> glosses as <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sarīrapaṭilābha</i>. | “Reincarnation” is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>paṭilābha</i>. In the 20th century, Buddhist writers shied away from using “reincarnation”, which they associated with the Hindu idea of a transmigrating soul. But there is nothing in the word “reincarnation” that implies a soul. Rather, it simply refers to going into another body, which is precisely what is meant by <i lang='pi' translate='no'>attabhāva</i>. It is unwise to hang a heavy philosophical cloak upon the feeble hook of a single word.
