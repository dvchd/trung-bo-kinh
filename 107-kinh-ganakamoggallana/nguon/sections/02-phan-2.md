## mn107:11.1  `cck14.81, ms11M_159, nya11, vri14.53`
- **PLI**: Ye kho te, brāhmaṇa, bhikkhū sekkhā apattamānasā anuttaraṁ yogakkhemaṁ patthayamānā viharanti tesu me ayaṁ evarūpī anusāsanī hoti.
- **EN**: That’s how I instruct the mendicants who are trainees—who haven’t achieved their heart’s desire, but live aspiring to the supreme sanctuary from the yoke.
- **NOTE**: The “trainee” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>sekha</i>) in the strict sense is restricted to those who have entered the ranks of the Noble Ones (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>ariyapuggala</i>) through the realization of the four noble truths (eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn48.53/en/sujato#3.1'>SN 48.53:3.1</a>); that is to say, the seven Noble Ones excluding the arahant, who is an “adept” beyond training (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>asekha</i>). However, in cases such as this it would be over-strict to insist that the teaching applies only to Noble Ones, as the Gradual Training is the recommended practice for all new monastics. Indeed, the burden of the text is to show how practice is taken up gradually, so above it says that this is how the Buddha “first guides them” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>paṭhamaṁ evaṁ vineti</i>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn107/en/sujato#3.3'>MN 107:3.3</a>). This agrees with the Chinese parallel, which here mentions a “young monk” (MA 144 at T i 652a29). Late canonical Pali texts introduce the idea of the “ordinary person of good character” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>puthujjanakalyāṇaka</i>, eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/cnd8/en/sujato#81.2'>Cnd 8:81.2</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/ps1.1/en/sujato#206.2'>Ps 1.1:206.2</a>), who is said to “train” like the trainees (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-pvr1.1/en/sujato#3.46'>Pvr 1.1:3.46</a>). The commentaries say they may be counted along with the seven Noble Ones as a “trainee” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>sekkhoti puthujjanakalyāṇakena saddhiṁ satta ariyā</i>, commentaries to Pārājika 1 and Jhānavibhaṅga).
