## mn27:13.3  `ms9M_1033`
- **PLI**: Abrahmacariyaṁ pahāya brahmacārī hoti ārācārī virato methunā gāmadhammā.
- **EN**: They give up unchastity. They are chaste, set apart, refraining from the vulgar act of sex.
- **NOTE**: “Chastity” is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>brahmacariya</i>, literally “divine conduct”. Here it is used in the narrow sense of refraining from sex, but more commonly it has a broader sense of “spiritual life”. Buddhist monastics are forbidden from any form of sexual activity. To engage in penetrative intercourse is an expulsion offense.

## mn27:13.4  `dr12.306, ms9M_1034`
- **PLI**: Musāvādaṁ pahāya musāvādā paṭivirato hoti saccavādī saccasandho theto paccayiko avisaṁvādako lokassa.
- **EN**: They give up lying. They speak the truth and stick to the truth. They’re honest and dependable, and don’t trick the world with their words.
- **NOTE**: This is the first of the four kinds of right speech. Just as the precept of not killing implies the positive injunction to live with compassion, the precepts on speech enjoin a positive and constructive use of speech. If a monastic falsely claims states of enlightenment or deep meditation they are expelled. Lying is further explained at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn41/en/sujato#9.2'>MN 41:9.2</a> as perjury.

- **NOTE**: For <i lang='pi' translate='no'>parimukhaṁ</i> (“present”) we find <i lang='pi' translate='no'>pratimukha</i> in Sanskrit, which can mean “presence” or the reflection of the face. Late canonical Pali explains <i lang='pi' translate='no'>parimukhaṁ</i> as “the tip of the nose or the reflection of the face (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>mukhanimitta</i>)”. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Parimukha</i> in Sanskrit is rare, but it appears in Pāṇini 4.4.29, which the commentary illustrates with the example of a servant “in the presence” of their master (cp. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn47.8/en/sujato'>SN 47.8</a>). A literal translation of the phrase might be, “having made memory attend to what is present”.
