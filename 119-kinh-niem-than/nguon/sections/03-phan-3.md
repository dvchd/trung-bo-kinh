Another group of six at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn55.3/en/sujato#4.1'>SN 55.3:4.1</a> and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an6.35/en/sujato'>AN 6.35</a> focus on the discernment side (observing impermanence, suffering in impermanence, not-self in suffering, giving up, fading away, and cessation).

## mn119:29.5
- **PLI**: “Evameva kho, bhikkhave, yassa kassaci kāyagatāsati bhāvitā bahulīkatā so, yassa yassa abhiññāsacchikaraṇīyassa dhammassa cittaṁ abhininnāmeti abhiññāsacchikiriyāya, tatra tatreva sakkhibhabbataṁ pāpuṇāti sati satiāyatane.
- **EN**: “In the same way, when a mendicant has developed and cultivated mindfulness of the body, they extend the mind to realize by insight each and every thing that can be realized by insight; and they are capable of realizing those things, since each and every one is within range.
- **NOTE**: The phrase <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sati sati āyatane</i> is tricky. Syntactically, it is a locative absolute construction, which Bodhi treats as conditional: “there being a suitable basis”. However this does not readily account for the repeated <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sati</i>, which he takes as the locative present participle from the verb <i lang='pi' translate='no'>atthi</i> (“being”). Ānandajoti, by contrast, takes the first <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sati</i> as “being” and the second as “mindfulness”, and treats the absolute construction as temporal: “while there is a basis for mindfulness”. Further, both accept the commentary’s gloss of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>āyatana</i> as <i lang='pi' translate='no'>kāraṇa</i> (“reason, basis”), although this sense of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>āyatana</i> is rare in the suttas (but see <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn12.25/en/sujato#14.3'>SN 12.25:14.3</a> = <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an4.171/en/sujato#5.3'>AN 4.171:5.3</a>). These readings of the absolute construction give it a limiting sense; the benefits apply only insofar as there is a “suitable basis” or a “basis for mindfulness”. But the expression is used by the Buddha of himself (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/an5.68/en/sujato#1.9'>AN 5.68:1.9</a>), so it seems odd that it would be limiting. Indeed, the tenor of the whole sentence is not limiting but expansive, as emphasized by the repeated reduplications: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>yassa yassa … tatra tatreva … sati sati</i>, which have a distributive sense. I therefore take <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sati</i> as “being” in both cases; the reduplication of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sati</i> as aligned with the preceding pronouns, emphasizing universality; the locative absolute as causal; and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>āyatana</i> in its normal sense of “scope, range”.
