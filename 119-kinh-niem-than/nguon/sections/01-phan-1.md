- **PLI**: “Kathaṁ bhāvitā ca, bhikkhave, kāyagatāsati kathaṁ bahulīkatā mahapphalā hoti mahānisaṁsā?
- **EN**: “And how, mendicants, is mindfulness of the body developed and cultivated to be very fruitful and beneficial?

## mn119:4.1  `nya4`
- **PLI**: Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu araññagato vā rukkhamūlagato vā suññāgāragato vā nisīdati pallaṅkaṁ ābhujitvā ujuṁ kāyaṁ paṇidhāya parimukhaṁ satiṁ upaṭṭhapetvā.
- **EN**: It’s when a mendicant—gone to a wilderness, or to the root of a tree, or to an empty hut—sits down cross-legged, sets their body straight, and brings mindfulness to the present.

## mn119:4.2
- **PLI**: So satova assasati satova passasati;
- **EN**: Just mindful, they breathe in. Mindful, they breathe out.

## mn119:4.3
- **PLI**: dīghaṁ vā assasanto ‘dīghaṁ assasāmī’ti pajānāti, dīghaṁ vā passasanto ‘dīghaṁ passasāmī’ti pajānāti;
- **EN**: Breathing in heavily they know: ‘I’m breathing in heavily.’ Breathing out heavily they know: ‘I’m breathing out heavily.’

## mn119:4.4
- **PLI**: rassaṁ vā assasanto ‘rassaṁ assasāmī’ti pajānāti, rassaṁ vā passasanto ‘rassaṁ passasāmī’ti pajānāti;
- **EN**: Breathing in lightly they know: ‘I’m breathing in lightly.’ Breathing out lightly they know: ‘I’m breathing out lightly.’

## mn119:4.5
- **PLI**: ‘sabbakāyapaṭisaṁvedī assasissāmī’ti sikkhati, ‘sabbakāyapaṭisaṁvedī passasissāmī’ti sikkhati;
- **EN**: They practice like this: ‘I’ll breathe in experiencing the whole body.’ They practice like this: ‘I’ll breathe out experiencing the whole body.’

## mn119:4.6
- **PLI**: ‘passambhayaṁ kāyasaṅkhāraṁ assasissāmī’ti sikkhati, ‘passambhayaṁ kāyasaṅkhāraṁ passasissāmī’ti sikkhati.
- **EN**: They practice like this: ‘I’ll breathe in stilling the physical process.’ They practice like this: ‘I’ll breathe out stilling the physical process.’

## mn119:4.7
- **PLI**: Tassa evaṁ appamattassa ātāpino pahitattassa viharato ye gehasitā sarasaṅkappā te pahīyanti.
- **EN**: As they meditate like this—diligent, keen, and resolute—memories and thoughts tied to domestic life are given up.
- **NOTE**: For “memories and thoughts tied to lay life” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>gehasitā sarasaṅkappā</i>) see <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn125/en/sujato#23.5'>MN 125:23.5</a> and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn54.8/en/sujato#5.2'>SN 54.8:5.2</a>. | <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Sarasaṅkappā</i> may be translated in two ways. Bodhi reads it as a <i lang='pi' translate='no'>dvanda</i> compound (“memories and thoughts”), which takes <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sara</i> as “memory” (Sanskrit <i lang='pi' translate='no'>smara</i> or <i lang='pi' translate='no'>smṛti</i>). The commentary, followed by Ānandajoti, explains it as <i lang='pi' translate='no'>dhāvanasaṅkappā</i> (“rushing thoughts”), which assumes the sense “flow” (Sanskrit <i lang='pi' translate='no'>saras</i>). Support for the <i lang='pi' translate='no'>dvanda</i> interpretation comes from Sanskrit texts, where we frequently find <i lang='pi' translate='no'>smṛtisaṁkalpa</i> as either two words (eg. Aitareya Upaniṣad 3.2) or a compound (Nyāyasūtra 4.2.34).

## mn119:4.8
- **PLI**: Tesaṁ pahānā ajjhattameva cittaṁ santiṭṭhati sannisīdati ekodi hoti samādhiyati.
- **EN**: Their mind becomes stilled internally; it settles, unifies, and becomes immersed in samādhi.
- **NOTE**: Each practice leads directly to immersion, reminding us that mindfulness meditation is “the basis of immersion” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>samādhinimitta</i>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn44/en/sujato#12.3'>MN 44:12.3</a>).

- **EN**: That’s how a mendicant develops mindfulness of the body.
