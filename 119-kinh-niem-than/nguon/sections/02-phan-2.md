## mn119:9.1  `cck14.195, dr13.189, ms11M_450, nya9`

- **EN**: Furthermore, suppose a mendicant were to see a corpse discarded in a charnel ground. And it had been dead for one, two, or three days, bloated, livid, and festering.

## mn119:9.2

- **EN**: As they meditate like this—diligent, keen, and resolute—memories and thoughts tied to domestic life are given up.

## mn119:9.5
- **PLI**: Tesaṁ pahānā ajjhattameva cittaṁ santiṭṭhati sannisīdati ekodi hoti samādhiyati.
- **EN**: Their mind becomes stilled internally; it settles, unifies, and becomes immersed in samādhi.

## mn119:9.6
- **PLI**: Evampi, bhikkhave, bhikkhu kāyagatāsatiṁ bhāveti.
- **EN**: That too is how a mendicant develops mindfulness of the body.

## mn119:18.1  `csp1ed11.135, csp2ed11.135, ms11M_454, msdiv155, nya18, pts-vp-pli3.93, sya14.209, vri14.137`
- **PLI**: Puna caparaṁ, bhikkhave, bhikkhu vivicceva kāmehi …pe… paṭhamaṁ jhānaṁ upasampajja viharati.
- **EN**: Furthermore, a mendicant, quite secluded from sensual pleasures, secluded from unskillful qualities, enters and remains in the first absorption, which has the rapture and bliss born of seclusion, while placing the mind and keeping it connected.
- **NOTE**: In the Satipaṭṭhānasutta, the practice of absorption is implicit in the contemplation of feelings (as spiritual pleasure), mind (as immersed mind, etc.), and principles (as the awakening factor of immersion), while the four absorptions are mentioned explicitly in the expanded version (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn22/en/sujato#21.32'>DN 22:21.32</a>). Here they take an even more central role.

## mn119:18.2
- **PLI**: So imameva kāyaṁ vivekajena pītisukhena abhisandeti parisandeti paripūreti parippharati, nāssa kiñci sabbāvato kāyassa vivekajena pītisukhena apphuṭaṁ hoti.
- **EN**: They drench, steep, fill, and spread themselves with rapture and bliss born of seclusion. There’s no part of the body that’s not spread with rapture and bliss born of seclusion.
- **NOTE**: As a meditator proceeds, their subjective experience of the “body” evolves from tactile sense impressions (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>phoṭṭhabba</i>), to the interior mental experience of bliss and light (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>manomayakāya</i>), to the direct personal realization of highest truth (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn70/en/sujato#23.2'>MN 70:23.2</a>: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>kāyena ceva paramasaccaṁ sacchikaroti</i>).

## mn119:18.3
- **PLI**: Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, dakkho nhāpako vā nhāpakantevāsī vā kaṁsathāle nhānīyacuṇṇāni ākiritvā udakena paripphosakaṁ paripphosakaṁ sanneyya, sāyaṁ nhānīyapiṇḍi snehānugatā snehaparetā santarabāhirā phuṭā snehena na ca pagghariṇī;
- **EN**: It’s like when a deft bathroom attendant or their apprentice pours bath powder into a bronze dish, sprinkling it little by little with water. They knead it until the ball of bath powder is soaked and saturated with moisture, spread through inside and out; yet no moisture oozes out.
- **NOTE**: The kneading is the “placing the mind and keeping it connected”, the water is bliss, while the lack of leaking speaks to the contained interiority of the experience.

## mn119:18.4
- **PLI**: evameva kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu imameva kāyaṁ vivekajena pītisukhena abhisandeti parisandeti paripūreti parippharati; nāssa kiñci sabbāvato kāyassa vivekajena pītisukhena apphuṭaṁ hoti.
- **EN**: In the same way, a mendicant drenches, steeps, fills, and spreads their body with rapture and bliss born of seclusion. There’s no part of the body that’s not spread with rapture and bliss born of seclusion.

## mn119:18.5
- **PLI**: Tassa evaṁ appamattassa …
- **EN**: As they meditate like this—diligent, keen, and resolute—memories and thoughts tied to lay life are given up.

## mn119:18.6

- **EN**: Furthermore, as the placing of the mind and keeping it connected are stilled, a mendicant enters and remains in the second absorption, which has the rapture and bliss born of immersion, with internal clarity and mind at one, without placing the mind and keeping it connected.

## mn119:19.2

- **EN**: They drench, steep, fill, and spread themselves with rapture and bliss born of immersion. There’s no part of the body that’s not spread with rapture and bliss born of immersion.

## mn119:19.3
- **PLI**: Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, udakarahado gambhīro ubbhidodako. Tassa nevassa puratthimāya disāya udakassa āyamukhaṁ na pacchimāya disāya udakassa āyamukhaṁ na uttarāya disāya udakassa āyamukhaṁ na dakkhiṇāya disāya udakassa āyamukhaṁ; devo ca na kālena kālaṁ sammā dhāraṁ anuppaveccheyya; atha kho tamhāva udakarahadā sītā vāridhārā ubbhijjitvā tameva udakarahadaṁ sītena vārinā abhisandeyya parisandeyya paripūreyya paripphareyya, nāssa kiñci sabbāvato udakarahadassa sītena vārinā apphuṭaṁ assa;
- **EN**: It’s like a deep lake fed by spring water. There’s no inlet to the east, west, north, or south, and the heavens would not properly bestow showers from time to time. But the stream of cool water welling up in the lake drenches, steeps, fills, and spreads throughout the lake. There’s no part of the lake that’s not spread through with cool water.
- **NOTE**: The simile emphasizes the water as bliss, while the lack of inflow expresses containment and unification. The water welling up is the rapture, which is the uplifting emotional response to the experience of bliss. These similes are also at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn2/en/sujato#78.1'>DN 2:78.1</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn10/en/sujato#2.16.1'>DN 10:2.16.1</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn39.16/en/sujato'>MN 39.16</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn77/en/sujato#26.3'>MN 77:26.3</a>, and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an5.28/en/sujato#3.4'>AN 5.28:3.4</a>.

## mn119:21.1  `ms11M_457, nya21, sya14.211`
- **PLI**: Puna caparaṁ, bhikkhave, bhikkhu sukhassa ca pahānā …pe… catutthaṁ jhānaṁ upasampajja viharati.
