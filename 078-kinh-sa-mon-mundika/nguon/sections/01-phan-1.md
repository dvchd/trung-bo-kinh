- **NOTE**: Uggāhamāna appears to have the sense “argumentative” found in the Sanskrit <i lang='pi' translate='no'>udgrāha</i> but not otherwise attested in Pali. Both sense and reading of Samaṇamaṇḍikā are unclear. He appears nowhere else. | Mallikā was the chief queen of Pasenadi, and her place is mentioned in a similar context at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn9/en/sujato#1.3'>DN 9:1.3</a>. The commentary explains that the brahmins, Jains, and others would assemble there to “debate their beliefs” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>samayaṁ pavadanti</i>), a rare early usage of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>samaya</i> in this sense. | Read <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ācīra</i> in the sense of “boundary, hedge” (Commentary: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>timbarūrukkhapantiyā parikkhittattā</i>; cf. Sanskrit <i lang='pi' translate='no'>prācīra</i>, “enclosure, hedge, fence, wall”).

## mn78:5.1  `nya5`
- **PLI**: “catūhi kho ahaṁ, gahapati, dhammehi samannāgataṁ purisapuggalaṁ paññapemi sampannakusalaṁ paramakusalaṁ uttamapattipattaṁ samaṇaṁ ayojjhaṁ.
- **EN**: “Householder, when an individual person has four qualities I describe them as an invincible ascetic—accomplished in the skillful, excelling in the skillful, attained to the highest attainment.
- **NOTE**: The “invincible ascetic” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>samaṇaṁ ayojjhaṁ</i>) appears only here. The Sanskrit <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ayodhya</i> occurs in the Atharva Veda as an epithet of Indra (19.13.7) and of the gods’ “impregnable” fortress (10.2.31). It seems to be a special term for an enlightened being in Uggāhamāna’s system; compare Upaka’s “Infinite Victor” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>anantajina</i>) of <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn26/en/sujato#25.22'>MN 26:25.22</a>.
