## mn87:1.1  `bj11.530, cck13.477, csp1ed10.309, csp2ed10.309, dr13.447, ms10M_1035, msdiv353, ndp10.352, nya1, pts-vp-pli2.106, sya13.489, sya13.490, vri13.316`
- **PLI**: Evaṁ me sutaṁ—
- **EN**: So I have heard.
- **NOTE**: King Pasenadi had what seems to be his first conversation and conversion with the Buddha in <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn3.1/en/sujato'>SN 3.1</a>. However, it would seem his faith was still weak, as he is frustrated by the devotion of his chief queen, Mallikā (“Jasmine”). Ultimately, her wise counsel persuades him. Similar conversations with Mallikā on attachment to loved ones are found at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn3.8/en/sujato'>SN 3.8</a> and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/ud5.1/en/sujato'>Ud 5.1</a>. King Pasenadi’s committment to non-attachment was sorely tested with the birth of a daughter (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn3.16/en/sujato'>SN 3.16</a>), and when Mallikā passed away (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/an5.49/en/sujato'>AN 5.49</a>).

## mn87:5.2  `cck13.479, ms10M_1037, msdiv355, ndp10.354, pts-vp-pli2.108`
- **PLI**: Atha kho rājā pasenadi kosalo mallikaṁ deviṁ āmantesi:
- **EN**: Then King Pasenadi addressed Queen Mallikā,
- **NOTE**: In addition to the conversations on the theme of attachment to loved ones, unrelated incidents with Mallikā appear at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an4.197/en/sujato'>AN 4.197</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-bu-vb-pc53/en/sujato#1.1'>Bu Pc 53:1.1</a>, and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-bu-vb-pc83/en/sujato#1.2.7'>Bu Pc 83:1.2.7</a>. She is listed as an eminent lay disciple (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/an8.91-117/en/sujato#1.1'>AN 8.91–117:1.1</a>) and maintained a monastery for philosophical debates (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn78/en/sujato#1.3'>MN 78:1.3</a>). This is what we know of her from early texts, while later Jātakas and other scriptures tell many colorful tales about her and her unique marriage. | Śvetāmbara Jains hold that, in the far distant past, the nineteenth founding teacher (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>tīrthaṅkara</i>) was the lady Malli (Triṣaṣṭiśalākāpuruṣacaritra 6.6). Perhaps the Buddhist stories of the wise lady Mallikā helped inspire this legend.
