## mn104:12.2
- **PLI**: Katamāni cattāri?
- **EN**: What four?

## mn104:12.3
- **PLI**: Vivādādhikaraṇaṁ, anuvādādhikaraṇaṁ, āpattādhikaraṇaṁ, kiccādhikaraṇaṁ—
- **EN**: Disciplinary issues due to disputes, accusations, offenses, or business.
- **NOTE**: These are formal legal proceedings in the Vinaya, explained at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd14/en/sujato#14.2.1'>Kd 14:14.2.1</a>. | Here and below I follow the renderings of Brahmali for legal terms.

## mn104:12.4
- **PLI**: imāni kho, ānanda, cattāri adhikaraṇāni.
- **EN**: These are the four kinds of disciplinary issues.

## mn104:13.1  `nya13`
- **PLI**: Satta kho panime, ānanda, adhikaraṇasamathā uppannuppannānaṁ adhikaraṇānaṁ samathāya vūpasamāya—
- **EN**: There are these seven means of settling any disciplinary issues that might arise.
- **NOTE**: Explained in more detail in the Vinaya (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd14/en/sujato#14.16.1'>Kd 14:14.16.1</a>).

## mn104:13.2
- **PLI**: sammukhāvinayo dātabbo, sativinayo dātabbo, amūḷhavinayo dātabbo, paṭiññāya kāretabbaṁ, yebhuyyasikā, tassapāpiyasikā, tiṇavatthārako.
- **EN**: Resolution face-to-face to be applied. Resolution through recollection to be granted. Resolution because of past insanity to be granted. Acting according to what has been admitted. Majority decision. Further penalty. Covering over as if with grass.

## mn104:16.1  `bj12.58, cck14.52, dr13.49, ms11M_98, msdiv49, nya16, pts-vp-pli2.248`
- **PLI**: Kathañcānanda, sativinayo hoti?
- **EN**: And how is there resolution through recollection?

## mn104:16.2
- **PLI**: Idhānanda, bhikkhū bhikkhuṁ evarūpāya garukāya āpattiyā codenti pārājikena vā pārājikasāmantena vā:
- **EN**: It’s when a mendicant accuses a mendicant of a heavy offense; one entailing expulsion, or close to it:
- **NOTE**: Pali editions vary as to whether the accuser is singular or plural. However, even those editions that use the plural here (Mahāsaṅgīti, PTS) shift to singular below (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn104/en/sujato#17.6'>MN 104:17.6</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn104/en/sujato#19.2'>MN 104:19.2</a>), while in the Vinaya it is also singular (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd14/en/sujato#14.29.7'>Kd 14:14.29.7</a>).

## mn104:16.3
- **PLI**: ‘saratāyasmā evarūpiṁ garukaṁ āpattiṁ āpajjitā pārājikaṁ vā pārājikasāmantaṁ vā’ti?
- **EN**: ‘Venerable, do you recall committing the kind of serious offense that entails expulsion or close to it?’

## mn104:16.4
- **PLI**: So evamāha:
- **EN**: They say:

## mn104:16.5
- **PLI**: ‘na kho ahaṁ, āvuso, sarāmi evarūpiṁ garukaṁ āpattiṁ āpajjitā pārājikaṁ vā pārājikasāmantaṁ vā’ti.
- **EN**: ‘No, reverend, I don’t recall committing such an offense.’
- **NOTE**: The Vinaya operates on good faith, so that the testimony of a clear-headed mendicant is sufficient to clear them from offenses in most cases.

## mn104:16.6
- **PLI**: Tassa kho, ānanda, bhikkhuno sativinayo dātabbo.
- **EN**: The resolution through recollection should be granted to them.

## mn104:16.7

- **EN**: That’s how there is the resolution through recollection. And that’s how certain disciplinary issues are settled, that is,

## mn104:16.8
- **PLI**: sativinayena.
- **EN**: by resolution through recollection.

## mn104:17.1  `cck14.53, csp1ed11.36, csp2ed11.36, ms11M_99, msdiv50, ndp11.42, nya17, sya14.55, vri14.34`
- **PLI**: Kathañcānanda, amūḷhavinayo hoti?
- **EN**: And how is there resolution because of past insanity?

## mn104:17.2
- **PLI**: Idhānanda, bhikkhū bhikkhuṁ evarūpāya garukāya āpattiyā codenti pārājikena vā pārājikasāmantena vā:
- **EN**: It’s when a mendicant accuses a mendicant of the kind of serious offense that entails expulsion, or close to it:
- **NOTE**: The offenses entailing expulsion for a mendicant are: willingly engaging in sexual intercourse; intentional killing of a human being; theft of something of value; and deliberately making a false claim to exalted spiritual attainments such as awakening or absorption.

## mn104:17.3
- **PLI**: ‘saratāyasmā evarūpiṁ garukaṁ āpattiṁ āpajjitā pārājikaṁ vā pārājikasāmantaṁ vā’ti?
- **EN**: ‘Venerable, do you recall committing the kind of serious offense that entails expulsion or close to it?’

## mn104:17.4
- **PLI**: So evamāha:
- **EN**: They say:

## mn104:17.5
- **PLI**: ‘na kho ahaṁ, āvuso, sarāmi evarūpiṁ garukaṁ āpattiṁ āpajjitā pārājikaṁ vā pārājikasāmantaṁ vā’ti.
- **EN**: ‘No, reverend, I don’t recall committing such an offense.’

## mn104:17.6
- **PLI**: Tamenaṁ so nibbeṭhentaṁ ativeṭheti:
- **EN**: But though they try to get out of it, the mendicant pursues the issue:

## mn104:17.7
- **PLI**: ‘iṅghāyasmā sādhukameva jānāhi yadi sarasi evarūpiṁ garukaṁ āpattiṁ āpajjitā pārājikaṁ vā pārājikasāmantaṁ vā’ti.
- **EN**: ‘Surely the venerable must know perfectly well if you recall committing an offense that entails expulsion or close to it!’

## mn104:17.8
- **PLI**: So evamāha:
- **EN**: They say:

## mn104:17.9
- **PLI**: ‘ahaṁ kho, āvuso, ummādaṁ pāpuṇiṁ cetaso vipariyāsaṁ.
- **EN**: ‘Reverend, I had gone mad, I was out of my mind.

## mn104:17.10
- **PLI**: Tena me ummattakena bahuṁ assāmaṇakaṁ ajjhāciṇṇaṁ bhāsitaparikkantaṁ.
- **EN**: And while I was mad I did and said many things that are not proper for an ascetic.

## mn104:17.11
- **PLI**: Nāhaṁ taṁ sarāmi.
- **EN**: I don’t remember any of that,

## mn104:17.12
- **PLI**: Mūḷhena me etaṁ katan’ti.
- **EN**: I was mad when I did it.’
- **NOTE**: This evidently refers to an episode of psychosis, during which time a person is subject to hallucinations and delusions, and may exhibit bizarre behavior. There is a blanket exemption in Vinaya for all acts committed in such a state.

## mn104:17.13
- **PLI**: Tassa kho, ānanda, bhikkhuno amūḷhavinayo dātabbo.
- **EN**: The resolution because of past insanity should be granted to them.

## mn104:17.14

- **EN**: That’s how there is resolution because of past insanity. And that’s how certain disciplinary issues are settled, that is,

- **EN**: by resolution because of past insanity.

## mn104:18.1  `dr13.50, ms11M_100, msdiv51, nya18, sya14.56`
- **PLI**: Kathañcānanda, paṭiññātakaraṇaṁ hoti?
- **EN**: And how is there acting according to what has been admitted?

## mn104:18.2
- **PLI**: Idhānanda, bhikkhu codito vā acodito vā āpattiṁ sarati, vivarati uttānīkaroti.
- **EN**: It’s when a mendicant, whether accused or not, recalls an offense and clarifies it and reveals it.

## mn104:18.3
- **PLI**: Tena, ānanda, bhikkhunā vuḍḍhataraṁ bhikkhuṁ upasaṅkamitvā ekaṁsaṁ cīvaraṁ katvā pāde vanditvā ukkuṭikaṁ nisīditvā añjaliṁ paggahetvā evamassa vacanīyo:
- **EN**: After approaching a more senior mendicant, that mendicant should arrange his robe over one shoulder, bow to that mendicant’s feet, squat on their heels, raise their cupped palms, and say:
- **NOTE**: This is the standard way of clearing offenses by confessing them as soon as they are recalled and recognized. Mendicants still confess with this formula today. Most offenses can be cleared in this way, but others require the relinquishment of a certain object (such as money), or undergoing a period of probation, while expulsion offenses cannot be cleared.

## mn104:18.4
- **PLI**: ‘ahaṁ, bhante, itthannāmaṁ āpattiṁ āpanno, taṁ paṭidesemī’ti.
- **EN**: ‘Sir, I have fallen into such-and-such an offense. I confess it.’

## mn104:18.5
- **PLI**: So evamāha:
- **EN**: The senior mendicant says:

## mn104:18.6
- **PLI**: ‘passasī’ti?
- **EN**: ‘Do you see it?’
