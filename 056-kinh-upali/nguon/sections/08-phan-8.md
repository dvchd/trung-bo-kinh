## mn56:30.1  `bj11.82, ms10M_208, msdiv77, nya30, pts-vp-pli1.387, vri13.56`
- **PLI**: “Kadā saññūḷhā pana te, gahapati, ime samaṇassa gotamassa vaṇṇā”ti?
- **EN**: “But when did you compose these praises of the ascetic Gotama’s beautiful qualities, householder?”
- **NOTE**: “When did you compose” is also at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn21/en/sujato#1.6.4'>DN 21:1.6.4</a>.

## mn56:30.2
- **PLI**: “Seyyathāpi, bhante, nānāpupphānaṁ mahāpuppharāsi, tamenaṁ dakkho mālākāro vā mālākārantevāsī vā vicittaṁ mālaṁ gantheyya;
- **EN**: “Sir, suppose there was a large heap of many different flowers. A deft garland-maker or their apprentice could tie them into a colorful garland.

## mn56:30.3
- **PLI**: evameva kho, bhante, so bhagavā anekavaṇṇo anekasatavaṇṇo.
- **EN**: In the same way, the Buddha has many beautiful qualities to praise, many hundreds of such qualities.
- **NOTE**: Upāli is playing on the dual senses of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vaṇṇa</i> as both “praise” and “beauty”. In doing so, he implicitly answers Mahāvīra’s question: he is adept at poetic improvisation (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>paṭibhāna</i>).

## mn56:30.4
- **PLI**: Ko hi, bhante, vaṇṇārahassa vaṇṇaṁ na karissatī”ti?
- **EN**: Who, sir, would not praise the praiseworthy?”

## mn56:31.1  `nya31`
- **PLI**: Atha kho nigaṇṭhassa nāṭaputtassa bhagavato sakkāraṁ asahamānassa tattheva uṇhaṁ lohitaṁ mukhato uggacchīti.
- **EN**: Unable to bear this honor paid to the Buddha, the Jain Ñātika spewed hot blood from his mouth there and then.
- **NOTE**: The Pali commentary says this was the cause of his death in Pāvā soon after, which supports the contention that Pāvā is Pāvārika. The Jain Kalpasūtra, however, tells a very different story, of a triumphant leader who, having lived a long and exemplary life, mindfully departed this world in honor and dignity, to the consternation of the gods.
