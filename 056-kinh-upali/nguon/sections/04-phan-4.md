- **PLI**: sutaṁ te daṇḍakīraññaṁ kāliṅgāraññaṁ majjhāraññaṁ mātaṅgāraññaṁ araññaṁ araññabhūtan”ti?
- **EN**: Have you heard how the wildernesses of Daṇḍaka, Kaliṅga, Mejjha, and Mātaṅga came to be that way?”
- **NOTE**: This passage is referenced at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mil5.1.6/en/sujato#16.3'>Mil 5.1.6:16.3</a>. | Daṇḍaka was a vast wilderness covering much of the Deccan; it features heavily in the Rāmāyaṇa. Vettam Mani’s <em>Puranic Encyclopedia</em> informs us that Daṇḍa was a son of Ikṣvāku, founder of the solar dynasty, who gave Daṇḍa the country between the mountains Himālaya and Vindhya. But Daṇḍa raped Arā, the daughter of the hermit Śukra. Śukra destroyed the country of Daṇḍa in a rain of fire. See too Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa 3.5. Buddhist accounts may be found in the Sarabhaṅga Jātaka at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/ja522/en/sujato'>Ja 522</a> and Mahāvastu 99. This sage is possibly noted by name at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn116/en/sujato#6.13'>MN 116:6.13</a>. | Kaliṅga was a coastal realm in modern Odisha and Andhra Pradesh. Its downfall is related in <a href='https://suttacentral.net/ja522/en/sujato'>Ja 522</a>, although that was a kammic result of vile sin against ascetics rather than by psychic power. | Mejjha’s destruction is told in <a href='https://suttacentral.net/ja497/en/sujato'>Ja 497</a>. There, Mātaṅga features as a wise sage, and it is not clear to me how this relates to the wilderness of that name. In the suttas, the person Mātaṅga was regarded as an enlightened sage of the past (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn116/en/sujato#6.17'>MN 116:6.17</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/snp1.7/en/sujato#26.1'>Snp 1.7:26.1</a>).
