## mn30:2.1  `nya2`
- **PLI**: Atha kho piṅgalakoccho brāhmaṇo yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavatā saddhiṁ sammodi.
- **EN**: Then the brahmin Piṅgalakoccha went up to the Buddha, and exchanged greetings with him.
- **NOTE**: Not mentioned elsewhere in the Pali, this brahmin’s name means “tawny” (or “blotchy”) Koccha. His name suggests he was of the lineage of Kutsa Āṅgirasa, an oft-mentioned sage and ally (and sometime foe) of Indra in the Rig Veda. The name Kautsa appears in the same lineage lists as Vātsya (eg. Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 6.5.4; see <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn27/en/sujato#2.2'>MN 27:2.2</a>).

## mn30:2.2
- **PLI**: Sammodanīyaṁ kathaṁ sāraṇīyaṁ vītisāretvā ekamantaṁ nisīdi. Ekamantaṁ nisinno kho piṅgalakoccho brāhmaṇo bhagavantaṁ etadavoca:
- **EN**: When the greetings and polite conversation were over, he sat down to one side and said to the Buddha:

## mn30:2.3
- **PLI**: “yeme, bho gotama, samaṇabrāhmaṇā saṅghino gaṇino gaṇācariyā ñātā yasassino titthakarā sādhusammatā, bahujanassa,
- **EN**: “Worthy Gotama, there are those ascetics and brahmins who lead an order and a community, and tutor a community. They’re well-known and famous religious founders, deemed holy by many people.

## mn30:2.4
- **PLI**: seyyathidaṁ—pūraṇo kassapo, makkhali gosālo, ajito kesakambalo, pakudho kaccāyano, sañcayo belaṭṭhaputto, nigaṇṭho nāṭaputto,
- **EN**: Namely: Pūraṇa Kassapa, the bamboo-staffed ascetic Gosāla, Ajita of the hair blanket, Pakudha Kaccāyana, Sañjaya Belaṭṭhiputta, and the Jain ascetic of the Ñātika clan.

## mn30:2.5
- **PLI**: sabbete sakāya paṭiññāya abbhaññaṁsu sabbeva nābbhaññaṁsu, udāhu ekacce abbhaññaṁsu ekacce nābbhaññaṁsū”ti?
- **EN**: According to their own claims, did all of them have direct knowledge, or none of them, or only some?”
- **NOTE**: While some such as Mahāvīra the Ñātika (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn14/en/sujato#17.2'>MN 14:17.2</a>) and Pūraṇa Kassapa (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/an9.38/en/sujato#2.1'>AN 9.38:2.1</a>) claimed to have direct knowledge, others such as Ajita Kesakambala denied that such knowledge was possible (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn2/en/sujato#23.2'>DN 2:23.2</a>).

## mn30:2.6
- **PLI**: “Alaṁ, brāhmaṇa, tiṭṭhatetaṁ—
- **EN**: “Enough, brahmin, let this be:
- **NOTE**: The Buddha responded the same way when asked this question by Subhadda (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn16/en/sujato#5.26.5'>DN 16:5.26.5</a>).

## mn30:2.7

- **EN**: ‘According to their own claims, did all of them have direct knowledge, or none of them, or only some?’
