## mn108:10.14  `cck14.89, csp1ed11.61, csp2ed11.61, ms11M_174, msdiv81, ndp11.71`
- **PLI**: “Atthi kho, brāhmaṇa, tena bhagavatā jānatā passatā arahatā sammāsambuddhena bhikkhūnaṁ sikkhāpadaṁ paññattaṁ, pātimokkhaṁ uddiṭṭhaṁ.
- **EN**: “The Blessed One, who knows and sees, the perfected one, the fully awakened Buddha laid down training rules and recited the monastic code for the mendicants.
- **NOTE**: This is the <i lang='pi' translate='no'>pātimokkha</i>, which in early days had “over a hundred and fifty rules” and today, in the Pali, has 227 for monks (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/an3.84/en/sujato#1.3'>AN 3.84:1.3</a>). The story of how the Buddha came to lay down the first rule is related in the Vinaya (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-bu-vb-pj1/en/sujato'>Bu Pj 1</a>). Before this, however, the joint recitation consisted of the verses known as Ovāda Pātimokkha (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn14/en/sujato#3.28.1'>DN 14:3.28.1</a>). See too <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn2/en/sujato#42.1'>DN 2:42.1</a> and note there. The primary purpose of the recitation was to create a unified community through shared allegiance to a particular code of ethics.

## mn108:10.15
- **PLI**: Te mayaṁ tadahuposathe yāvatikā ekaṁ gāmakhettaṁ upanissāya viharāma te sabbe ekajjhaṁ sannipatāma;
- **EN**: On the day of the sabbath all of us who live in dependence on one village district gather together as one.
- **NOTE**: The “sabbath” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>uposatha</i>) falls on the eighth and fourteenth or fifteenth days of the lunar cycle, that is, roughly each week. However the <i lang='pi' translate='no'>pātimokkha</i> is recited only twice a month, leaving aside the eighth days. See the detailed account in the Vinaya (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd2/en/sujato'>Kd 2</a>). | A “village district” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>gāmakhetta</i>) is the village and surrounding farmlands. The Vinaya account specifies how to create a “boundary” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>sīmā</i>) within which all resident mendicants must attend. This may or may not correspond with the village district. But it seems reasonable to suppose that the common practice was, in fact, for mendicants from the same village district to join together.

## mn108:10.16
- **PLI**: sannipatitvā yassa taṁ pavattati taṁ ajjhesāma.
- **EN**: We invite one who transmits the code to recite it.
- **NOTE**: The choice of the verb <i lang='pi' translate='no'>pavattati</i> (“transmits, rolls forth”) is deliberate. It echoes the first sermon, where the Buddha “rolled forth” the wheel of the Dhamma. All mendicants are supposed to memorize the rules and be ready to recite them when called upon. The commentary explains the phrase as “arrives having rehearsed”.

## mn108:10.17
- **PLI**: Tasmiñce bhaññamāne hoti bhikkhussa āpatti hoti vītikkamo taṁ mayaṁ yathādhammaṁ yathānusiṭṭhaṁ kāremāti.
- **EN**: If anyone remembers an offense or transgression while they’re reciting, we make them act in line with the teaching and in line with the instructions.
- **NOTE**: Offenses should be confessed as soon as practicable, but if they have not been previously confessed due to lapse of memory and the like, the recitation may be interrupted to confess them. These days, mendicants normally confess before the recitation to avoid interruptions.

## mn108:10.18
- **PLI**: Na kira no bhavanto kārenti;
- **EN**: It’s not the good fellows who make us act,

## mn108:10.19
- **PLI**: dhammo no kāreti”.
- **EN**: it’s the teaching that makes us act.”
- **NOTE**: The Saṅgha proceeds according to the guidelines of the Dhamma and Vinaya, rather than the authority of any individuals. This is again in accordance with some of the Buddha’s final words to Ānanda: “The teaching and training that I have taught and pointed out for you shall be your Teacher after my passing” (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn16/en/sujato#6.1.1'>DN 16:6.1.1</a>).

## mn108:11.1  `ms11M_175, nya11`
- **PLI**: “Atthi nu kho, bho ānanda, ekabhikkhupi yaṁ tumhe etarahi sakkarotha garuṁ karotha mānetha pūjetha; sakkatvā garuṁ katvā upanissāya viharathā”ti?
- **EN**: “Worthy Ānanda, is there even a single mendicant who you honor, respect, revere, venerate, and rely on?”

## mn108:11.2
- **PLI**: “Natthi kho, brāhmaṇa, ekabhikkhupi yaṁ mayaṁ etarahi sakkaroma garuṁ karoma mānema pūjema; sakkatvā garuṁ katvā upanissāya viharāmā”ti.
- **EN**: “There is not, brahmin.”
- **NOTE**: The Mahāsaṅgīti and BJT editions read <i lang='pi' translate='no'>natthi</i> here (“there is not”). The PTS edition reads <i lang='pi' translate='no'>atthi</i> (“there is”). The commentary is silent, and none of these editions record any variants. I suspect the PTS edition has amended the text to create a contrast between the “single mendicant” who is respected and the one who is taken as refuge. But the contrast per the MS and BJT editions, rather, is between respecting a “single mendicant”, and respecting “whoever” has the qualities that make them worthy of respect.
