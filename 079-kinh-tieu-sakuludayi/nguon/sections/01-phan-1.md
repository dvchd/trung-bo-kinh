## mn79:7.5  `ms10M_760`
- **PLI**: Api ca, udāyi, tiṭṭhatu pubbanto, tiṭṭhatu aparanto.
- **EN**: Nevertheless, Udāyī, leave aside the first beginning and the final end.
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Pubbanta</i> and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>aparanta</i> (literally “prior end” and “posterior end”) are technical terms found exclusively in Buddhism, and only used in the context of speculative views. It seems the Buddha had a contextual reason to avoid the generic words for “past” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>atīta</i>) and “future” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>anāgata</i>), and I reflect that in my translation. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Pubbanta</i> is semantically identical with <i lang='pi' translate='no'>pubbakoti</i>, the “first point” of existence or transmigration (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn48.50/en/sujato#5.3'>SN 48.50:5.3</a>). This is emphasized by Nāgarjuna, who introduces the terms <i lang='pi' translate='no'>pubbanta</i> and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>aparanta</i> immediately after discussing the “first point” of transmigration, and associates them with the “extremes” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>anta</i>) of eternalism and annihilationism (Mūlamadhyamakakārikā 25.20–23). Thus such talk is not merely about the past as in what happened yesterday, or the future as in what I’ll do next week, but unmoored speculations about the “first beginning” and the “final end”.
