## mn103:3.1  `bj12.46, cck14.41, csp1ed11.28, csp2ed11.28, dr13.38, ms11M_74, msdiv35, ndp11.32, nya3, pts-vp-pli2.239, vri14.26`
- **PLI**: Tasmātiha, bhikkhave, ye vo mayā dhammā abhiññā desitā, seyyathidaṁ—
- **EN**: In that case, each and every one of you should train in the things I have taught from my direct knowledge, that is:

## mn103:3.2
- **PLI**: cattāro satipaṭṭhānā cattāro sammappadhānā cattāro iddhipādā pañcindriyāni pañca balāni satta bojjhaṅgā ariyo aṭṭhaṅgiko maggo, tattha sabbeheva samaggehi sammodamānehi avivadamānehi sikkhitabbaṁ.
- **EN**: the four kinds of mindfulness meditation, the four right efforts, the four bases of psychic power, the five faculties, the five powers, the seven awakening factors, and the noble eightfold path. You should train in these things in harmony, appreciating each other, without dispute.
- **NOTE**: These are the sets of practices later called the 37 <i lang='pi' translate='no'>bodhiyapakkhiyā dhammā</i>, the “wings to awakening”. Here they are presented as an essential summary of the Buddha’s teachings. They form the backbone of the final book of the Saṁyuttanikāya, the Maggavagga (or Mahāvagga). It is likely that the Buddha was referring to the earliest recension of this text. They are found as a summary of the Buddha’s teachings at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn16/en/sujato#3.50.5'>DN 16:3.50.5</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn29/en/sujato#17.3'>DN 29:17.3</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn104/en/sujato#5.3'>MN 104:5.3</a>, and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an8.19/en/sujato#17.2'>AN 8.19:17.2</a> = <a href='https://suttacentral.net/ud5.5/en/sujato#25.2'>Ud 5.5:25.2</a>.

- **EN**: ‘These two venerables disagree on both the meaning and the phrasing.’ So you should approach whichever mendicant you think is most amenable and say to them:
- **NOTE**: “Meaning” is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>attha</i>, the “purpose”, “goal”, or “benefit” of the teaching. “Phrasing” is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>byañjana</i> (also spelled <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vyañjana</i>), the “letter”, “syllable”, or “expression”, with the root sense of “differentiating”. Such passages show that concern for both aspects were present from the earliest times. | “Most amenable” is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>suvacatara</i>, literally, “more easy to speak to”.
