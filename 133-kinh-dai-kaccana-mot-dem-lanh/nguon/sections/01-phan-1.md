## mn133:1.2
- **PLI**: ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā rājagahe viharati tapodārāme.
- **EN**: At one time the Buddha was staying near Rājagaha in the Hot Springs Monastery.
- **NOTE**: The hot springs near Rājagaha were a popular place for monks to bathe, so much so that they prompted a rule ensuring that the monks did not monopolize the springs (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-bu-vb-pc57/en/sujato'>Bu Pc 57</a>). They are still in use and just as popular as ever.

## mn133:1.3
- **PLI**: Atha kho āyasmā samiddhi rattiyā paccūsasamayaṁ paccuṭṭhāya yena tapodo tenupasaṅkami gattāni parisiñcituṁ.
- **EN**: Then Venerable Samiddhi rose at the crack of dawn and went to the hot springs to bathe.
- **NOTE**: Soon after he went forth, a deity in the same location tempted Samiddhi to enjoy sensual delights (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn1.20/en/sujato'>SN 1.20</a>). Had he succumbed, that would have been a case of “faltering amid presently arisen phenomena”. Samiddhi further appears in discourses at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn136/en/sujato'>MN 136</a>, where he had gone forth only three years, and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an9.14/en/sujato'>AN 9.14</a>, where he seems more mature. In <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn4.22/en/sujato'>SN 4.22</a>, Māra threatens to shake his faith, prompting him to ask the Buddha about Māra’s true nature (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn35.65/en/sujato'>SN 35.65</a>). This event is recalled in his verse at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/thag1.46/en/sujato'>Thag 1.46</a>.
