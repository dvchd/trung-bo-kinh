## mn29:1.1  `csp1ed9.250, csp2ed9.250, dr12.322, ms9M_1077, msdiv307, ndp9.243, nya1, pts-vp-pli1.192, sya12.362, vri12.253`
- **PLI**: Evaṁ me sutaṁ—
- **EN**: So I have heard.

## mn29:1.2
- **PLI**: ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā rājagahe viharati gijjhakūṭe pabbate acirapakkante devadatte.
- **EN**: At one time the Buddha was staying near Rājagaha, on the Vulture’s Peak Mountain, not long after Devadatta had left.
- **NOTE**: Devadatta was the Buddha’s cousin and nemesis. After going forth together with several other relatives of the Buddha (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd17/en/sujato#1.4.1'>Kd 17:1.4.1</a>), his initial success in meditation was corrupted by his desire for gains and fame (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn17.35/en/sujato'>SN 17.35</a>). He tried to take over the Saṅgha but was denounced by the Buddha. His attempt to create a schismatic faction failed and he died in disgrace.

## mn29:1.3
- **PLI**: Tatra kho bhagavā devadattaṁ ārabbha bhikkhū āmantesi:
- **EN**: There the Buddha spoke to the mendicants about Devadatta:

## mn29:2.1  `bj10.464, cck12.345, ms9M_1078, nya2`
- **PLI**: “Idha, bhikkhave, ekacco kulaputto saddhā agārasmā anagāriyaṁ pabbajito hoti:
- **EN**: “Mendicants, take the case of a gentleman who has gone forth out of faith from the lay life to homelessness, thinking,

## mn29:2.2
- **PLI**: ‘otiṇṇomhi jātiyā jarāya maraṇena sokehi paridevehi dukkhehi domanassehi upāyāsehi, dukkhotiṇṇo dukkhapareto,
- **EN**: ‘I’m swamped by rebirth, old age, and death; by sorrow, lamentation, pain, sadness, and distress. I’m swamped by suffering, mired in suffering.

## mn29:2.3
- **PLI**: appeva nāma imassa kevalassa dukkhakkhandhassa antakiriyā paññāyethā’ti.
- **EN**: Hopefully I can make an end to this entire mass of suffering.’

## mn29:2.4
- **PLI**: So evaṁ pabbajito samāno lābhasakkārasilokaṁ abhinibbatteti.
- **EN**: When they’ve gone forth they generate possessions, honor, and popularity.

## mn29:2.5
- **PLI**: So tena lābhasakkārasilokena attamano hoti paripuṇṇasaṅkappo.
- **EN**: They’re happy with that, and they’ve got all they wished for.

## mn29:2.6
- **PLI**: So tena lābhasakkārasilokena attānukkaṁseti paraṁ vambheti:
- **EN**: And they glorify themselves and put others down because of that:

## mn29:2.7
- **PLI**: ‘ahamasmi lābhasakkārasilokavā, ime panaññe bhikkhū appaññātā appesakkhā’ti.
- **EN**: ‘I’m the one with possessions, honor, and popularity. These other mendicants are obscure and insignificant.’

## mn29:2.8
- **PLI**: So tena lābhasakkārasilokena majjati pamajjati pamādaṁ āpajjati, pamatto samāno dukkhaṁ viharati.
- **EN**: And so they become indulgent and fall into negligence on account of those possessions, honor, and popularity. And being negligent they live in suffering.
- **NOTE**: The Chinese parallel at EA 43.4 says that this discourse was given during the time he was being lavishly supported by Prince Ajātasattu (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn17.36/en/sujato'>SN 17.36</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd17/en/sujato#2.1.1'>Kd 17:2.1.1</a>).

## mn29:2.9  `bj10.466, cck12.346, csp1ed9.251, csp2ed9.251, dr12.323, ms9M_1079, vri12.254`
- **PLI**: Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, puriso sāratthiko sāragavesī sārapariyesanaṁ caramāno mahato rukkhassa tiṭṭhato sāravato atikkammeva sāraṁ atikkamma phegguṁ atikkamma tacaṁ atikkamma papaṭikaṁ, sākhāpalāsaṁ chetvā ādāya pakkameyya ‘sāran’ti maññamāno.
- **EN**: Suppose there was a person in need of heartwood. And while wandering in search of heartwood he’d come across a large tree standing with heartwood. But, passing over the heartwood, softwood, bark, and shoots, he’d cut off the branches and leaves and leave imagining they were heartwood.
- **NOTE**: Compare the “noble quest” on <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn26/en/sujato'>MN 26</a> and the search for a “bull elephant” in <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn27/en/sujato'>MN 27</a>. In each case the metaphor emphasizes the specific and uncompromising nature of the goal. The moral is to not stop until the journey is truly complete.
