## mn29:5.14
- **PLI**: So tāya samādhisampadāya na majjati nappamajjati na pamādaṁ āpajjati appamatto samāno ñāṇadassanaṁ ārādheti.
- **EN**: Being diligent, they achieve knowledge and vision.
- **NOTE**: While “knowledge and vision” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>ñāṇadassana</i>) that is “in accordance with reality” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>yathābhūta</i>) refers to the vision of the four noble truths at stream-entry, “knowledge and vision” by itself does not have a well established technical meaning. At <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn2/en/sujato#83.1'>DN 2:83.1</a> it is said to be a vision of the body with its consciousness, although this passage is rare and in <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn77/en/sujato#29.11'>MN 77:29.11</a> is not described as “knowledge and vision”. Elsewhere it is used in a variety of senses for a kind of clear and enhanced spiritual understanding.

## mn29:6.16
- **PLI**: So tena ñāṇadassanena na majjati nappamajjati na pamādaṁ āpajjati, appamatto samāno asamayavimokkhaṁ ārādheti.
- **EN**: Nor do they become indulgent and fall into negligence regarding that knowledge and vision. Being diligent, they achieve irreversible freedom.
- **NOTE**: The “irreversible freedom” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>asamaya</i>, literally “not temporary”) is that of arahantship, as opposed to the “temporary freedom” of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>samādhi</i>, from which one can fall away due to bad conduct (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/an5.149/en/sujato'>AN 5.149</a>), like Devadatta. | Text inconsistently has <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vimokkha</i> (“liberation”) here and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vimutti</i> (“freedom”) in the next line, although elsewhere the suttas always read <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vimutti</i>. It seems that when these attainments came to be included in the <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Vimokkha</i> Chapter of the Paṭisambhidāmagga (a late canonical Abhidhamma-style text), they were renamed <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vimokkha</i> to fit the context (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/ps1.5/en/sujato#2.10'>Ps 1.5:2.10</a>). The commentary then cited that passage here using <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vimokkha</i>, but the commentarial gloss must have contaminated the text. I translate under the assumption that the correct reading is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vimutti</i>, although it makes no practical difference.
