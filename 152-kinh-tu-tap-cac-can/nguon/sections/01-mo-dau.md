## mn152:1.2
- **PLI**: ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā gajaṅgalāyaṁ viharati suveḷuvane.
- **EN**: At one time the Buddha was staying near Kajaṅgalā in a bamboo grove.
- **NOTE**: The names here are spelled <i lang='pi' translate='no'>gajaṅgalā</i> and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>suveḷuvana</i>, but I keep consistency with <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an10.28/en/sujato'>AN 10.28</a>, where a nun residing at this town gives a penetrative discourse to the townsfolk. In the Vinaya it is mentioned as the easternmost point of the “middle country”, just beyond which lay Mahāsāla (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd5/en/sujato#13.12.2'>Kd 5:13.12.2</a>). The Milindapañha says it was a brahmin village on the slopes of the Himalayas where its main protagonist, the monk Nāgasena, was born (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mil2/en/sujato#22.3'>Mil 2:22.3</a>). Assuming both these are the same place, we might look somewhere near Darjeeling for this town. In any case, it represents the furthest east that the Buddha taught. It is sometimes identified with <span lang='lzh'>羯蠅揭羅</span> mentioned by Xuang Zang, but that has been located near Agra south of Sāvatthī.

## mn152:2.1  `nya2`
- **PLI**: Atha kho uttaro māṇavo pārāsiviyantevāsī yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavatā saddhiṁ sammodi.
- **EN**: Then the student Uttara, a pupil of the brahmin Pārāsariya, approached the Buddha, and exchanged greetings with him.
- **NOTE**: A brahmin teacher named Pārāsariya (also spelled Pārāpariya or Pārāsiviya) features in <a href='https://suttacentral.net/ja222/en/sujato'>Ja 222</a> and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/ja353/en/sujato'>Ja 353</a> as one who warns that doing evil will lead to regret. The three similarly-named monks in the Theragāthā (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/thag1.116/en/sujato'>Thag 1.116</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/thag16.2/en/sujato'>Thag 16.2</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/thag16.10/en/sujato'>Thag 16.10</a>) share the same clan name (Sanskrit Pārāśarya). These are descendants of the great sage Parāśara Śāktya, who is mentioned in Rig Veda 7.1.18 alongside Śatayātu, Vasiṣṭha; his prayers to Agni are recorded at Rig Veda 1.65–73, and to Soma at 9.97.31–44.

## mn152:2.6
- **PLI**: “Idha, bho gotama, cakkhunā rūpaṁ na passati, sotena saddaṁ na suṇāti—
- **EN**: “Worthy Gotama, it’s when the eye sees no sight and the ear hears no sound.
- **NOTE**: This is perhaps a distorted reference to certain Upaniṣadic teachings that speak of the undifferentiated state of divinity that contains the potential for the faculties of seeing, etc. See <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn29/en/sujato#16.13'>DN 29:16.13</a> and note there. Perhaps, too, it refers to the notion that in deep sleep the senses are withdrawn into the “space of the heart” (Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 2.1.17).
