## mn116:1.1  `bj12.206, cck14.166, dr13.159, ms11M_368, msdiv133, ndp11.131, nya1, pts-vp-pli3.68, sya14.175, vri14.114`
- **PLI**: Evaṁ me sutaṁ—
- **EN**: So I have heard.
- **NOTE**: This is a devotional sutta that lists the names of sages of the past. It is recited as a protection chant in Sri Lanka. The Chinese parallel shares only the opening passages, after which it is quite different. Moreover, only three of the names are clearly the same: Ariṭṭha, Upariṭṭha, and Sudassana (EA 38.7 at T ii 723a–c). | Ñāṇamoḷī and Bodhi both rightly note that it is difficult to distinguish names from attributes without the commentary. Yet the commentary leaves many problems unsolved, as it omits many names.

## mn116:3.2  `bj12.208, cck14.167, dr13.160, ms11M_374, ndp11.132, pts-vp-pli3.69, sya14.176, vri14.115`
- **PLI**: Bhūtapubbaṁ, bhikkhave, pañca paccekabuddhasatāni imasmiṁ isigilismiṁ pabbate ciranivāsino ahesuṁ.
- **EN**: Once upon a time, five hundred independent Buddhas dwelt for a long time on this Isigili.
- **NOTE**: The concept of the <i lang='pi' translate='no'>paccekabuddha</i> allows that there were awakened sages in the long periods of time when there is no <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sambuddha</i> such as Gotama. Both are Buddhas in the sense of being awakened, but they differ in their relation to others. It is sometimes said that they do not teach, but the tradition attributes to them a number of sayings (notably <a href='https://suttacentral.net/snp1.3/en/sujato'>Snp 1.3</a>). They are sometimes said to be “solitary”, but here 500 dwell on the same hill, while <i lang='pi' translate='no'>pacceka</i> Brahmās appear in pairs (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn6.6/en/sujato#1.3'>SN 6.6:1.3</a>). Compare <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-bu-vb-np9/en/sujato#1.36.1'>Bu NP 9:1.36.1</a>, where “independent” funds are set up for “independent” robes (rather than combining them); the same idea occurs at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an11.16/en/sujato#11.2'>AN 11.16:11.2</a>. Likewise, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn1.37/en/sujato#1.7'>SN 1.37:1.7</a> speaks of “independent” verses. Thus the burden of sense in the Pali is that <i lang='pi' translate='no'>pacceka</i> means “independent”, which here has the sense of not forming a fourfold community, but simply living and practicing independently.

## mn116:3.10
- **PLI**: Taṁ suṇātha, sādhukaṁ manasi karotha, bhāsissāmī”ti.
- **EN**: Listen and apply your mind well, I will speak.”
- **NOTE**: I give the probable meaning of each name, and attempt, perhaps unwisely, to locate other sages of the past with similar names. In almost every case it is impossible to establish such links with any confidence, due to the lack of contextual details, the prevalence of common names used by many people, the use of names based on places or doctrinal concepts, and so on. Nonetheless, certain patterns emerge, especially the fact that several of the names seem to be shared with Jain <i lang='pi' translate='no'>tīrthaṅkaras</i>. These are the twenty-four founding fathers of Jainism through the ages, like the Buddhist concept of past Buddhas. As many as eight names might be shared with <i lang='pi' translate='no'>tīrthaṅkaras</i>, while several others might be other Jain sages. Even the name of the sutta itself is the name of a sage Isigiri in Isibhāsiyāiṁ 34. It could be that both Buddhism and Jainism drew on shared traditions of ancient sages, but again, these similarities are all tentative at best.

## mn116:4.1  `cck14.168, csp1ed11.114, csp2ed11.114, ms11M_375, msdiv134, nya4, sya14.177`
- **PLI**: “Ariṭṭho nāma, bhikkhave, paccekasambuddho imasmiṁ isigilismiṁ pabbate ciranivāsī ahosi;
- **EN**: “The independent Buddhas who dwelt for a long time on this Isigili were named Ariṭṭha,
- **NOTE**: An agent of Kuvera has this name (meaning “indestructible”) at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn32/en/sujato#7.47'>DN 32:7.47</a>, where it is next to Nemi (below at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn116/en/sujato#6.7'>MN 116:6.7</a>). Ariṭṭha and Nemi often appear compounded in Sanskrit as Ariṣṭanemi. This was the name of a past Buddha (Mahāvastu 1.140), the 22nd Jain <i lang='pi' translate='no'>tīrthaṅkara</i> (Kalpasūtra 170–183), and a Brahmanical sage of remarkable power (Mahābhārata 3.182.8c). Ariṣṭanemi Tārkṣya, although a mythical being, was said to have composed Rig Veda 10.178.
