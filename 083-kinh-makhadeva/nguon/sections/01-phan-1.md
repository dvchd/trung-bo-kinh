- **EN**: So I have heard.
- **NOTE**: Versions of this story are told in the Maghadeva Jātaka (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/ja9/en/sujato'>Ja 9</a>) and Nimi Jātaka (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/ja541/en/sujato'>Ja 541</a>).

## mn83:1.2
- **PLI**: ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā mithilāyaṁ viharati maghadevaambavane.
- **EN**: At one time the Buddha was staying near Mithilā in the Maghadeva Mango Grove.
- **NOTE**: Mithilā was the capital of Videha, to the north east of the Vajjian federation, nestled against the Himalayas. Mithilā was a dominant kingdom before the Buddha, its king Janaka featuring prominently in early Upaniṣads. It features rarely in the suttas (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn91/en/sujato'>MN 91</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/thig6.2/en/sujato'>Thig 6.2</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/thig13.4/en/sujato'>Thig 13.4</a>) and had apparently declined in importance. It is unclear whether Mithilā had been subsumed into the Vajjian federation or remained as a minor independent kingdom until conquered by Magadha some years later. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn19/en/sujato#36.7'>DN 19:36.7</a> establishes the mythic origins of the land from a Buddhist perspective, whereas Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 1.4.1.10–19 depicts its origins in terms of the spread of fire-worship from the west by the founding king Māthava Videgha with his priest Gotama Rāhūgaṇa.

## mn83:6.1  `cck13.411, dr13.385, ms10M_891, nya6, sya13.418`
- **PLI**: Rājā kho panānanda, maghadevo caturāsītivassasahassāni kumārakīḷitaṁ kīḷi, caturāsītivassasahassāni oparajjaṁ kāresi, caturāsītivassasahassāni rajjaṁ kāresi, caturāsītivassasahassāni imasmiṁyeva maghadevaambavane agārasmā anagāriyaṁ pabbajito brahmacariyamacari.
- **EN**: For 84,000 years King Maghadeva played games as a child, for 84,000 years he acted as viceroy, for 84,000 years he ruled the realm, and for 84,000 years he led the spiritual life after going forth here in this mango grove.
- **NOTE**: 84,000 is 12 times 7 times 1000, where 12 is the months of the year (a full cycle) and in addition, a multiple of 4, the number of fullness and balance. 7 signifies the entire cycle of birth and death, being the days of the week and the visible “planets” (Sun, Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, and Saturn). 1000 is a major multiplier. Thus the overall sense of 84,000 is an abundant fullness (especially of time), in which sense it became a popular mystic number in Buddhism.

## mn83:6.2
- **PLI**: So cattāro brahmavihāre bhāvetvā kāyassa bhedā paraṁ maraṇā brahmalokūpago ahosi.
- **EN**: And having developed the four divine meditations, when his body broke up, after death, he was reborn in a good place, a realm of divinity.
- **NOTE**: Living the “spiritual (divine) life” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>brahmacariya</i>), he practiced the “divine meditations” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>brahmavihāra</i>) and was reborn in the “divine realm of Brahmā” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>brahmaloka</i>). The Buddha is presenting an idealized vision of the ancient Brahmanical path as a coherent spiritual practice guided by the notion of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>brahma</i> as spiritual or divine excellence (see too <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn99/en/sujato#22.14'>MN 99:22.14</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn13/en/sujato#38.2'>DN 13:38.2</a>). | As the exemplar of an ideal Brahmanized king, Maghadeva is perhaps to be identified with Māthava Videgha, the founder and Brahmanizer of Mithilā. His name ought probably be read <i lang='pi' translate='no'>mādhava</i> (“possessor of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>madhu</i>”) where <i lang='pi' translate='no'>madhu</i> (“honey”) is a common term for Soma. Māthava Videgha was the first king who carried out the Soma rites in the region, bringing Agni to “burn over” (civilize) the newly-brahmanized land.
