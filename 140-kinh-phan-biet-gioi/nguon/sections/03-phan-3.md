## mn140:16.5

## mn140:19.1  `cck14.403, ms11M_1023, msdiv354, ndp11.327, nya19`
- **PLI**: Athāparaṁ viññāṇaṁyeva avasissati parisuddhaṁ pariyodātaṁ.
- **EN**: What remains is sheer consciousness, pure and bright.
- **NOTE**: The phrase “sheer consciousness” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>viññāṇaṁyeva</i>) echoes Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 2.4.12, where the ultimate manifestation of the Self, the “principle reality”, is said to be “a sheer mass of consciousness” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>vijñānaghana eva</i>). ## mn140:20.1  `cck14.405, csp1ed11.287, csp2ed11.287, ms11M_1029, msdiv360, nya20, vri14.293`
- **PLI**: Athāparaṁ upekkhāyeva avasissati parisuddhā pariyodātā mudu ca kammaññā ca pabhassarā ca.
- **EN**: There remains only equanimity, pure, bright, pliable, workable, and radiant.
- **NOTE**: This is the purified equanimity of the fourth absorption. Although the teaching is framed in terms of the development of discernment (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>vipassanā</i>), nonetheless it follows the pattern of the absorptions, leading through the first four “form” absorptions to the radiant consciousness that can experience the formless.

- **EN**: It’s like when a deft goldsmith or a goldsmith’s apprentice prepares a forge, fires the crucible, picks up some native gold with tongs and puts it in the crucible. That native gold becomes pliable, workable, and radiant, not brittle, and is ready to be worked. Then the goldsmith can successfully create any kind of ornament they want, whether a bracelet, earrings, a necklace, or a golden garland.
- **NOTE**: This simile is developed further at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an3.102/en/sujato#2.1'>AN 3.102:2.1</a>.
