## mn121:10.1  `cck14.217, ms11M_504, msdiv182, ndp11.172, nya10, pts-vp-pli3.108`
- **PLI**: Puna caparaṁ, ānanda, bhikkhu amanasikaritvā ākiñcaññāyatanasaññaṁ, amanasikaritvā nevasaññānāsaññāyatanasaññaṁ, animittaṁ cetosamādhiṁ paṭicca manasi karoti ekattaṁ.
- **EN**: Furthermore, a mendicant—ignoring the perception of the dimension of nothingness and the perception of the dimension of neither perception nor non-perception—focuses on the oneness dependent on the signless immersion of the heart.
- **NOTE**: The defining characteristic of the “signless immersion of the heart” is that consciousness does not “follow after signs” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>nimittānusāri viññāṇaṁ</i>, eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an6.13/en/sujato#5.3'>AN 6.13:5.3</a>). This is explained in <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn138/en/sujato#10.2'>MN 138:10.2</a> as not being distracted or affected by the features of sense impressions, as the “signs” are created by greed, hate, and delusion (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn43/en/sujato#37.1'>MN 43:37.1</a>). By itself, however, it does not necessarily indicate that one is awakened (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/an6.60/en/sujato#8.2'>AN 6.60:8.2</a>), as good conduct is still required for progress (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/an7.56/en/sujato#13.1'>AN 7.56:13.1</a>). It was practiced by Moggallāna before awakening, who had to be urged to “not follow any signs” (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn40.9/en/sujato'>SN 40.9</a>). It may also be used to describe the meditation of an arahant (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn41.7/en/sujato#6.12'>SN 41.7:6.12</a>).

## mn121:10.2

- **EN**: Their mind leaps forth, gains confidence, settles down, and becomes decided in that signless immersion of the heart.

- **EN**: They understand:

## mn121:10.4

- **EN**: ‘Here there is no stress due to the perception of the dimension of nothingness or the perception of the dimension of neither perception nor non-perception.

## mn121:10.5
- **PLI**: imameva kāyaṁ paṭicca saḷāyatanikaṁ jīvitapaccayā’ti.
- **EN**: There is only this modicum of stress, namely that related to the six sense fields dependent on this body and conditioned by life.’
- **NOTE**: Unlike with deep absorption, where there is no experience of the physical body, the signless meditation is a state of advanced discernment or insight where the six senses are seen as they truly are. This explains why it was a way for the Buddha to ease the pains of old age (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn16/en/sujato#2.25.13'>DN 16:2.25.13</a>). It seems that in such a state, the Buddha was able to function normally while seeing through the pain in his body.

- **EN**: They understand: ‘This field of perception is empty of the perception of the dimension of nothingness. It is empty of the perception of the dimension of neither perception nor non-perception.

## mn121:10.7
- **PLI**: imameva kāyaṁ paṭicca saḷāyatanikaṁ jīvitapaccayā’ti.

## mn121:13.1  `cck14.219, dr13.213, ms11M_506, msdiv184, ndp11.173, nya13, sya14.233`
- **PLI**: Yepi hi keci, ānanda, atītamaddhānaṁ samaṇā vā brāhmaṇā vā parisuddhaṁ paramānuttaraṁ suññataṁ upasampajja vihariṁsu, sabbe te imaṁyeva parisuddhaṁ paramānuttaraṁ suññataṁ upasampajja vihariṁsu.
- **EN**: Whatever ascetics and brahmins enter and remain in the pure, ultimate, supreme emptiness—whether in the past, future, or present—all of them enter and remain in this same pure, ultimate, supreme emptiness.
- **NOTE**: Here the emptiness is distinguished as “supreme” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>anuttara</i>). In early Pali, Nibbāna is not described as either empty or as not-self, as these are properties of the path. This passage is probably the closest we get to that, but even this is relative, since the body and life persist.
