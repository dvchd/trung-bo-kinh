# Phần 1  (50 đoạn)

## mn121:1.1  `bj12.264, cck14.212, csp1ed11.147, csp2ed11.147, dr13.207, ms11M_496, msdiv176, ndp11.168, nya1, pts-vp-pli3.104, sya14.226, vri14.148`
- **PLI**: Evaṁ me sutaṁ—
- **EN**: So I have heard.
- **NOTE**: This sutta gives a unique approach to meditation on emptiness, using the perception of relative emptiness as a foundation for absorption. | The topic of emptiness does not play the central role in Pali texts that it did in later Buddhism. This is perhaps at least partly due to different doctrinal developments in the early Buddhist schools. Passages that in the Pali mention impermanence, suffering, and not-self, in parallels of the (Mūla)-Sarvāstivāda often include emptiness as well. This does not indicate any doctrinal contradiction, as emptiness is explained as meaning not-self. But it does suggest that the presentation in terms of emptiness was more popular in that group of schools.

## mn121:1.2
- **PLI**: ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṁ viharati pubbārāme migāramātupāsāde.
- **EN**: At one time the Buddha was staying near Sāvatthī in the stilt longhouse of Migāra’s mother in the Eastern Monastery.

## mn121:2.1  `nya2`
- **PLI**: Atha kho āyasmā ānando sāyanhasamayaṁ paṭisallānā vuṭṭhito yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṁ abhivādetvā ekamantaṁ nisīdi. Ekamantaṁ nisinno kho āyasmā ānando bhagavantaṁ etadavoca:
- **EN**: Then in the late afternoon, Venerable Ānanda came out of retreat and went to the Buddha. He bowed, sat down to one side, and said to him:

## mn121:3.1  `ms11M_497, nya3`
- **PLI**: “Ekamidaṁ, bhante, samayaṁ bhagavā sakkesu viharati nagarakaṁ nāma sakyānaṁ nigamo.
- **EN**: “Sir, this one time the Buddha was staying in the land of the Sakyans where they have a town named Townsville.

## mn121:3.2
- **PLI**: Tattha me, bhante, bhagavato sammukhā sutaṁ, sammukhā paṭiggahitaṁ:
- **EN**: There I heard and learned this in the presence of the Buddha:
- **NOTE**: “I heard and learned this in the presence” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>sammukhā sutaṁ, sammukhā paṭiggahitaṁ</i>) is the phrase used in the suttas when reporting a teaching heard directly from the Buddha, eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn55.52/en/sujato#5.1'>SN 55.52:5.1</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn22.90/en/sujato#9.1'>SN 22.90:9.1</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn47/en/sujato#10.7'>MN 47:10.7</a>, etc.

## mn121:3.3
- **PLI**: ‘suññatāvihārenāhaṁ, ānanda, etarahi bahulaṁ viharāmī’ti.
- **EN**: ‘Ānanda, these days I usually practice the meditation on emptiness.’
- **NOTE**: The same thing is said by Sāriputta at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn151/en/sujato#2.3'>MN 151:2.3</a>, upon which the Buddha encourages him to reflect on the purity of his almsfood. Before the Second Council, the elder monk Sabbakāmī revealed that emptiness was his main practice too (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd22/en/sujato#2.5.9'>Kd 22:2.5.9</a>), while at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/thig3.3/en/sujato#2.1'>Thig 3.3:2.1</a>, the bhikkhunī Uttamā also claims to practice emptiness meditation. The release through emptiness is classified along with absorption as a superhuman attainment (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-bu-vb-pj4/en/sujato#4.1.6'>Bu Pj 4:4.1.6</a>). At <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn43/en/sujato#33.1'>MN 43:33.1</a> = <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn41.7/en/sujato#4.1'>SN 41.7:4.1</a>, the “release of the heart through emptiness” is a reflection on not-self (see too <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn35.85/en/sujato#1.4'>SN 35.85:1.4</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an9.36/en/sujato#2.4'>AN 9.36:2.4</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/snp5.16/en/sujato#4.1'>Snp 5.16:4.1</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/thag19.1/en/sujato#27.2'>Thag 19.1:27.2</a>).

## mn121:3.4
- **PLI**: Kacci metaṁ, bhante, sussutaṁ suggahitaṁ sumanasikataṁ sūpadhāritan”ti?
- **EN**: I trust I properly heard, learned, applied the mind, and remembered that from the Buddha?”
- **NOTE**: Notice the process of learning teachings through active engagement. Simply “hearing” it is just the beginning.

## mn121:3.5  `cck14.213, dr13.208, ms11M_498, ndp11.169, pts-vp-pli3.105, sya14.227, vri14.149`
- **PLI**: “Taggha te etaṁ, ānanda, sussutaṁ suggahitaṁ sumanasikataṁ sūpadhāritaṁ.
- **EN**: “Indeed, Ānanda, you properly heard, learned, applied the mind, and remembered that.

## mn121:3.6
- **PLI**: Pubbepāhaṁ, ānanda, etarahipi suññatāvihārena bahulaṁ viharāmi.
- **EN**: Now, as before, I usually practice the meditation on emptiness.

## mn121:4.1  `nya4`
- **PLI**: Seyyathāpi, ānanda, ayaṁ migāramātupāsādo suñño hatthigavassavaḷavena, suñño jātarūparajatena, suñño itthipurisasannipātena atthi cevidaṁ asuññataṁ yadidaṁ—
- **EN**: Consider this stilt longhouse of Migāra’s mother. It’s empty of elephants, cows, horses, and mares; of gold and silver; and of gatherings of men and women.
- **NOTE**: Emptiness is not a metaphysical property, but a relative state: something is empty of something.

## mn121:4.2
- **PLI**: bhikkhusaṅghaṁ paṭicca ekattaṁ;
- **EN**: There is only this that is not emptiness, namely, the oneness dependent on the mendicant Saṅgha.
- **NOTE**: Oneness, like emptiness, is a relative concept. Those things that are more distracting and tempting are absent, leaving only that which is more peaceful.

## mn121:4.3
- **PLI**: evameva kho, ānanda, bhikkhu amanasikaritvā gāmasaññaṁ, amanasikaritvā manussasaññaṁ, araññasaññaṁ paṭicca manasi karoti ekattaṁ.
- **EN**: In the same way, a mendicant—ignoring the perception of the village and the perception of people—focuses on the oneness dependent on the perception of wilderness.

## mn121:4.4
- **PLI**: Tassa araññasaññāya cittaṁ pakkhandati pasīdati santiṭṭhati adhimuccati.
- **EN**: Their mind leaps forth, gains confidence, settles down, and becomes decided in that perception of wilderness.

## mn121:4.5
- **PLI**: So evaṁ pajānāti:
- **EN**: They understand:

## mn121:4.6
- **PLI**: ‘ye assu darathā gāmasaññaṁ paṭicca tedha na santi, ye assu darathā manussasaññaṁ paṭicca tedha na santi, atthi cevāyaṁ darathamattā yadidaṁ—
- **EN**: ‘Here there is no stress due to the perception of village or the perception of people.
- **NOTE**: “Stress” is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>darathā</i>.

## mn121:4.7
- **PLI**: araññasaññaṁ paṭicca ekattan’ti.
- **EN**: There is only this modicum of stress, namely the oneness dependent on the perception of wilderness.’

## mn121:4.8
- **PLI**: So ‘suññamidaṁ saññāgataṁ gāmasaññāyā’ti pajānāti, ‘suññamidaṁ saññāgataṁ manussasaññāyā’ti pajānāti, ‘atthi cevidaṁ asuññataṁ yadidaṁ—
- **EN**: They understand: ‘This field of perception is empty of the perception of the village. It is empty of the perception of people.

## mn121:4.9
- **PLI**: araññasaññaṁ paṭicca ekattan’ti.
- **EN**: There is only this that is not emptiness, namely the oneness dependent on the perception of wilderness.’

## mn121:4.10
- **PLI**: Iti yañhi kho tattha na hoti tena taṁ suññaṁ samanupassati, yaṁ pana tattha avasiṭṭhaṁ hoti taṁ ‘santamidaṁ atthī’ti pajānāti.
- **EN**: And so they regard it as empty of what is not there, but as to what remains they understand that it is present.
- **NOTE**: That is to say, the “purity” of the emptiness is not because everything is empty, but because what is empty is accurately recognized as empty.

## mn121:4.11
- **PLI**: Evampissa esā, ānanda, yathābhuccā avipallatthā parisuddhā suññatāvakkanti bhavati.
- **EN**: That’s how emptiness manifests in them—genuine, undistorted, and pure.
