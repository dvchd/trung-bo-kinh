## mn33:2.1  `cck12.391, csp1ed9.282, csp2ed9.282, ms9M_1157, nya2, sya12.411`
- **PLI**: “Ekādasahi, bhikkhave, aṅgehi samannāgato gopālako abhabbo gogaṇaṁ pariharituṁ phātiṁ kātuṁ.
- **EN**: “Mendicants, a cowherd with eleven factors can’t maintain and expand a herd of cattle.
- **NOTE**: Since organizing teachings by number is the hallmark of the Aṅguttara Nikāya, it comes as no surprise to see this pattern repeated in the Book of the Elevens at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an11.17/en/sujato'>AN 11.17</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an11.22-29/en/sujato'>AN 11.22–29</a>, and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an11.502-981/en/sujato'>AN 11.502–981</a>.

## mn33:2.2
- **PLI**: Katamehi ekādasahi?
- **EN**: What eleven?

## mn33:2.3
- **PLI**: Idha, bhikkhave, gopālako na rūpaññū hoti, na lakkhaṇakusalo hoti, na āsāṭikaṁ hāretā hoti, na vaṇaṁ paṭicchādetā hoti, na dhūmaṁ kattā hoti, na titthaṁ jānāti, na pītaṁ jānāti, na vīthiṁ jānāti, na gocarakusalo hoti anavasesadohī ca hoti. Ye te usabhā gopitaro gopariṇāyakā te na atirekapūjāya pūjetā hoti.
- **EN**: It’s when a cowherd doesn’t know form, is unskilled in characteristics, doesn’t pick out flies’ eggs, doesn’t dress wounds, doesn’t spread smoke, doesn’t know the ford, doesn’t know satisfaction, doesn’t know the trail, is not skilled in ranges, milks dry, and doesn’t show extra respect to the bulls who are fathers and captains of the herd.
- **NOTE**: “Knowing form” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>rūpaññū</i>) means being able to keep track of the herd by reckoning or recognizing them. A better translation would be “appearance”, but I use “form” for consistency. | “Ford” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>tittha</i>) is a way across the river. | “Spreads smoke” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>dhūmaṁ kattā</i>) is the traditional practice of warding off flies and other pests by burning cow dung, etc. | “Satisfy” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>pīta</i>) is less literal than “drink”, but clarifies the simile later on.

## mn33:9.1  `ms9M_1163, ndp9.272, nya9, vri12.286`

- **EN**: And how does a mendicant not know the ford?
- **NOTE**: A <i lang='pi' translate='no'>tittha</i> (“ford”) is a path to salvation, usually used of non-Buddhist religions, whose founders are “ford-makers” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>titthakāra</i>).

## mn33:9.2
- **PLI**: Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu ye te bhikkhū bahussutā āgatāgamā dhammadharā vinayadharā mātikādharā, te kālena kālaṁ upasaṅkamitvā na paripucchati, na paripañhati:
- **EN**: It’s when a mendicant doesn’t from time to time go up to those mendicants who are very learned—inheritors of the heritage, who have memorized the teachings, the monastic law, and the outlines—and ask them questions:
- **NOTE**: “Inheritors of the heritage” is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>āgatāgamā</i>, where <i lang='pi' translate='no'>āgama</i> means “what has come down”, namely the scriptural heritage. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Āgama</i> is a synonym for <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nikāya</i> in the sense of “collection of scripture”. | The “outlines” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>mātikā</i>, literally “matrix”) are the summary outlines of topics that served as seeds for the development of Abhidhamma. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn16/en/sujato#3.50.5'>DN 16:3.50.5</a> features one of the earliest of such lists, the 37 path factors that the Buddha “taught from his own direct knowledge”. These serve as outline for the section on the path in the Saṁyutta, from where they were adopted in various Abhidhamma texts such as the Vibhaṅga.
