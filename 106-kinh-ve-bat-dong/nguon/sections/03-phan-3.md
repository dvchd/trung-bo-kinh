## mn106:12.1  `csp1ed11.52, csp2ed11.52, ms11M_146, msdiv72, nya12`
- **PLI**: Idhānanda, bhikkhu evaṁ paṭipanno hoti:
- **EN**: Take a mendicant who practices like this:

## mn106:12.2
- **PLI**: ‘no cassa, no ca me siyā; na bhavissati, na me bhavissati; yadatthi, yaṁ bhūtaṁ—taṁ pajahāmī’ti.
- **EN**: ‘It might not be, and it might not be mine. It will not be, and it will not be mine. I am giving up what exists, what has come to be.’

## mn106:12.3
- **PLI**: Evaṁ upekkhaṁ paṭilabhati.
- **EN**: In this way they gain equanimity.

## mn106:12.4
- **PLI**: So taṁ upekkhaṁ nābhinandati, nābhivadati, na ajjhosāya tiṭṭhati.
- **EN**: They don’t approve, welcome, or keep clinging to that equanimity.

## mn106:12.5
- **PLI**: Tassa taṁ upekkhaṁ anabhinandato anabhivadato anajjhosāya tiṭṭhato na tannissitaṁ hoti viññāṇaṁ na tadupādānaṁ.
- **EN**: So their consciousness doesn’t have that as support and fuel for grasping.

## mn106:12.6
- **PLI**: Anupādāno, ānanda, bhikkhu parinibbāyatī”ti.
- **EN**: A mendicant free of grasping becomes extinguished.”

## mn106:13.1  `cck14.76, dr13.73, ms11M_147, msdiv73, nya13, pts-vp-pli2.266, sya14.80`
- **PLI**: “Acchariyaṁ, bhante, abbhutaṁ, bhante.
- **EN**: “It’s incredible, sir, it’s amazing!

## mn106:13.2
- **PLI**: Nissāya nissāya kira no, bhante, bhagavatā oghassa nittharaṇā akkhātā.
- **EN**: It seems the Buddha has explained to us how to cross over the flood by relying on one support or another.
- **NOTE**: This passage shows how the method of attaining such states is secondary, as one might practice it well and yet still have clinging. Any of these “supports”, i.e. the various methods taught above, suffices to develop the mind, but what matters is letting go. | “One support or another” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>nissāya nissāya</i>) appears at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn16/en/sujato#4.27.2'>DN 16:4.27.2</a>, where it means “right beside”. For the sense “support” in this context, compare <a href='https://suttacentral.net/iti107/en/sujato#2.3'>Iti 107:2.3</a>: “supporting each other … in order to cross over the flood” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>aññamaññaṁ nissāya … oghassa nittharaṇatthāya</i>).

## mn106:13.3
- **PLI**: Katamo pana, bhante, ariyo vimokkho”ti?
- **EN**: But sir, what is noble liberation?”

## mn106:13.4
- **PLI**: “Idhānanda, bhikkhu ariyasāvako iti paṭisañcikkhati:
- **EN**: “Ānanda, it’s when a noble disciple reflects like this:

- **PLI**: ‘ye ca diṭṭhadhammikā kāmā, ye ca samparāyikā kāmā;

## mn106:13.6
- **PLI**: yā ca diṭṭhadhammikā kāmasaññā, yā ca samparāyikā kāmasaññā;
- **EN**: sensual perceptions in this life and in lives to come,

## mn106:13.7
- **PLI**: ye ca diṭṭhadhammikā rūpā, ye ca samparāyikā rūpā;
- **EN**: visions in this life and in lives to come,

## mn106:13.8
- **PLI**: yā ca diṭṭhadhammikā rūpasaññā, yā ca samparāyikā rūpasaññā;
- **EN**: perceptions of visions in this life and in lives to come,

## mn106:13.9
- **PLI**: yā ca āneñjasaññā, yā ca ākiñcaññāyatanasaññā, yā ca nevasaññānāsaññāyatanasaññā—

## mn106:13.10

## mn106:13.11

- **EN**: But this is freedom from death, namely the liberation of the mind by not grasping.’

## mn106:14.1  `nya14`
- **PLI**: Iti, kho, ānanda, desitā mayā āneñjasappāyā paṭipadā, desitā ākiñcaññāyatanasappāyā paṭipadā, desitā nevasaññānāsaññāyatanasappāyā paṭipadā, desitā nissāya nissāya oghassa nittharaṇā, desito ariyo vimokkho.
- **EN**: So, Ānanda, I have taught the ways of practice suitable for attaining the imperturbable, the dimension of nothingness, and the dimension of neither perception nor non-perception. I have taught how to cross the flood by relying on one support or another, and I have taught noble liberation.
