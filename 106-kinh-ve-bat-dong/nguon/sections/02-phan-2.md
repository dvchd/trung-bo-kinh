## mn106:6.6

## mn106:6.7

## mn106:6.8

## mn106:6.10
- **PLI**: Tassa evaṁpaṭipannassa tabbahulavihārino āyatane cittaṁ pasīdati.
- **EN**: Practicing in this way and meditating on it often their mind becomes confident in this dimension.

- **EN**: Being confident, they either attain the dimension of nothingness now, or are freed by wisdom.

## mn106:6.12

- **EN**: When their body breaks up, after death, it’s possible that that conducive consciousness will be reborn in the dimension of nothingness.

## mn106:6.13
- **PLI**: Ayaṁ, bhikkhave, paṭhamā ākiñcaññāyatanasappāyā paṭipadā akkhāyati.
- **EN**: This is said to be the first way of practice suitable for attaining the dimension of nothingness.

## mn106:10.1  `bj12.88, cck14.75, dr13.72, ms11M_145, msdiv71, ndp11.60, nya10, pts-vp-pli2.265, sya14.79, vri14.49`
- **PLI**: Evaṁ vutte, āyasmā ānando bhagavantaṁ etadavoca:
- **EN**: When he said this, Venerable Ānanda said to the Buddha:

## mn106:10.2
- **PLI**: “idha, bhante, bhikkhu evaṁ paṭipanno hoti:
- **EN**: “Sir, take a mendicant who practices like this:

## mn106:10.3
- **PLI**: ‘no cassa, no ca me siyā; na bhavissati, na me bhavissati; yadatthi yaṁ, bhūtaṁ—taṁ pajahāmī’ti.
- **EN**: ‘It might not be, and it might not be mine. It will not be, and it will not be mine. I am giving up what exists, what has come to be.’
- **NOTE**: See Bodhi’s extensive discussion in his translation of <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn22.55/en/sujato#1.3'>SN 22.55:1.3</a> (<em>Connected Discourses</em>, vol. iii, note 75). This formula appears several times throughout the suttas, in two forms: the personal form used by non-Buddhists (“I might not be …”, eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn22.81/en/sujato#12.8'>SN 22.81:12.8</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an10.29/en/sujato#19.1'>AN 10.29:19.1</a>), and the impersonal form as adapted by the Buddha here (“It might not be …”). The formula seems to have been shared among different sectarian groups, like the similar reflection above (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn106/en/sujato#8.2'>MN 106:8.2</a>). But whereas that reflection is associated with Brahmans and Jains—both classified as “eternalists”—this view is said to be that of the “annihilationists” (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn22.81/en/sujato#11.10'>SN 22.81:11.10</a>).
