# Phần 1  (50 đoạn)

## mn106:1.1  `bj12.82, cck14.70, dr13.67, ms11M_137, msdiv66, ndp11.56, nya1, sya14.74, vri14.46`
- **PLI**: Evaṁ me sutaṁ—
- **EN**: So I have heard.
- **NOTE**: This sutta describes practices that lead to attaining the formless meditations: three ways to attain the “imperturbable”, three ways to continue to the dimension of nothingness, and finally a way to attain neither perception nor non-perception. One theme of the text is that deep states of absorption are supported by reflective wisdom. The sutta concludes by teaching what is beyond all these, namely not grasping.

## mn106:2.2
- **PLI**: Māyākatametaṁ, bhikkhave, bālalāpanaṁ.
- **EN**: This is made by illusion, mendicants, lamented by fools.
- **NOTE**: This phrase is sometimes translated as if it was joined to the previous. However, the repeated use of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>bhikkhave</i>, the shift from plural to singular, and the independent occurrence of the former portion (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/an10.46/en/sujato#6.5'>AN 10.46:6.5</a>) all suggest it is a distinct sentence. | As for <i lang='pi' translate='no'>bālalāpana</i>, at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn22.95/en/sujato#13.2'>SN 22.95:13.2</a> we find <i lang='pi' translate='no'>bālalāpinī</i> in a similar context, which there follows right after the death of the physical body. This suggests that <i lang='pi' translate='no'>lāpinī</i> there has the sense “lament” which is attested in Sanskrit. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Bālalāpana</i> is found at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/thig5.2/en/sujato#2.2'>Thig 5.2:2.2</a> of a beautiful body; see too <a href='https://suttacentral.net/ja421/en/sujato#5.3'>Ja 421:5.3</a>. In later Sanskrit, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>bālalapitaṁ</i> is used by Jayarāśi Bhaṭṭa in his Tattvopaplavasiṁha in the sense “prattle of fools”.

## mn106:3.7
- **PLI**: Yannūnāhaṁ vipulena mahaggatena cetasā vihareyyaṁ abhibhuyya lokaṁ adhiṭṭhāya manasā.
- **EN**: Why don’t I meditate with an abundant, expansive heart, having mastered the world and stabilized the mind?

## mn106:3.8
- **PLI**: Vipulena hi me mahaggatena cetasā viharato abhibhuyya lokaṁ adhiṭṭhāya manasā ye pāpakā akusalā mānasā abhijjhāpi byāpādāpi sārambhāpi te na bhavissanti.
- **EN**: Then I will have no more bad, unskillful notions such as desire, ill will, and aggression.

## mn106:3.9
- **PLI**: Tesaṁ pahānā aparittañca me cittaṁ bhavissati appamāṇaṁ subhāvitan’ti.
- **EN**: And by giving them up my mind, no longer limited, will become limitless and well developed.’

## mn106:3.10
- **PLI**: Tassa evaṁpaṭipannassa tabbahulavihārino āyatane cittaṁ pasīdati.
- **EN**: Practicing in this way and meditating on it often their mind becomes confident in this dimension.

## mn106:3.11
- **PLI**: Sampasāde sati etarahi vā āneñjaṁ samāpajjati paññāya vā adhimuccati.
- **EN**: Being confident, they either attain the imperturbable now, or are freed by wisdom.
- **NOTE**: The Pali has <i lang='pi' translate='no'>paññāya vā adhimuccati</i> (“they resolve with wisdom”). This appears to be an old confusion between <i lang='pi' translate='no'>adhimuccati</i> (“resolves”) and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vimuccati</i> (“is freed”). Elsewhere in Pali we find one who is “freed by wisdom” (eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn22.45/en/sujato#1.6'>SN 22.45:1.6</a>) but we don’t find <i lang='pi' translate='no'>adhimuccati</i> used in this way. The commentary accepts the reading <i lang='pi' translate='no'>adhimuccati</i>, but explains it as referring to either the realization of arahantship or the development of the path to arahantship, which agrees with the idea of “freedom”. Finally, the Chinese parallel (MA 75 at T i 542b23) has <span lang='lzh'>解</span>, while the Tibetan parallel in Śamathadeva’s commentary on the Abhidharmakośabhāṣya (D (4094) mngon pa, ju 228a2) has <i lang='pi' translate='no'>rnam par grol ba</i>, both of which appear to translate <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vimuccati</i>. If this reading is accepted, it also adds greater coherence to the sutta as a whole, for after discussing the various ways to the imperturbable, the final section is devoted to the “noble liberation”, where <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vimokkha</i> is a synonym of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vimutti</i> (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn106/en/sujato#13.3'>MN 106:13.3</a>).

## mn106:3.12
- **PLI**: Kāyassa bhedā paraṁ maraṇā, ṭhānametaṁ vijjati yaṁ taṁsaṁvattanikaṁ viññāṇaṁ assa āneñjūpagaṁ.
- **EN**: When their body breaks up, after death, it’s possible that that conducive consciousness will be reborn in the imperturbable.
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Saṁvattanika</i> means “conducive, leading to”. The state of consciousness developed in meditation leads to a rebirth in a plane where the normal level of consciousness is that of the corresponding meditation attainment. Thus if they have developed the fourth absorption, they will be reborn in a corresponding Brahmā realm.

## mn106:3.13
- **PLI**: Ayaṁ, bhikkhave, paṭhamā āneñjasappāyā paṭipadā akkhāyati.
- **EN**: This is said to be the first way of practice suitable for attaining the imperturbable.
