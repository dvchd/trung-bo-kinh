## mn85:7.16
- **PLI**: na bhagavā celapaṭikaṁ akkamissati.
- **EN**: The Buddha will not step upon white cloth.
- **NOTE**: According to the commentary, Bodhi was childless. He thought that if the Buddha stepped on the cloth it would show that he would have a child in the future, whereas if he did not then he would have no child. In the commentary to <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dhp157/en/sujato'>Dhp 157</a>, the Buddha confirms to Bodhi that he will have no child. Under this explanation, then, by refusing to step on the cloth the Buddha was giving Bodhi an answer. The canonical account, however, reads as if the Buddha is simply refusing to indulge Bodhi’s superstition. Nonetheless, the fact that the Vinaya account (see below) concerns a woman who cannot have a child confirms that the blessing concerns fertility.

## mn85:7.17
- **PLI**: Pacchimaṁ janataṁ tathāgato anukampatī”ti.
- **EN**: The Realized One has sympathy for future generations.”
- **NOTE**: Pali editions vary between “has regard” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>apaloketi</i>) and “has compassion” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>anukampati</i>), while the Chinese and Sanskrit fragments agree on “compassion”. The reading <i lang='pi' translate='no'>apaloketi</i> (or <i lang='pi' translate='no'>oloketi</i> in the Dhammapada commentary) seems to have arisen to conform with the idea that the Buddha was looking into the future to see if Bodhi would have a child.
