## mn8:1.2
- **PLI**: ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṁ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme.
- **EN**: At one time the Buddha was staying near Sāvatthī in Jeta’s Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika’s monastery.
- **NOTE**: Of the two parallels to this sutta, MA 91 takes place at the Ghositārāma in Kosambī, while EA 47.9 is set in the Squirrels’ Feeding Ground at Rājagaha. The specifics of narrative backgrounds often vary; they are not “the Buddha’s word”, but rather were added by editors at some point.

## mn8:2.1  `nya2`
- **PLI**: Atha kho āyasmā mahācundo sāyanhasamayaṁ paṭisallānā vuṭṭhito yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṁ abhivādetvā ekamantaṁ nisīdi. Ekamantaṁ nisinno kho āyasmā mahācundo bhagavantaṁ etadavoca:
- **EN**: Then in the late afternoon, Venerable Mahācunda came out of retreat and went to the Buddha. He bowed, sat down to one side, and said to the Buddha:
- **NOTE**: There are various Cundas whose connection is unclear. Mahācunda was one of the great disciples (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn118/en/sujato'>MN 118</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an6.17/en/sujato'>AN 6.17</a>), who later brought the Dhamma to the land of the Cetīs (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/an6.46/en/sujato'>AN 6.46</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an10.24/en/sujato'>AN 10.24</a>). He once stayed with Channa and Sāriputta (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn144/en/sujato#4.1'>MN 144:4.1</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn35.87/en/sujato#1.2'>SN 35.87:1.2</a>); the commentaries say he was in fact Sāriputta’s younger brother. They also identify him with the “novice Cunda” who reported the deaths of Sāriputta (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn47.13/en/sujato#1.3'>SN 47.13:1.3</a>) and Mahāvīra (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn29/en/sujato#2.1'>DN 29:2.1</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn104/en/sujato#3.1'>MN 104:3.1</a>), explaining that the title “novice” was a nickname that persisted from the time he ordained as a young novice. It is unclear whether the commentaries take the Cundaka of <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn16/en/sujato#4.39.2'>DN 16:4.39.2</a> = <a href='https://suttacentral.net/ud8.5/en/sujato#14.3'>Ud 8.5:14.3</a> to be the same person, but he is there performing a similar role as carer adjacent to the Buddha’s death. Later there appears a Cūḷacunda (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/tha-ap52/en/sujato#13.3'>Tha Ap 52:13.3</a>). Thus there may have been one person known by different names, or several people whose stories have become conflated.

- **NOTE**: These are discussed many times in the suttas, for example doctrines of the self at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn2/en/sujato#8.2'>MN 2:8.2</a> and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn44/en/sujato'>MN 44</a>; of the cosmos at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn63/en/sujato'>MN 63</a> and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn72/en/sujato'>MN 72</a>; and of both at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn1/en/sujato#1.30.1'>DN 1:1.30.1</a> and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn102/en/sujato#14.1'>MN 102:14.1</a>.

## mn8:3.3
- **PLI**: ādimeva nu kho, bhante, bhikkhuno manasikaroto evametāsaṁ diṭṭhīnaṁ pahānaṁ hoti, evametāsaṁ diṭṭhīnaṁ paṭinissaggo hotī”ti?
- **EN**: How does a mendicant who is focusing on the starting point give up and let go of these views?”
- **NOTE**: The idiom <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ādimeva</i> elsewhere occurs only when the Buddha gives a mendicant instructions for a retreat. Typically (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn47.3/en/sujato#1.6'>SN 47.3:1.6</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn47.15/en/sujato#1.4'>SN 47.15:1.4</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn47.16/en/sujato#1.4'>SN 47.16:1.4</a>) he says to “purify the starting point of skillful qualities”, namely ethics (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>sīla</i>) and views (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>diṭṭhi</i>), on the latter of which Mahācunda is seeking clarification. | In the Pali the question is phrased directly and is sometimes translated “do they give them up?”, but the idiom <i lang='pi' translate='no'>evametāsaṁ</i> shifts the sense to “how do they give them up?” (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn74/en/sujato#6.14'>MN 74:6.14</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn137/en/sujato#16.8'>MN 137:16.8</a>). And that is indeed the answer the Buddha gives. The commentary explains it similarly (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>etena upāyena etāsaṁ</i>), and both Chinese texts have <span lang='lzh'>云何</span> here, which typically stands for <i lang='pi' translate='no'>kathaṁ</i> (“how”).

## mn8:3.6
- **PLI**: yattha cetā diṭṭhiyo uppajjanti yattha ca anusenti yattha ca samudācaranti taṁ ‘netaṁ mama, nesohamasmi, na me so attā’ti—evametaṁ yathābhūtaṁ sammappaññā passato evametāsaṁ diṭṭhīnaṁ pahānaṁ hoti, evametāsaṁ diṭṭhīnaṁ paṭinissaggo hoti.
- **EN**: A mendicant gives up and lets go of these views by truly seeing with right wisdom where they arise, where they settle in, and where they operate as: ‘This is not mine, I am not this, this is not my self.’
- **NOTE**: This is the insight of stream-entry, where wrong views are fully abandoned by means of the clarified wisdom that sees the truth of conditionality. The remainder of the sutta discusses the practice leading to this point.

## mn8:4.1  `ms9M_194, nya4, pts-vp-pli1.41, sya12.73`
- **PLI**: Ṭhānaṁ kho panetaṁ, cunda, vijjati yaṁ idhekacco bhikkhu vivicceva kāmehi vivicca akusalehi dhammehi savitakkaṁ savicāraṁ vivekajaṁ pītisukhaṁ paṭhamaṁ jhānaṁ upasampajja vihareyya.
- **EN**: It’s possible that a certain mendicant, quite secluded from sensual pleasures, secluded from unskillful qualities, might enter and remain in the first absorption, which has the rapture and bliss born of seclusion, while placing the mind and keeping it connected.

## mn8:4.2
- **PLI**: Tassa evamassa:
- **EN**: They might think

## mn8:4.3
- **PLI**: ‘sallekhena viharāmī’ti.
- **EN**: they’re practicing self-effacement.
- **NOTE**: The term “self-effacement” that lends the sutta its title is Pali <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sallekha</i>, which has the sense of “rubbing out” (an inscription or mark). It is associated with such virtues as contentment, seclusion, and simplicity (eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn3/en/sujato#3.25'>MN 3:3.25</a>). Here it refers to the strength of character to persist in “effacing” unwholesome qualities through developing their opposites, no matter what others might do. | Not to be confused with the Jain <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sallekhanā</i>, where practitioners at the end of life refuse all food (Tattvārthasūtra 7.22). In fact, this sutta appears to emphasize the gentle nature of the Buddha’s approach in deliberate contrast with the stern austerities of the Jains.

## mn8:4.4
- **PLI**: Na kho panete, cunda, ariyassa vinaye sallekhā vuccanti.
- **EN**: But in the training of the Noble One these are not called ‘self-effacement’;

## mn8:4.5
- **PLI**: Diṭṭhadhammasukhavihārā ete ariyassa vinaye vuccanti.
- **EN**: they’re called ‘blissful meditations in this life’.
- **NOTE**: The sutta is not deprecating the <i lang='pi' translate='no'>jhānas</i>, for they are a fundamental part of the early Buddhist path, and as such are included later under the heading of “right immersion” (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn8/en/sujato#12.19'>MN 8:12.19</a>). Indeed, the sutta ends with the Buddha encouraging Cunda to practice <i lang='pi' translate='no'>jhāna</i>. Rather, this text emphasizes the need to actively develop all aspects of the path, humble or exalted, and integrate them in every aspect of life, rather than solely relying on meditation in seclusion.
