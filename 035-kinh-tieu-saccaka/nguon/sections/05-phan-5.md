- **EN**: “But how do you define a mendicant who is a perfected one, with defilements ended, who has completed the spiritual journey, done what had to be done, laid down the burden, achieved their heart’s goal, utterly ended the fetter of continued existence, and is rightly freed through enlightenment?”

## mn35:25.2

- **EN**: “It’s when a mendicant truly sees any kind of form at all—past, future, or present; internal or external; solid or subtle; inferior or superior; far or near: <em>all</em> form—with right understanding: ‘This is not mine, I am not this, this is not my self.’ And having seen this with right understanding they’re freed by not grasping.

## mn35:26.1  `nya26`
- **PLI**: Evaṁ vimuttacitto kho, aggivessana, bhikkhu tīhi anuttariyehi samannāgato hoti—
- **EN**: A mendicant whose mind is freed like this has three unsurpassable qualities:

## mn35:26.2
- **PLI**: dassanānuttariyena, paṭipadānuttariyena, vimuttānuttariyena.
- **EN**: unsurpassable seeing, practice, and freedom.
- **NOTE**: Also at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn33/en/sujato#1.10.119'>DN 33:1.10.119</a>, which is doubtless lifted from the current passage. A list of six unsurpassables is found at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an6.30/en/sujato'>AN 6.30</a>, but the only common item is “seeing” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>dassana</i>), which is defined as seeing the Buddha and his disciples. Here “seeing” is either seeing the Buddha which gives rise to faith, or else “seeing” the four noble truths; practice is the noble eightfold path; and freedom is the result of that path, arahantship. | As to why this unique set is chosen here, it is perhaps notable that three of the final similes of Uddālaka’s “subtlest” self (as above, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn35/en/sujato#20.10'>MN 35:20.10</a>) in the Chāndogya Upaniṣad speak of <em>seeing</em> (the kernel of a seed, 6.12.1–2), <em>walking</em> (from one village to the next, 6.14.2), and <em>freedom</em> (from punishment, 6.16.2).

## mn35:26.3
- **PLI**: Evaṁ vimuttacitto kho, aggivessana, bhikkhu tathāgataññeva sakkaroti garuṁ karoti māneti pūjeti—
- **EN**: They honor, respect, esteem, and venerate only the Realized One:

## mn35:26.4
- **PLI**: buddho so bhagavā bodhāya dhammaṁ deseti, danto so bhagavā damathāya dhammaṁ deseti, santo so bhagavā samathāya dhammaṁ deseti, tiṇṇo so bhagavā taraṇāya dhammaṁ deseti, parinibbuto so bhagavā parinibbānāya dhammaṁ desetī”ti.
- **EN**: ‘The Blessed One is awakened, tamed, serene, crossed over, and quenched. And he teaches Dhamma for awakening, self-control, serenity, crossing over, and extinguishment.’”

## mn35:30.5
- **PLI**: “yamidaṁ, bho gotama, dāne puññañca puññamahī ca taṁ dāyakānaṁ sukhāya hotū”ti.
- **EN**: “Worthy Gotama, may the merit and the growth of merit in this gift be for the happiness of the donors.”
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Puññamahī</i> (“flourishing of merit”), for which there are several variants in text and commentary, is found only here and at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an7.53/en/sujato#6.10'>AN 7.53:6.10</a>, where it also concerns the dedication of merit to another. It might refer to the glorious results of merit, for which compare Chāndogya Upaniṣad 8.2, “endowed with that (divine fruit for which he wished) he flourishes (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>tena sampanno mahīyate</i>). Or else it might invoke the idea that merit flourishes depending on the recipient (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn142/en/sujato'>MN 142</a>), like a seed on the earth (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>mahī</i>; cp. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>puññakkhetta</i>), which fits nicely with the Buddha’s response here. | Bhikkhu Bodhi’s remark that Saccaka “must have still considered himself a saint” is unwarranted, as the same phrase at AN 7.53 is used by a devoted lay follower. Clearly he was arrogant, but it was others who called him <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sādhu</i>.

- **EN**: “Aggivessana, whatever comes from giving to a recipient of a religious donation such as yourself—who is not free of greed, hate, and delusion—will accrue to the donors.
