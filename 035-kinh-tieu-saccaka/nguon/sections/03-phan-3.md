## mn35:20.10
- **PLI**: ‘etaṁ mama, esohamasmi, eso me attā’”ti?
- **EN**: ‘This is mine, I am this, this is my self’?”
- **NOTE**: This directly rejects all forms of self, but especially the highest conception of self as expressed in the Upaniṣads. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Etaṁ mama</i> occurs as <i lang='pi' translate='no'>mamedaṁ</i> (“this is mine”, Maitrī Upaniṣad 3.2), which in context is a limited view of self. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Eso’hamasmi</i> is Yājñavalkya’s <i lang='pi' translate='no'>so’hamasmi</i> (“I am that”, Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 1.4.1; BU 5.15.1 = Īśā Upaniṣad 16; Chāndogya Upaniṣad 4.11.1, 4.12.1, 4.13.1; Śāṅkhāyana Āraṇyaka 3.6), which Uddālaka’s <i lang='pi' translate='no'>tattvamasi</i> puts in second person (Chāndogya Upaniṣad 6.8.7). The same passage also parallels <i lang='pi' translate='no'>eso me attā</i> with <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sa ātmā</i> (“that is the self”), for which we also find <i lang='pi' translate='no'>eṣa ma ātmā</i> (CU 3.14.3), and in the optative mood, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ma idaṁ syād ātman</i> (BU 1.2.7).
