## mn117:1.1  `bj12.212, cck14.170, dr13.164, ms11M_389, msdiv136, ndp11.135, nya1, sya14.180, vri14.118`
- **PLI**: Evaṁ me sutaṁ—
- **EN**: So I have heard.
- **NOTE**: This is one of the two major treatments of the noble eightfold path in the Middle Discourses, the other being <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn141/en/sujato'>MN 141</a>, where each factor is defined in detail. This discourse emphasizes the interrelationships between the factors, especially as they support right immersion. The meaning of the title is explained in the final passage.

## mn117:3.1  `ms11M_390, nya3`
- **PLI**: “Katamo ca, bhikkhave, ariyo sammāsamādhi saupaniso saparikkhāro?
- **EN**: “And what is noble right immersion with its vital conditions and its prerequisites?

## mn117:3.2
- **PLI**: Seyyathidaṁ—sammādiṭṭhi, sammāsaṅkappo, sammāvācā, sammākammanto, sammāājīvo, sammāvāyāmo, sammāsati;
- **EN**: They are: right view, right purpose, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, and right mindfulness.

## mn117:3.3
- **PLI**: yā kho, bhikkhave, imehi sattahaṅgehi cittassa ekaggatā parikkhatā—
- **EN**: Unification of mind with these seven factors as prerequisites

## mn117:3.4
- **PLI**: ayaṁ vuccati, bhikkhave, ariyo sammāsamādhi saupaniso itipi, saparikkhāro itipi.
- **EN**: is called noble right immersion ‘with its vital conditions’ and also ‘with its prerequisites’.
- **NOTE**: While it is possible to obtain immersion outside the context of the noble eightfold path, without all the factors of the path working together it does not result in liberation and hence is not “right” immersion. | This became known in the Abhidhamma as <i lang='pi' translate='no'>lokuttara jhāna</i>, where it is used in the same sense as the sutta: a meditative absorption which, empowered by all the path factors in concert, leads to the realization of the Dhamma. This is contrasted with the <i lang='pi' translate='no'>lokiya jhāna</i> that leads only to rebirth in the Brahmā realm; see comment on <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn7/en/sujato#19.6'>MN 7:19.6</a>. Later, the commentarial tradition analyzed it further as a series of mind moments that occur at the occasion of realization.

## mn117:6.1  `csp1ed11.117, csp2ed11.117, ms11M_392, ndp11.136, nya6, vri14.119`
- **PLI**: Katamā ca, bhikkhave, sammādiṭṭhi?
- **EN**: And what is right view?

## mn117:6.2
- **PLI**: Sammādiṭṭhimpahaṁ, bhikkhave, dvāyaṁ vadāmi—
- **EN**: Right view is twofold, I say.

## mn117:6.3
- **PLI**: atthi, bhikkhave, sammādiṭṭhi sāsavā puññabhāgiyā upadhivepakkā;
- **EN**: There is right view that is accompanied by defilements, partakes of good deeds, and ripens in attachments.
- **NOTE**: This unique phrase refers to the kind of right view that results in a good rebirth but not liberation. This kind of right view is not specifically Buddhist. Compare <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sāsavaṁ upādāniyaṁ</i> (the five grasping aggregates “accompanied by defilements and prone to being grasped”, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn22.48/en/sujato#2.2'>SN 22.48:2.2</a>) and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>iddhi sāsavā saupadhikā, ‘no ariyā’ti</i> (“psychic powers that are accompanied by defilements and attachments, and are said to be ignoble”, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn28/en/sujato#18.3'>DN 28:18.3</a>). | For “partakes of good deeds” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>puññabhāgiyā</i>), see <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an6.63/en/sujato'>AN 6.63</a>, where it refers to a rebirth that results from “defilement”.

## mn117:6.4
- **PLI**: atthi, bhikkhave, sammādiṭṭhi ariyā anāsavā lokuttarā maggaṅgā.
- **EN**: And there is right view that is noble, undefiled, transcendent, a factor of the path.
- **NOTE**: This is the right view of one on the noble path. The suttas do not elsewhere distinguish these two kinds of path factors, and the “transcendent” analysis is not found in the parallels in Chinese (MA 189 at T i 735b) or Tibetan (Up 6.080 at D 4094 mngon pa, nyu 43b7). A similar distinction, however, is made in the Chinese translation of the Saṁyukta Āgama of the (Mūla)-Sarvāstivāda at SA 785 and SA 789, which contains analyses of the transcendent factors that are similar to those in Pali. | “Transcendent” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>lokuttara</i>) means “leading beyond the world (of transmigration)”. Elsewhere in early texts it is used of discourses (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn20.7/en/sujato#1.6'>SN 20.7:1.6</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn55.53/en/sujato#2.2'>SN 55.53:2.2</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an2.47/en/sujato#1.5'>AN 2.47:1.5</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an5.79/en/sujato#5.2'>AN 5.79:5.2</a>; see too <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn96/en/sujato#12.12'>MN 96:12.12</a>), and of certain reflective realizations of a noble one (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn48/en/sujato#14.7'>MN 48:14.7</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn122/en/sujato#18.1'>MN 122:18.1</a>).

## mn117:8.1  `bj12.214, cck14.172, dr13.166, ms11M_393, nya8, sya14.182`
- **PLI**: Katamā ca, bhikkhave, sammādiṭṭhi ariyā anāsavā lokuttarā maggaṅgā?
- **EN**: And what is right view that is noble, undefiled, transcendent, a factor of the path?

## mn117:8.2
- **PLI**: Yā kho, bhikkhave, ariyacittassa anāsavacittassa ariyamaggasamaṅgino ariyamaggaṁ bhāvayato paññā paññindriyaṁ paññābalaṁ dhammavicayasambojjhaṅgo sammādiṭṭhi maggaṅgaṁ—
- **EN**: It’s the wisdom—the faculty of wisdom, the power of wisdom, the awakening factor of investigation of principles, and right view as a factor of the path—in one intent on the noble, intent on the undefiled, who possesses the noble path and develops the noble path.
- **NOTE**: Whereas elsewhere right view is explained in terms of <em>what</em> you understand (the four noble truths <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn141/en/sujato#24.1'>MN 141:24.1</a>), this analysis shifts focus to <em>how</em> you understand: wisdom functions in the context of the path as a whole. Later texts expand on this (eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mnd2/en/sujato#82.4'>Mnd 2:82.4</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/ne17/en/sujato#17.3'>Ne 17:17.3</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/ds2.1.1/en/sujato#28.2'>Ds 2.1.1:28.2</a>). | As to the phrase “intent on the noble, intent on the undefiled”, the expressions <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ariyacitta</i> and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>anāsavacitta</i> are unique. It is tempting to translate <i lang='pi' translate='no'>anāsavacitta</i> as “undefiled mind”, but that would pertain only to the arahant who has completed the path, whereas this is talking about one still in the process of developing the path. Here <i lang='pi' translate='no'>citta</i> must mean “intention” (eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn120/en/sujato#3.4'>MN 120:3.4</a>; cf. the later term <i lang='pi' translate='no'>bodhicitta</i>). Commentary is silent.

## mn117:9.1  `nya9`

- **EN**: They make an effort to give up wrong view and embrace right view: that’s their right effort.

## mn117:9.2

- **EN**: Mindfully they give up wrong view and embrace right view: that’s their right mindfulness.

## mn117:9.3
- **PLI**: Itiyime tayo dhammā sammādiṭṭhiṁ anuparidhāvanti anuparivattanti, seyyathidaṁ—sammādiṭṭhi, sammāvāyāmo, sammāsati.
- **EN**: So these three things keep running and circling around right view, namely: right view, right effort, and right mindfulness.
- **NOTE**: The path is linear in that each factor depends on the previous (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn117/en/sujato#34.3'>MN 117:34.3</a>, see too eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn45.1/en/sujato'>SN 45.1</a>). But it is not <em>just</em> linear, as the factors are always interacting. Right view understands what to do; right effort does the work; and right mindfulness monitors and checks.
