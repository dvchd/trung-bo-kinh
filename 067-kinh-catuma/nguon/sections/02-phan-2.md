## mn67:16.1  `bj11.212, cck13.205, dr13.181, ms10M_430, msdiv162, ndp10.145, nya16, sya13.199`
- **PLI**: Katamañca, bhikkhave, ūmibhayaṁ?
- **EN**: And what, mendicants, is the danger of waves?
- **NOTE**: Indian literature uses the wave as metaphor for a “surge” of disturbing emotion.

## mn67:16.2
- **PLI**: Idha, bhikkhave, ekacco kulaputto saddhā agārasmā anagāriyaṁ pabbajito hoti:
- **EN**: It’s when a gentleman has gone forth out of faith from the lay life to homelessness, thinking:

## mn67:16.3
- **PLI**: ‘otiṇṇomhi jātiyā jarāya maraṇena sokehi paridevehi dukkhehi domanassehi upāyāsehi dukkhotiṇṇo dukkhapareto;
- **EN**: ‘I’m swamped by rebirth, old age, and death; by sorrow, lamentation, pain, sadness, and distress. I’m swamped by suffering, mired in suffering.

## mn67:16.4
- **PLI**: appeva nāma imassa kevalassa dukkhakkhandhassa antakiriyā paññāyethā’ti.
- **EN**: Hopefully I can make an end to this entire mass of suffering.’

## mn67:16.5
- **PLI**: Tamenaṁ tathā pabbajitaṁ samānaṁ sabrahmacārī ovadanti, anusāsanti:
- **EN**: When they’ve gone forth, their spiritual companions advise and instruct them:

## mn67:16.6
- **PLI**: ‘evaṁ te abhikkamitabbaṁ, evaṁ te paṭikkamitabbaṁ, evaṁ te ālokitabbaṁ, evaṁ te vilokitabbaṁ, evaṁ te samiñjitabbaṁ, evaṁ te pasāritabbaṁ, evaṁ te saṅghāṭipattacīvaraṁ dhāretabban’ti.
- **EN**: ‘You should go out like this, and come back like that. You should look to the front like this, and to the side like that. You should contract your limbs like this, and extend them like that. This is how you should bear your outer robe, bowl, and robes.’
- **NOTE**: These activities are elsewhere framed as the practice of “situational awareness” (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn10/en/sujato#8.1'>MN 10:8.1</a>).

## mn67:16.7
- **PLI**: Tassa evaṁ hoti:
- **EN**: They think:

## mn67:16.8
- **PLI**: ‘mayaṁ kho pubbe agāriyabhūtā samānā aññe ovadāma, anusāsāma.
- **EN**: ‘Formerly, as laypeople, we advised and instructed others.
- **NOTE**: When challenged, he uses the plural as a defense mechanism, disguising his personal reaction as an undefined “us”, implicating the Saṅgha as a whole.

## mn67:16.9
- **PLI**: Ime panamhākaṁ puttamattā maññe, nattamattā maññe, amhe ovaditabbaṁ anusāsitabbaṁ maññantī’ti.
- **EN**: And now these mendicants—who you’d think were our children or grandchildren—imagine they can advise and instruct us!’

## mn67:16.10
- **PLI**: So sikkhaṁ paccakkhāya hīnāyāvattati.
- **EN**: They resign the training and return to a lesser life.

## mn67:16.11
- **PLI**: Ayaṁ vuccati, bhikkhave, ūmibhayassa bhīto sikkhaṁ paccakkhāya hīnāyāvatto.
- **EN**: This is called a mendicant who resigns the training and returns to a lesser life for fear of the danger of waves.

## mn67:16.12
- **PLI**: ‘Ūmibhayan’ti kho, bhikkhave, kodhupāyāsassetaṁ adhivacanaṁ.
- **EN**: ‘Danger of waves’ is a term for anger and distress.

## mn67:17.1  `cck13.206, csp1ed10.124, csp2ed10.124, dr13.182, mc2.135, ms10M_431, msdiv163, ndp10.146, nya17, pts-vp-pli1.461, sya13.200, vri13.133`
- **PLI**: Katamañca, bhikkhave, kumbhīlabhayaṁ?
- **EN**: And what, mendicants, is the danger of gharials?
- **NOTE**: The gharial is a large crocodilian of the Ganges. The adult male has a distinctive “pot” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>ghara</i>) at the tip of its snout, from which it takes its name. The Pali <i lang='pi' translate='no'>kumbhīla</i> (Sanskrit <i lang='pi' translate='no'>kumbhīra</i>) similarly stems from <i lang='pi' translate='no'>kumbha</i>, “pot”. Gharials can be seen breaking water to swallow fish, their long toothy snouts making them an apt symbol for gluttony. | Referenced by the nun Sumedhā in her verses at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/thig16.1/en/sujato#55.3'>Thig 16.1:55.3</a>.
