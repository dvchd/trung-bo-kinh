## mn5:2.1  `bj10.56, cck12.40, dr12.38, ms9M_124, nya2, pts-vp-pli1.25, sya12.43`
- **PLI**: “Cattārome, āvuso, puggalā santo saṁvijjamānā lokasmiṁ.
- **EN**: “Reverends, these four individuals are found in the world.
- **NOTE**: This discourse emphasizes how even small, apparently trivial defects betray a deeper corruption. Rather than hiding them away in shame, they will only be healed when brought to light.

## mn5:2.2
- **PLI**: Katame cattāro?
- **EN**: What four?

## mn5:2.3
- **PLI**: Idhāvuso, ekacco puggalo sāṅgaṇova samāno ‘atthi me ajjhattaṁ aṅgaṇan’ti yathābhūtaṁ nappajānāti.
- **EN**: One individual with a blemish doesn’t truly understand: ‘There is a blemish in me.’
- **NOTE**: “Blemish” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>aṅgaṇa</i>) is used literally as a mark or discoloration on the face (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn15/en/sujato#8.3'>MN 15:8.3</a>), and metaphorically in the sense of a psychological defect. It stems from the root √<i lang='pi' translate='no'>añj</i> in the sense of something smeared on the skin such as ointment (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn75/en/sujato#23.13'>MN 75:23.13</a>) or eyeshadow (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn82/en/sujato#25.14'>MN 82:25.14</a>).

## mn5:2.4
- **PLI**: Idha panāvuso, ekacco puggalo sāṅgaṇova samāno ‘atthi me ajjhattaṁ aṅgaṇan’ti yathābhūtaṁ pajānāti.
- **EN**: But another individual with a blemish does truly understand: ‘There is a blemish in me.’

## mn5:2.5
- **PLI**: Idhāvuso, ekacco puggalo anaṅgaṇova samāno ‘natthi me ajjhattaṁ aṅgaṇan’ti yathābhūtaṁ nappajānāti.
- **EN**: One individual without a blemish doesn’t truly understand: ‘There is no blemish in me.’

## mn5:2.6
- **PLI**: Idha panāvuso, ekacco puggalo anaṅgaṇova samāno ‘natthi me ajjhattaṁ aṅgaṇan’ti yathābhūtaṁ pajānāti.
- **EN**: But another individual without a blemish does truly understand: ‘There is no blemish in me.’

## mn5:2.7
- **PLI**: Tatrāvuso, yvāyaṁ puggalo sāṅgaṇova samāno ‘atthi me ajjhattaṁ aṅgaṇan’ti yathābhūtaṁ nappajānāti, ayaṁ imesaṁ dvinnaṁ puggalānaṁ sāṅgaṇānaṁyeva sataṁ hīnapuriso akkhāyati.
- **EN**: In this case, of the two individuals with a blemish, the one who doesn’t understand is said to be worse,

## mn5:2.8
- **PLI**: Tatrāvuso, yvāyaṁ puggalo sāṅgaṇova samāno ‘atthi me ajjhattaṁ aṅgaṇan’ti yathābhūtaṁ pajānāti, ayaṁ imesaṁ dvinnaṁ puggalānaṁ sāṅgaṇānaṁyeva sataṁ seṭṭhapuriso akkhāyati.

## mn5:2.9
- **PLI**: Tatrāvuso, yvāyaṁ puggalo anaṅgaṇova samāno ‘natthi me ajjhattaṁ aṅgaṇan’ti yathābhūtaṁ nappajānāti, ayaṁ imesaṁ dvinnaṁ puggalānaṁ anaṅgaṇānaṁyeva sataṁ hīnapuriso akkhāyati.
- **EN**: And of the two individuals without a blemish, the one who doesn’t understand is said to be worse,

## mn5:2.10
- **PLI**: Tatrāvuso, yvāyaṁ puggalo anaṅgaṇova samāno ‘natthi me ajjhattaṁ aṅgaṇan’ti yathābhūtaṁ pajānāti, ayaṁ imesaṁ dvinnaṁ puggalānaṁ anaṅgaṇānaṁyeva sataṁ seṭṭhapuriso akkhāyatī”ti.

## mn5:4.1  `cck12.41, dr12.39, ms9M_127, msdiv59, nya4, sya12.44`
- **PLI**: “Tatrāvuso, yvāyaṁ puggalo sāṅgaṇova samāno ‘atthi me ajjhattaṁ aṅgaṇan’ti yathābhūtaṁ nappajānāti, tassetaṁ pāṭikaṅkhaṁ—na chandaṁ janessati na vāyamissati na vīriyaṁ ārabhissati tassaṅgaṇassa pahānāya;
- **EN**: “Reverends, take the case of the individual who has a blemish and does not understand it. You can expect that they won’t generate enthusiasm, make an effort, or rouse up energy to give up that blemish.
- **NOTE**: When Sāriputta and Moggallāna address each other, they use their name together with “reverend” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>āvuso</i>). Here we just have <i lang='pi' translate='no'>āvuso</i>. I take it that Moggallāna is asking questions on behalf of the group, and Sāriputta addresses the group.

## mn5:4.2
- **PLI**: so sarāgo sadoso samoho sāṅgaṇo saṅkiliṭṭhacitto kālaṁ karissati.
- **EN**: And they will die with greed, hate, and delusion, blemished, with a corrupted mind.

## mn5:4.3
- **PLI**: Seyyathāpi, āvuso, kaṁsapāti ābhatā āpaṇā vā kammārakulā vā rajena ca malena ca pariyonaddhā.
- **EN**: Suppose a bronze cup was brought from a shop or smithy enveloped with dirt or stains.

## mn5:4.4
- **PLI**: Tamenaṁ sāmikā na ceva paribhuñjeyyuṁ na ca pariyodapeyyuṁ, rajāpathe ca naṁ nikkhipeyyuṁ.

## mn5:4.5

## mn5:4.6

## mn5:6.1  `cck12.42, csp1ed9.31, csp2ed9.31, dr12.40, ms9M_129, ndp9.35, nya6, sya12.45, vri12.33`

- **NOTE**: “Feature of beauty” is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>subhanimitta</i>. In early Pali, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nimitta</i> is used for a feature or quality of the mind that, when focused on, promotes the growth of similar or related qualities. Thus focusing on beauty fosters the desire for that beauty.
