# Phần 1  (50 đoạn)

## mn1:1.1  `bj10.2, cck12.1, csp1ed9.1, csp2ed9.1, dr12.1, ms9M_2, msdiv1, ndp9.3, nya1, pts-vp-pli1.1, sya12.1, vri12.1`
- **PLI**: Evaṁ me sutaṁ—
- **EN**: So I have heard.
- **NOTE**: Just as the Dīghanikāya begins with the complex and demanding Brahmajālasutta, the Majjhima opens with one of the most abstruse discourses in the entire canon. It examines the ways that the process of perception and identification evolves with progress on the path. It was translated, together with its commentary and extensive analysis, by Bhikkhu Bodhi as <em>The Discourse on the Root of Existence</em>. The commentarial background is also found in the Mūlapariyāya Jātaka (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/ja245/en/sujato'>Ja 245</a>). The commentary connects this sutta with the Gotamakacetiyasutta (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/an3.125/en/sujato'>AN 3.125</a>), but there is no internal evidence to support this.

## mn1:1.2
- **PLI**: ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā ukkaṭṭhāyaṁ viharati subhagavane sālarājamūle.
- **EN**: At one time the Buddha was staying near Ukkaṭṭhā in the Subhaga Forest at the root of a magnificent sal tree.
- **NOTE**: Ukkaṭṭhā, near Sāvatthī, is mentioned only rarely, and always in the context of extraordinary teachings and events that emphasize the cosmic grandeur of the Buddha against the brahmins, likely because it was the home of the prominent Kosalan brahmin Pokkharasāti (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn3/en/sujato#1.2.1'>DN 3:1.2.1</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn14/en/sujato#3.29.1'>DN 14:3.29.1</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn49/en/sujato#2.1'>MN 49:2.1</a>).

## mn1:1.3
- **PLI**: Tatra kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi:
- **EN**: There the Buddha addressed the mendicants,

## mn1:1.4
- **PLI**: “bhikkhavo”ti.
- **EN**: “Mendicants!”
- **NOTE**: The pattern of this discourse answers to such passages as Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 3.7, where Yājñavalkya expounds a series of principles in relation to which the “immortal self” is conceived. The commentary says that this discourse was delivered to a group of former brahmins who had become conceited when they learned the Buddha’s teaching. While the text certainly responds to ideas and methods of Brahmanical texts, that interpretation is not supported by the text.

- **EN**: “Venerable sir,” they replied.

## mn1:1.6

- **EN**: The Buddha said this:

## mn1:2.1  `nya2`
- **PLI**: “sabbadhammamūlapariyāyaṁ vo, bhikkhave, desessāmi.
- **EN**: “Mendicants, I will teach you the explanation of the root of all things.
- **NOTE**: In his third discourse, speaking to Brahmanical ascetics, the Buddha reframed the “all” as the experience of the six senses (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn35.28/en/sujato'>SN 35.28</a>). The distinctive “conceiving” pattern of this sutta is therefore also applied to the “all” of the six senses (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn35.30/en/sujato#1.19'>SN 35.30:1.19</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn35.90/en/sujato#3.7'>SN 35.90:3.7</a>). More broadly, the same pattern is also applied to the “aggregates, elements, and sense fields” (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn35.31/en/sujato#1.21'>SN 35.31:1.21</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn35.91/en/sujato#4.1'>SN 35.91:4.1</a>). | The meaning of “root” is clarified later (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn1/en/sujato#171.4'>MN 1:171.4</a>) as “taking pleasure”, i.e. craving, which is the “root” of suffering.

## mn1:3.1  `ms9M_3, msdiv2, nya3, sya12.2, vri12.2`
- **PLI**: “Idha, bhikkhave, assutavā puthujjano ariyānaṁ adassāvī ariyadhammassa akovido ariyadhamme avinīto, sappurisānaṁ adassāvī sappurisadhammassa akovido sappurisadhamme avinīto—
- **EN**: “Take an unlearned ordinary person who has not seen the noble ones, and is neither skilled nor trained in the teaching of the noble ones. They’ve not seen true persons, and are neither skilled nor trained in the teaching of the true persons.
- **NOTE**: An “unlearned ordinary person”, who has not realized any of the stages of the noble path, is contrasted with one who has entered the path. | “Noble one” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>ariya</i>) loosely conveys the sense “cultured” or “civilized”; it is a term for the inheritors of the Aryan culture that originated among the proto-Indo-European peoples of the Pontic–Caspian steppe. | “True person” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>sappurisa</i>) indicates one who is authentic and genuine in their realization of the truth, and hence is virtuous and good. Both “noble one” and “true person” are technical terms referring to any person who has at least entered the path to stream-entry.

## mn1:3.2
- **PLI**: pathaviṁ pathavito sañjānāti;
- **EN**: They perceive earth as earth.
- **NOTE**: Although their perception (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>saññā</i>) is accurate, to perceive something “as” something is to recognize it filtered through memory and concepts learned in the past, a subtle pre-processing that interprets present experience in light of expectations and desires. | The ending <i lang='pi' translate='no'>-to</i> here and throughout is the “ablative of perspective”, which is used with verbs of cognition to express the idea of seeing something in a certain light; for example, one contemplates the body “as impermanent” (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn74/en/sujato#9.1'>MN 74:9.1</a>).

## mn1:3.3
- **PLI**: pathaviṁ pathavito saññatvā pathaviṁ maññati, pathaviyā maññati, pathavito maññati, pathaviṁ meti maññati, pathaviṁ abhinandati.
- **EN**: Having perceived earth as earth, they conceive it to be earth, they conceive it in earth, they conceive it as earth, they conceive that ‘earth is mine’, they approve earth.
- **NOTE**: To “conceive” or “imagine” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>maññati</i>) is, according to the commentary, to think in terms of a “self”, proliferating experience through craving, conceit, or views until it is constructed <em>for me</em>. This usage draws upon such passages as Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 4.3.20, where due to ignorance, a person “imagines” in a dream the fearful things they saw when awake, or at the highest level, “imagines I am this all” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>ahamevedaṁ sarvo’smīti manyate</i>). | Each of the five phrases takes the “perception of earth as earth” and conceives, imagines, or construes that perception in progressively more differentiated and objectified ways, until it becomes something that is owned and enjoyed.

## mn1:3.4

## mn1:3.5

- **EN**: Because they haven’t completely understood it, I say.
- **NOTE**: “Complete understanding” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>pariññā</i>) is the understanding of the arahant that permanently cuts through all delusions and conceits.

## mn1:4.1  `bj10.4, cck12.2, ms9M_4, nya4`
- **PLI**: Āpaṁ āpato sañjānāti;
- **EN**: They perceive water as water.
- **NOTE**: The sutta proceeds through the four main physical elements or properties before proceeding to beings and then various deities. The difference between these things is not as clear-cut as one might think. The elements were worshiped as gods, while the gods were often anthropomorphized natural phenomena such as the sky (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>deva</i>) or the sun (“sublime radiance”). To identify with a material element is to share the essence of a powerful force of nature. | A similar list, but with fewer items, is found starting at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn49/en/sujato#11.1'>MN 49:11.1</a>.
