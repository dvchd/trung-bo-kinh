## mn1:26.1  `ms9M_26, nya26, sya12.6`
- **PLI**: Nibbānaṁ nibbānato sañjānāti;
- **EN**: They perceive extinguishment as extinguishment.
- **NOTE**: It is puzzling to see “extinguishment” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>nibbāna</i>; Sanskrit <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nirvāṇa</i>) here, as it is the end of conceiving. The similar sequence at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn49/en/sujato#23.1'>MN 49:23.1</a> culminates with “all”. Three interpretations: (1) <em>Simple textual error</em>. Of the three Chinese parallels, EA 44.6 mentions <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nibbāna</i> here, while MA 106 and T 56 do not. If two separate texts have the same error, it predates the separation between the schools, or arose later through contamination. (2) <em>The five kinds of “extinguishment in the present life”</em>. These are false liberations believed by sectarians (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn1/en/sujato#3.19.1'>DN 1:3.19.1</a>). The commentary says they are meant here, but it seems unlikely, given that below the stream-enterer is enjoined to not identify with <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nibbāna</i>, whereas they have already dispelled such misconceptions of the path. (3) <em>An unenlightened person’s misconception of the Buddhist goal</em>. At least some ancient Buddhists read it this way, as this passage is quoted in an Abhidhamma discussion as to whether the deathless as an object of thought can be a fetter (Kathāvatthu 9.2). The Theravāda commentary, rejecting this, says it was the view of the Pubbaseliyas, a branch of the Mahāsaṅghikas.

## mn1:27.1  `cck12.6, csp1ed9.5, csp2ed9.5, dr12.6, ms9M_28, msdiv7, ndp9.7, nya27`
- **PLI**: Yopi so, bhikkhave, bhikkhu sekkho appattamānaso anuttaraṁ yogakkhemaṁ patthayamāno viharati, sopi pathaviṁ pathavito abhijānāti;
- **EN**: A mendicant who is a trainee, who hasn’t achieved their heart’s desire, but lives aspiring to the supreme sanctuary from the yoke, directly knows earth as earth.
- **NOTE**: A “trainee” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>sekha</i>), who has realized stream-entry, once-return, or non-return, has had a vision of the path and Nibbāna. Yet since they have not fully relinquished the fetters that bind them to transmigration, they continue to deepen their practice of the noble eightfold path. Their “direct knowing” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>abhiññā</i>) is purified by the twin powers of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>samatha</i> and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vipassanā</i> meditation, rather than “perception” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>saññā</i>), which is filtered through the five hindrances and other cognitive distortions. This distinction between perception and higher awareness (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>vijñāna</i> or <i lang='pi' translate='no'>prajñāna</i>) was first made by Yājñavalkya (Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 2.4.12 and 4.5.13).

## mn1:27.2
- **PLI**: pathaviṁ pathavito abhiññāya pathaviṁ mā maññi, pathaviyā mā maññi, pathavito mā maññi, pathaviṁ meti mā maññi, pathaviṁ mābhinandi.
- **EN**: Having directly known earth as earth, let them not conceive it to be earth, let them not conceive it in earth, let them not conceive it as earth, let them not conceive that ‘earth is mine’, let them not approve earth.
- **NOTE**: The sutta depicts progress through the path in three grammatical modes. The ordinary person conceives; the trainee ought not conceive; the perfected one does not conceive. This echoes the “three rounds” of the first sermon (Dhammacakkappavattanasutta, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn56.11/en/sujato'>SN 56.11</a>): “there is” suffering; suffering “should be understood”; suffering “has been understood”.

## mn1:51.1  `cck12.7, ms9M_31, msdiv8, nya51, sya12.7`
- **PLI**: Yopi so, bhikkhave, bhikkhu arahaṁ khīṇāsavo vusitavā katakaraṇīyo ohitabhāro anuppattasadattho parikkhīṇabhavasaṁyojano sammadaññāvimutto, sopi pathaviṁ pathavito abhijānāti;
- **EN**: A mendicant who is perfected—with defilements ended, who has completed the spiritual journey, done what had to be done, laid down the burden, achieved their heart’s goal, utterly ended the fetter of continued existence, and is rightly freed through enlightenment—directly knows earth as earth.
- **NOTE**: The “perfected one” is the <i lang='pi' translate='no'>arahant</i>, literally “worthy one”, who is the Buddhist spiritual ideal. Their direct knowing is so powerful that it has cut through all fetters binding them to transmigration.
