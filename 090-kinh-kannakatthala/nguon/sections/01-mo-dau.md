## mn90:1.1  `bj11.562, cck13.502, csp1ed10.327, csp2ed10.327, dr13.471, ms10M_1103, msdiv375, ndp10.373, nya1, sya13.517, vri13.334`
- **PLI**: Evaṁ me sutaṁ—
- **EN**: So I have heard.
- **NOTE**: The narrative of this sutta is entertainingly chaotic, with characters appearing and disappearing at random, many of whom are unknown or little-known elsewhere. This is a lifelike impression of how hard it is to maintain a rational dialogue among the comings and goings. Historically it gives a brief but telling character portrait of Viḍūḍabha, the ill-starred prince of Kosala.

- **NOTE**: Ujuññā was a Kosalan town at which the Buddha discussed asceticism with the naked ascetic Kassapa in <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn8/en/sujato'>DN 8</a>. | “Deer parks” were nature reservations where the animals were safe from hunters.

- **EN**: ‘There is no ascetic or brahmin who will ever claim to be all-knowing and all-seeing, to know and see everything without exception: that is not possible.’
- **NOTE**: The Buddha was well aware of the fact that people did, in fact, make such claims, as he discussed them several times (eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn76/en/sujato#21.1'>MN 76:21.1</a>). He just thought the claims were wrong. One unique detail of the phrasing here is that it is in future tense (also used below at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn90/en/sujato#8.5'>MN 90:8.5</a>). The implication seems to be that not only is it the case that no one currently makes such claims, but that no one ever will in the future. | Compare with other statements on omniscience at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn14/en/sujato#17.3'>MN 14:17.3</a> and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn71/en/sujato#5.2'>MN 71:5.2</a>.
