- **NOTE**: Read <i lang='pi' translate='no'>bhuñjati</i> at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn98/en/sujato#10.30'>MN 98:10.30</a> with <i lang='pi' translate='no'>yathābhuttañca bhuñjatha</i> at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn17/en/sujato#1.9.4'>DN 17:1.9.4</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn26/en/sujato#6.7'>DN 26:6.7</a>, and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn129/en/sujato#35.7'>MN 129:35.7</a>. ## mn98:11.1  `csp1ed10.410, csp2ed10.410, dr13.588, ms10M_1429, nya11`
- **PLI**: Na cāhaṁ brāhmaṇaṁ brūmi,
- **EN**: I don’t call someone a brahmin

## mn98:11.2
- **PLI**: yonijaṁ mattisambhavaṁ;
- **EN**: after the mother’s womb they’re born from.

## mn98:11.3
- **PLI**: Bhovādi nāma so hoti,
- **EN**: If they still have attachments,

## mn98:11.4
- **PLI**: sace hoti sakiñcano;
- **EN**: they’re just someone who says ‘worthy’.
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Bho</i> (“worthy”) is a term of address used by brahmins when speaking to one another or to mendicants.

## mn98:11.5
- **PLI**: Akiñcanaṁ anādānaṁ,
- **EN**: Having nothing, taking nothing:

## mn98:11.6
- **PLI**: tamahaṁ brūmi brāhmaṇaṁ.
- **EN**: that’s who I declare a brahmin.
- **NOTE**: The Buddha categorically rejects Bhāradvāja’s genealogical understanding of a brahmin and endorses Vāseṭṭha’s understanding in terms of good conduct. Nonetheless, while a brahmin must have good conduct, the true meaning of the word lies in liberation and transcendence. In this, the Buddha’s position approaches that of Yājñavalkya, who argued that a brahmin, knowing the Self, gives up desire for sons, wealth, and heaven and wanders seeking alms (Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 3.5.1); ultimately, only one who knows the imperishable is a brahmin (3.8.10).
