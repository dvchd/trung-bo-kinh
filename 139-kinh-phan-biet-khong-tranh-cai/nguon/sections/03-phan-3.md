## mn139:12.3
- **PLI**: Kathañca, bhikkhave, janapadaniruttiyā ca abhiniveso hoti samaññāya ca atisāro?
- **EN**: And how do you insist on popular definitions and overstep normal labels?
- **NOTE**: None of the terms below are regional or dialectical, so <i lang='pi' translate='no'>janapadanirutti</i> means “expressions current among the people”, “popular terms”, “common vernacular” rather than “regional dialects”. | A Sanskrit fragment paralleling this passage lists various words for “person”, which likewise are not regional or dialectical terms (SHT ii 163aA3: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>(jīvo) vā jantur-vā (poṣo) vā pudgalo vā manujo</i>).

## mn139:12.4
- **PLI**: Idha, bhikkhave, tadevekaccesu janapadesu ‘pātī’ti sañjānanti, ‘pattan’ti sañjānanti, ‘vittan’ti sañjānanti, ‘sarāvan’ti sañjānanti ‘dhāropan’ti sañjānanti, ‘poṇan’ti sañjānanti, ‘pisīlavan’ti sañjānanti.
- **EN**: It’s when among different populations they label the same thing as a ‘cup’, a ‘bowl’, a ‘jar’, a ‘scoop’, a ‘vessel’, a ‘dish’, or a ‘plate’.
- **NOTE**: All these terms are either attested in Pali and/or Sanskrit or are readily explicable from Pali roots. This is not about “translating” the Dhamma wholesale, but about using different popular terms, as in the U.S. they refer to the same thing as “soda” or “pop”. | <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Pātī</i> (“cup”) is from the root <i lang='pi' translate='no'>√pā</i>, “to drink”, used in Pali for cups of bronze, gold, or silver. | <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Patta</i> (“bowl”), the masculine form of the same root, is the standard word for a mendicant’s alms bowl. | For <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vittha</i> (“jar”; variants <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vitta</i>, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>piṭṭha</i>) see <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vitthaka</i>, a small jar for sewing gear (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd15/en/sujato#11.5.15'>Kd 15:11.5.15</a>). | <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Sarāva</i> (“scoop”) is a common word for a scoop for water (eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an3.57/en/sujato#3.2'>AN 3.57:3.2</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd15/en/sujato#14.3.30'>Kd 15:14.3.30</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/kv1.1/en/sujato#356.6'>Kv 1.1:356.6</a>; Sanskrit <i lang='pi' translate='no'>śarāva</i>, eg. Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 5.1.4.12; Chāndogya Upaniṣad 8.8.1; Manu 6.56; also in Jain texts). | For <i lang='pi' translate='no'>dhāropa</i> (“vessel”) accept variant <i lang='pi' translate='no'>harosa</i> by analogy with <i lang='pi' translate='no'>puṭosa</i> (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn118/en/sujato#8.9'>MN 118:8.9</a>), yielding the sense “carrier for food”, “vessel”. | <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Poṇa</i> (“dish”) is probably a concave dish with “sloped” sides (cp. English “hollowware”). | <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Pisīlava</i> (“plate”) was used for the butter placed on the altar (Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 2.5.3.6: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>piśīle vā pātryau vā</i>).
