## mn9:62.3
- **PLI**: kāyasaṅkhāro, vacīsaṅkhāro, cittasaṅkhāro.
- **EN**: Choices by way of body, speech, and mind.
- **NOTE**: This set of three refers to deeds carried out through the body, speech, and mind (eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn57/en/sujato#8.2'>MN 57:8.2</a>). “Mind” is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>citta</i>, while the Sanskrit Dṛṣṭisampannasūtra has <i lang='pi' translate='no'>mano</i> in the parallel passage (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sf172/san/tripathi#tri23.17b'>SF 172</a>). In dependent origination these are synonyms (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn12.61/en/sujato#2.1'>SN 12.61:2.1</a>). Not to be confused with the same terms when used in the context of mindfulness of breathing (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn41.6/en/sujato#1.5'>SN 41.6:1.5</a>).

## mn9:65.1
- **PLI**: Yato kho, āvuso, ariyasāvako avijjañca pajānāti, avijjāsamudayañca pajānāti, avijjānirodhañca pajānāti, avijjānirodhagāminiṁ paṭipadañca pajānāti—
- **EN**: A noble disciple understands ignorance, its origin, its cessation, and the practice that leads to its cessation …
- **NOTE**: “Ignorance” is the only item not found in the Chinese and Sanskrit parallels. Perhaps the original form of the sutta treated ignorance solely under its inverse, which is right view itself. Thus the lack of ignorance as a separate item could have been deliberate, emphasizing how the entirety of right view, encompassing all items in the discourse, implies the opposite of ignorance.

## mn9:66.1
- **PLI**: Āsavasamudayā avijjāsamudayo, āsavanirodhā avijjānirodho, ayameva ariyo aṭṭhaṅgiko maggo avijjānirodhagāminī paṭipadā, seyyathidaṁ—
- **EN**: Ignorance originates from defilement. Ignorance ceases when defilement ceases. The practice that leads to the cessation of ignorance is simply this noble eightfold path …”
- **NOTE**: The current presentation is unique, as ignorance is itself one of the defilements (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>āsava</i>). Ignorance is normally the final item in dependent origination, the “head” from which all follows (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/snp5.1/en/sujato#51.1'>Snp 5.1:51.1</a>). Nonetheless, causality is not a linear process, so a starting point is identified only for practical reasons. At <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an10.62/en/sujato#2.4'>AN 10.62:2.4</a> the five hindrances are said to be the “fuel” for ignorance.

## mn9:70.4
- **PLI**: Avijjāsamudayā āsavasamudayo, avijjānirodhā āsavanirodho, ayameva ariyo aṭṭhaṅgiko maggo āsavanirodhagāminī paṭipadā, seyyathidaṁ—
- **EN**: Defilement originates from ignorance. Defilement ceases when ignorance ceases. The practice that leads to the cessation of defilement is simply this noble eightfold path, that is:
- **NOTE**: Ignorance and defilements are locked in a cycle of mutual dependent conditioning: ignorance begets more ignorance. Compare the mutual conditioning of consciousness with name and form at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn15/en/sujato#20.1'>DN 15:20.1</a>. One implication of this is that no first point of ignorance and hence no first point of transmigration can be known.
