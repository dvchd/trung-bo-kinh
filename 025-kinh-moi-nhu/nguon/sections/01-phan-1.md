## mn25:2.1  `cck12.283, csp1ed9.206, csp2ed9.206, dr12.266, ms9M_906, nya2`
- **PLI**: “Na, bhikkhave, nevāpiko nivāpaṁ nivapati migajātānaṁ:
- **EN**: “Mendicants, a sower does not sow seed for deer thinking,
- **NOTE**: “Sower sows seed” renders the triplicated expression <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nevāpiko nivāpaṁ nivapati</i>. The root sense of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vap</i> is to “cast down” or “sow” seed; it is also used of offerings for the ancestors in the <i lang='pi' translate='no'>śrāddha</i> ceremony. It is tempting to translate this phrase as a “trapper” who lays “bait”. But the aim of the “sower” is simply to grow a crop, and to do this they need to keep the deer out. That is the guile of the story: they appear harmless. | For an amicable solution to this problem, see the conclusion to the Rurumigarāja Jātaka (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/ja482/en/sujato'>Ja 482</a>) | <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Migajāta</i> (“deer”) might be better translated as “wild animals” here.

- **EN**: ‘Wow, this third herd of deer is so sneaky and devious, they must be some kind of strange spirits with magical abilities!
- **NOTE**: The repeated <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nāma</i> is strongly emphatic. | <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Parajana</i> sometimes means “stranger” (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mil6.3.1/en/sujato#13.2'>Mil 6.3.1:13.2</a>), but the commentary here glosses it as <i lang='pi' translate='no'>yakkha</i>, and a <i lang='pi' translate='no'>yakkha</i> named Parajana makes an appearance in (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn31/en/sujato#21.1'>MN 31:21.1</a>).
