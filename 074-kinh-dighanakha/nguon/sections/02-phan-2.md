- **EN**: “It seems the Buddha speaks of giving up and letting go of all these things through direct knowledge.”

## mn74:14.4
- **PLI**: Iti hidaṁ āyasmato sāriputtassa paṭisañcikkhato anupādāya āsavehi cittaṁ vimucci.
- **EN**: Reflecting like this, Venerable Sāriputta’s mind was freed from the defilements by not grasping.
- **NOTE**: Sāriputta became a stream-enterer on hearing a short teaching from the monk Assaji (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd1/en/sujato#23.5.6'>Kd 1:23.5.6</a>), whereupon he told his friend Moggallāna. They both promptly left their former teacher Sañjaya, a traumatic loss to Sañjaya and his community. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an4.172/en/sujato#2.1'>AN 4.172:2.1</a> says that Sāriputta became enlightened a fortnight after he ordained, a detail confirmed in multiple parallels to this passage; <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn111/en/sujato#2.8'>MN 111:2.8</a> details the practice he undertook in that fortnight. This means that the current discussion must have taken place only a fortnight after leaving Sañjaya. Now, Sañjaya’s philosophy was to systematically avoid taking any position (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn2/en/sujato#32.2'>DN 2:32.2</a>), which is similar to Dīghanakha’s doctrine. It seems likely, then, that Dīghanakha was a disciple of Sañjaya. This supposition finds support in several parallels, which say Dīghanakha was annoyed to learn that Sāriputta had rejected other teachers and gone over to the Buddha (eg. Avadānaśataka, Vaidya 1958a 256: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sarve tīrthakarā nigṛhītāḥ</i>; also in Mahāprajñāpāramitā-upadeśa-śāstra and Mahāvibhāṣā). These sources do not specify Sañjaya, but who else could it be?
