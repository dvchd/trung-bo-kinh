## mn26:21.7
- **PLI**: Ye sotavanto pamuñcantu saddhaṁ;
- **EN**: Let those with ears to hear commit to faith.
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Pamuñcantu saddhaṁ</i> has long troubled translators, as the basic sense of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>pamuñcantu</i> is “release”. Sanskrit variants include <i lang='pi' translate='no'>pramodantu</i> (“celebrate”) or <i lang='pi' translate='no'>praṇudantu kāṅkṣāḥ</i> (“dispel doubts”). I think it is a poetic variant of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>adhimuñcantu</i>, to “decide” or “commit” to faith. Pali commonly uses synonymous verbs to reinforce the sense of the noun. In <a href='https://suttacentral.net/snp5.19/en/sujato'>Snp 5.19</a>, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>muttasaddho</i>, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>pamuñcassu saddhaṁ</i>, and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>adhimuttacittaṁ</i> are all used in this sense.

- **NOTE**: The five mendicants appear abruptly here and the reason for their behavior is only clear in light of the fuller accounts (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn36/en/sujato#33.4'>MN 36:33.4</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn85/en/sujato#33.4'>MN 85:33.4</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn100/en/sujato#30.5'>MN 100:30.5</a>). Their names are listed in the Vinaya account: Koṇḍañña, Vappa, Bhaddiya, Mahānāma, and Assaji (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd1/en/sujato#6.31.6'>Kd 1:6.31.6</a>). | Note that he calls them “mendicants”, a term typically reserved for Buddhist renunciates.
