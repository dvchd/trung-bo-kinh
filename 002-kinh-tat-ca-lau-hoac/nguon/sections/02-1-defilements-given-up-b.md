# 1. Defilements Given Up by Seeing  (51 đoạn)

## mn2:5.0
- **PLI**: 1. Dassanāpahātabbaāsava
- **EN**: 1. Defilements Given Up by Seeing

## mn2:5.1  `ms9M_54, msdiv17, nya5, sya12.13`
- **PLI**: Katame ca, bhikkhave, āsavā dassanā pahātabbā?
- **EN**: And what are the defilements that should be given up by seeing?
- **NOTE**: “Seeing” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>dassana</i>) the four noble truths with the wisdom of insight.

## mn2:5.2
- **PLI**: Idha, bhikkhave, assutavā puthujjano ariyānaṁ adassāvī ariyadhammassa akovido ariyadhamme avinīto, sappurisānaṁ adassāvī sappurisadhammassa akovido sappurisadhamme avinīto—
- **EN**: Take an unlearned ordinary person who has not seen the noble ones, and is neither skilled nor trained in the teaching of the noble ones. They’ve not seen true persons, and are neither skilled nor trained in the teaching of the true persons.
- **NOTE**: “Noble Ones” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>ariyā</i>) and “true persons” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>sappurisā</i>) both refer to the eight kinds of individual who have entered the eightfold path to awakening.

## mn2:5.3
- **PLI**: manasikaraṇīye dhamme nappajānāti, amanasikaraṇīye dhamme nappajānāti.
- **EN**: They don’t understand to which things they should apply the mind and to which things they should not apply the mind.

## mn2:5.4
- **PLI**: So manasikaraṇīye dhamme appajānanto amanasikaraṇīye dhamme appajānanto, ye dhammā na manasikaraṇīyā, te dhamme manasi karoti, ye dhammā manasikaraṇīyā te dhamme na manasi karoti.
- **EN**: So they apply the mind to things they shouldn’t and don’t apply the mind to things they should.

## mn2:7.1  `ms9M_57, nya7, pts-vp-pli1.8, sya12.14`
- **PLI**: Tassa amanasikaraṇīyānaṁ dhammānaṁ manasikārā manasikaraṇīyānaṁ dhammānaṁ amanasikārā anuppannā ceva āsavā uppajjanti uppannā ca āsavā pavaḍḍhanti.
- **EN**: Because of applying the mind to what they should not and not applying the mind to what they should, unarisen defilements arise and arisen defilements grow.

## mn2:7.2  `bj10.22, ms9M_58, msdiv18`
- **PLI**: So evaṁ ayoniso manasi karoti:
- **EN**: This is how they apply the mind irrationally:

## mn2:7.3
- **PLI**: ‘ahosiṁ nu kho ahaṁ atītamaddhānaṁ? Na nu kho ahosiṁ atītamaddhānaṁ? Kiṁ nu kho ahosiṁ atītamaddhānaṁ? Kathaṁ nu kho ahosiṁ atītamaddhānaṁ? Kiṁ hutvā kiṁ ahosiṁ nu kho ahaṁ atītamaddhānaṁ?
- **EN**: ‘Did I exist in the past? Did I not exist in the past? What was I in the past? How was I in the past? After being what, what did I become in the past?
- **NOTE**: These are existential questions framed from a metaphysical perspective, i.e. they are based on the underlying assumption of a self. They are “irrational” because they avoid the question of cause: they only ask <em>what</em> happens, not <em>why</em> it happens.

## mn2:7.4
- **PLI**: Bhavissāmi nu kho ahaṁ anāgatamaddhānaṁ? Na nu kho bhavissāmi anāgatamaddhānaṁ? Kiṁ nu kho bhavissāmi anāgatamaddhānaṁ? Kathaṁ nu kho bhavissāmi anāgatamaddhānaṁ? Kiṁ hutvā kiṁ bhavissāmi nu kho ahaṁ anāgatamaddhānan’ti?
- **EN**: Will I exist in the future? Will I not exist in the future? What will I be in the future? How will I be in the future? After being what, what will I become in the future?’

## mn2:7.5
- **PLI**: Etarahi vā paccuppannamaddhānaṁ ajjhattaṁ kathaṅkathī hoti:
- **EN**: Or they are undecided about the present thus:

## mn2:7.6
- **PLI**: ‘ahaṁ nu khosmi? No nu khosmi? Kiṁ nu khosmi? Kathaṁ nu khosmi? Ayaṁ nu kho satto kuto āgato? So kuhiṁ gāmī bhavissatī’ti?
- **EN**: ‘Am I? Am I not? What am I? How am I? This sentient being—where did it come from? And where will it go?’
- **NOTE**: Compare Rig Veda 10.129.6: “From where was it born, from where this creation”; and the Jain Ācāraṅgasūtra 1.1.1.2, “What was I, what shall I become after departing from here”.

## mn2:8.1  `cck12.14, csp1ed9.11, csp2ed9.11, dr12.13, ms9M_59, msdiv19, ndp9.13, nya8, sya12.15, vri12.12`
- **PLI**: Tassa evaṁ ayoniso manasikaroto channaṁ diṭṭhīnaṁ aññatarā diṭṭhi uppajjati.
- **EN**: When they apply the mind irrationally in this way, one of the following six views arises in them and is taken as a genuine fact.
- **NOTE**: To wonder is natural, but when we take speculations seriously they become dogmas, insisted on as the truth, though in reality we have no way of knowing.

## mn2:8.2
- **PLI**: ‘Atthi me attā’ti vā assa saccato thetato diṭṭhi uppajjati;
- **EN**: The view: ‘My self survives.’
- **NOTE**: See also the discussions on the “self” at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn44.10/en/sujato'>SN 44.10</a> and “gods” at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn100/en/sujato#42.4'>MN 100:42.4</a>. All three passages are phrased similarly. In each case the question, drawing on the doubts about the past and future as depicted above, is whether the self continues to exist (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>atthi</i>) or not (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>natthi</i>) after death.

## mn2:8.3
- **PLI**: ‘natthi me attā’ti vā assa saccato thetato diṭṭhi uppajjati;
- **EN**: The view: ‘My self does not survive.’

## mn2:8.4
- **PLI**: ‘attanāva attānaṁ sañjānāmī’ti vā assa saccato thetato diṭṭhi uppajjati;
- **EN**: The view: ‘I perceive the self with the self.’
- **NOTE**: Compare Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 4.4.23, which, in a discussion of the hereafter, says that a sage who is tamed and stilled (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>samāhito</i>) “sees the self in the self” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>ātmanyevātmānaṁ paśyati</i>). Note the different verb here; unlike the Upaniṣad, the sutta is not speaking of a sage who “sees” but of a theorist who “perceives”.

## mn2:8.5
- **PLI**: ‘attanāva anattānaṁ sañjānāmī’ti vā assa saccato thetato diṭṭhi uppajjati;
- **EN**: The view: ‘I perceive what is not-self with the self.’
- **NOTE**: The method of negation was employed by Yājñavalkya to discard false, shallow views of the Self: “This Self is that which is not that, not that” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>sa eṣa neti netyātmā</i>, Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 4.5.15, etc.).

## mn2:8.6
- **PLI**: ‘anattanāva attānaṁ sañjānāmī’ti vā assa saccato thetato diṭṭhi uppajjati;
- **EN**: The view: ‘I perceive the self with what is not-self.’
- **NOTE**: The theorist still relies on perception (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>saññā</i>) and hence does not see the Self, which is pure <i lang='pi' translate='no'>viññāṇa</i> cognized by <i lang='pi' translate='no'>viññāṇa</i>: “Through what should one know the Knower?” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>vijñātāramare kena vijānīyāt</i>, Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 2.4.14).

## mn2:8.7
- **PLI**: atha vā panassa evaṁ diṭṭhi hoti:
- **EN**: Or they have such a view:

## mn2:8.8
- **PLI**: ‘yo me ayaṁ attā vado vedeyyo tatra tatra kalyāṇapāpakānaṁ kammānaṁ vipākaṁ paṭisaṁvedeti so kho pana me ayaṁ attā nicco dhuvo sassato avipariṇāmadhammo sassatisamaṁ tatheva ṭhassatī’ti.
- **EN**: ‘This self of mine is he, the one who speaks, the one who knows, who experiences the results of good and bad deeds in all the different realms. This self is permanent, everlasting, eternal, and imperishable, and will last forever and ever.’
- **NOTE**: This view, attributed to the mendicant Sāti at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn36/en/sujato#5.11'>MN 36:5.11</a>, is reminiscent of Yājñavalkya’s discussion of the Self as a person’s “light” in the Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad. He illustrates the departure of the Self from the body at death by analogy with dreams, where a person sets aside the physical body and takes up a body of light (4.3.9). There (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>tatra</i>) he is the “agent” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>kartā</i>, 4.3.10) who “moves between the worlds contemplating and playing, as it were” (4.3.7). He creates his own experiences, seeing good and bad (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>dṛṣṭvaiva puṇyaṁ ca pāpaṁ ca</i>, 4.3.15). Being “immortal” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>amṛto</i>, 4.3.12), he wanders where he likes and returns unaffected (4.3.16). In 4.4.5, he additionally says that “as one acts, so one becomes”, doing good becoming good, and doing bad becoming bad. See too Aitareya Upaniṣad 3.1, which says the Self is that by which one sees, hears, smells, tastes, speaks and knows. | For the phrase <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sassatisamaṁ</i> (“lasting forever and ever”), compare Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 5.10.1: “He reaches that world free of sorrow and snow, where he lives forever and ever” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>sa lokam āgacchaty aśokam ahimam. tasmin vasati śāśvatīḥ samāḥ</i>).

## mn2:8.9
- **PLI**: Idaṁ vuccati, bhikkhave, diṭṭhigataṁ diṭṭhigahanaṁ diṭṭhikantāraṁ diṭṭhivisūkaṁ diṭṭhivipphanditaṁ diṭṭhisaṁyojanaṁ.
- **EN**: This is called a misconception, the thicket of views, the desert of views, the twist of views, the dodge of views, the fetter of views.
- **NOTE**: “Twist” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>visūka</i>) is used with “dodge” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>vipphandita</i>) and sometimes “duck” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>visevita</i>) for a horse fighting the bit (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn65/en/sujato#33.2'>MN 65:33.2</a>) or a crab escaping the torment of children (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn35/en/sujato#23.9'>MN 35:23.9</a>). <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Visūka</i> is also used for a “show” of dance, etc., where the commentarial gloss <i lang='pi' translate='no'>paṭāṇi</i> (“screw”) reinforces the sense “twist, gyrate”. As descriptions of views, they suggest the active process of denial and distortion through which views shape how we see the world.

## mn2:8.10
- **PLI**: Diṭṭhisaṁyojanasaṁyutto, bhikkhave, assutavā puthujjano na parimuccati jātiyā jarāya maraṇena sokehi paridevehi dukkhehi domanassehi upāyāsehi;
- **EN**: An unlearned ordinary person who is fettered by views is not freed from rebirth, old age, and death, from sorrow, lamentation, pain, sadness, and distress.

## mn2:8.11

- **EN**: They’re not freed from suffering, I say.

- **PLI**: Tassa amanasikaraṇīyānaṁ dhammānaṁ amanasikārā manasikaraṇīyānaṁ dhammānaṁ manasikārā anuppannā ceva āsavā na uppajjanti, uppannā ca āsavā pahīyanti.
- **EN**: Because of not applying the mind to what they should not and applying the mind to what they should, unarisen defilements don’t arise and arisen defilements are given up.

## mn2:11.1  `csp1ed9.12, csp2ed9.12, ms9M_64, msdiv21, nya11, vri12.13`
- **PLI**: So ‘idaṁ dukkhan’ti yoniso manasi karoti, ‘ayaṁ dukkhasamudayo’ti yoniso manasi karoti, ‘ayaṁ dukkhanirodho’ti yoniso manasi karoti, ‘ayaṁ dukkhanirodhagāminī paṭipadā’ti yoniso manasi karoti.
- **EN**: They rationally apply the mind: ‘This is suffering’ … ‘This is the origin of suffering’ … ‘This is the cessation of suffering’ … ‘This is the practice that leads to the cessation of suffering’.
- **NOTE**: This is the realization of the four noble truths. The suttas distinguish between someone who accepts the truth of the Dhamma either by faith or by logic, and someone who truly sees with direct experience (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn25.1/en/sujato'>SN 25.1</a>). This direct vision, here called “rational application of mind”, may be expressed any number of different ways.

## mn2:11.2
- **PLI**: Tassa evaṁ yoniso manasikaroto tīṇi saṁyojanāni pahīyanti—
- **EN**: And as they do so, they give up three fetters:

## mn2:11.3
- **PLI**: sakkāyadiṭṭhi, vicikicchā, sīlabbataparāmāso.
- **EN**: substantialist view, doubt, and misapprehension of precepts and observances.
- **NOTE**: They are a stream-enterer who has entered the first of the four stages of awakening. “Seeing” the four noble truths permanently severs these three fetters. This is the decisive difference between “seeing” and knowing by faith or logic (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn25.1/en/sujato'>SN 25.1</a>).

## mn2:11.4
- **PLI**: Ime vuccanti, bhikkhave, āsavā dassanā pahātabbā.
- **EN**: These are called the defilements that should be given up by seeing.
