## mn2:1.1  `csp1ed9.9, csp2ed9.9, ms9M_51, msdiv14, ndp9.11, nya1, pts-vp-pli1.7, sya12.12, vri12.10`

## mn2:2.1  `nya2`
- **PLI**: “sabbāsavasaṁvarapariyāyaṁ vo, bhikkhave, desessāmi.
- **EN**: “Mendicants, I will teach you the explanation of the restraint of all defilements.
- **NOTE**: “Defilements” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>āsava</i>) are the fundamental “pollutions” or “corruptions” that darken awareness, trapping people in transmigration. In practical application, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>āsava</i> can mean the “discharge” from a sore (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/an3.27/en/sujato#2.4'>AN 3.27:2.4</a>). The Jain Tattvārthasūtra 6.1–2 defines <i lang='pi' translate='no'>āsrava</i> as the influx of deeds; these pollute the pure soul. | Note that the Pali <i lang='pi' translate='no'>āsava</i> represents two distinct words, which are homonyms in Pali but differentiated in Sanskrit. The word here is Sanskrit <i lang='pi' translate='no'>āsrava</i> (or <i lang='pi' translate='no'>āśrava</i>), whereas Sanskrit <i lang='pi' translate='no'>āsava</i> means “intoxicant”, a sense found elsewhere in Pali (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-bu-vb-pc51/en/sujato#2.1.4'>Bu Pc 51:2.1.4</a>).

## mn2:2.2

- **EN**: Listen and apply your mind well, I will speak.”
- **NOTE**: This opening follows the pattern of <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn1/en/sujato'>MN 1</a>.

## mn2:3.1  `dr12.11, ms9M_52, msdiv15, nya3`
- **PLI**: “Jānato ahaṁ, bhikkhave, passato āsavānaṁ khayaṁ vadāmi, no ajānato no apassato.
- **EN**: “Mendicants, I say that the ending of defilements is for one who knows and sees, not for one who does not know or see.
- **NOTE**: The “ending of defilements” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>āsavānaṁ khayaṁ</i>) is a common sutta term for arahantship. | “Knowing and seeing” refers to the penetrating insight that arises from meditative immersion (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>samādhi</i>, eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an5.168/en/sujato#2.2'>AN 5.168:2.2</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an10.2/en/sujato#2.3'>AN 10.2:2.3</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn12.23/en/sujato#6.18'>SN 12.23:6.18</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn2/en/sujato#83.1'>DN 2:83.1</a>).

## mn2:3.2
- **PLI**: Kiñca, bhikkhave, jānato kiñca passato āsavānaṁ khayaṁ vadāmi?
- **EN**: For one who knows and sees what?

## mn2:3.3
- **PLI**: Yoniso ca manasikāraṁ ayoniso ca manasikāraṁ.
- **EN**: Rational application of mind and irrational application of mind.
- **NOTE**: “Rational application of mind” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>yoniso manasikāra</i>) is a distinctively Buddhist term. It draws on the frequent Vedic image of the “womb of truth” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>yonāv ṛtasya</i>, Rig Veda 9.13.9), the source of the laws and patterns that govern the natural order. The idea is that by applying the mind by way of cause or reason the hidden truth can be uncovered. Here the meditator’s insight is framed not as uncovering the objective truth about the world, but as reflexively understanding the means of insight itself.

## mn2:3.4
- **PLI**: Ayoniso, bhikkhave, manasikaroto anuppannā ceva āsavā uppajjanti, uppannā ca āsavā pavaḍḍhanti;
- **EN**: When you apply the mind irrationally, defilements arise, and once arisen they grow.
- **NOTE**: This passage and the next are comparable to the contemplation of the principles of the five hindrances and the seven awakening factors respectively in the fourth section of mindfulness meditation (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>dhammānupassanā</i>; see <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn10/en/sujato#36.4'>MN 10:36.4</a>).

## mn2:3.5
- **PLI**: yoniso ca kho, bhikkhave, manasikaroto anuppannā ceva āsavā na uppajjanti, uppannā ca āsavā pahīyanti.
- **EN**: When you apply the mind rationally, defilements don’t arise, and those that have already arisen are given up.

## mn2:4.1  `bj10.20, cck12.12, ms9M_53, msdiv16, nya4`
- **PLI**: Atthi, bhikkhave, āsavā dassanā pahātabbā, atthi āsavā saṁvarā pahātabbā, atthi āsavā paṭisevanā pahātabbā, atthi āsavā adhivāsanā pahātabbā, atthi āsavā parivajjanā pahātabbā, atthi āsavā vinodanā pahātabbā, atthi āsavā bhāvanā pahātabbā.
- **EN**: Some defilements should be given up by seeing, some by restraint, some by using, some by enduring, some by avoiding, some by dispelling, and some by developing.
- **NOTE**: Here the Buddha gives the scheme of the sutta: seven methods for getting rid of defilements. Omitting the defilements given up by seeing, the remainder are found at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an6.58/en/sujato'>AN 6.58</a>.
