## mn82:1.1  `bj11.420, cck13.384, csp1ed10.245, csp2ed10.245, ms10M_837, msdiv293, ndp10.281, nya1, pts-vp-pli2.55, sya13.388, vri13.255`
- **PLI**: Evaṁ me sutaṁ—
- **EN**: So I have heard.
- **NOTE**: The story of Raṭṭhapāla, the “guardian of the nation”, has become one of the most famous and beloved parables from early Buddhism. It is a testament to a young man’s resolution to go forth out of faith, a quality for which he was later honored (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/an1.210/en/sujato#1.1'>AN 1.210:1.1</a>). But the power of Raṭṭhapāla’s determination should not overshadow the moving conversation he has with King Koravya at the end.

## mn82:1.2
- **PLI**: ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā kurūsu cārikaṁ caramāno mahatā bhikkhusaṅghena saddhiṁ yena thullakoṭṭhikaṁ nāma kurūnaṁ nigamo tadavasari.
- **EN**: At one time the Buddha was wandering in the land of the Kurus together with a large Saṅgha of mendicants when he arrived at a town of the Kurus named Thullakoṭṭhika.
- **NOTE**: Thullakoṭṭhika (“place of fat granaries”; Sanskrit <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sthūlakoṣṭhaka</i>) only appears in the story of Raṭṭhapāla.

## mn82:6.6
- **PLI**: “Anuññātosi pana tvaṁ, raṭṭhapāla, mātāpitūhi agārasmā anagāriyaṁ pabbajjāyā”ti?
- **EN**: “But, Raṭṭhapāla, do you have your parents’ permission?”
- **NOTE**: This Vinaya rule is not mentioned elsewhere in the suttas, although it was required also of Sudinna (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-bu-vb-pj1/en/sujato#5.1.14'>Bu Pj 1:5.1.14</a>). It was instituted on the emotional request of the Buddha’s own father, who was distraught when his grandson Rāhula went forth while still a boy (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd1/en/sujato#54.4.2'>Kd 1:54.4.2</a>). It originally applied to novices, who went forth as young as fifteen or “when old enough to scare crows” (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd1/en/sujato#51.1.14'>Kd 1:51.1.14</a>). Over time the requirements for ordination tend to become stricter as things required in a particular context are applied generally. If this is the case here, the process must have happened quickly, so that even in the canonical full ordination procedure a candidate, who must be twenty years at least, must have permission of their parents (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd1/en/sujato#76.1.12'>Kd 1:76.1.12</a>). However, there are many people who cannot get such permission, such as orphans. Thus if one does give the going-forth to someone without permission of the parents, it is a minor offense of wrong-doing for the mendicant performing the ordination, but it does not invalidate it (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd1/en/sujato#54.6.5'>Kd 1:54.6.5</a>).

- **EN**: “I’ll make sure, sir, to get my parents’ permission.”
