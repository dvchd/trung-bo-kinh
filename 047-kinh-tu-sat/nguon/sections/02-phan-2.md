## mn47:10.8
- **PLI**: abhayūparatohamasmi, nāhamasmi bhayūparato, vītarāgattā kāme na sevāmi khayā rāgassā’ti.
- **EN**: “I am securely stilled, not insecurely stilled. The reason I don’t indulge in sensual pleasures is that I’m free of greed because greed has ended.”’
- **NOTE**: These exact words are not spoken by the Buddha in suttas. However the early Abhidhamma text Puggalapaññatti explains that “insecurely stilled” includes the seven trainees and ethical ordinary people, while the arahant is “securely stilled” (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pp2.1/en/sujato#11.1'>Pp 2.1:11.1</a>). Since the Puggalapaññatti consists mainly of lightly adapted quotes from the suttas, it is possible that this passage has been lost from the suttas as they stand. This would explain why these terms are invoked here as if they were known.

## mn47:13.4
- **PLI**: etaṁ pathohamasmi, etaṁ gocaro, no ca tena tammayo’ti.
- **EN**: I am the scope and the range of that, but I am not determined by that.’
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Etaṁ</i> is the accusative of relation, familiar from such phrases as <i lang='pi' translate='no'>taṁ gotamaṁ evaṁ kalyāṇo kittisaddo abbhuggato</i> (“a good word <em>of that</em> Gotama has spread”). | <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Ahamasmi</i> (“I am”) asserts identity (not possession per the commentary). It is an Upaniṣadic turn of phrase. | <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Tammaya</i> (“determined by that”, literally “made with that”) is found occasionally in the suttas (also <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn137/en/sujato#20.3'>MN 137:20.3</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn113/en/sujato#21.7'>MN 113:21.7</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an3.40/en/sujato#7.4'>AN 3.40:7.4</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an6.104/en/sujato'>AN 6.104</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/snp4.9/en/sujato#12.2'>Snp 4.9:12.2</a>). The Buddha’s usage echoes Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 4.4.5, which begins, “This self is indeed divinity, made with consciousness …” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>sa vā ayamātmā brahma vijñānamayo</i>). It goes on to list many other items of which the self is comprised (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>idaṁmayo</i>), the point being that the self is ultimately created by its actions (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>yathākārī yathācārī tathā bhavati</i>), a detail that emphasizes the causal sense of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>-maya</i>. Thus the term does not simply point to that which is the self, but rather that by which the self is shaped or determined. Yajñavalkya also uses <i lang='pi' translate='no'>etanmaya</i> in the same sense in such passages as Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 10.4.2.30: “that (self) is made with hymns, sacrifices, breaths, and divinities” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>candomaya stomamayaḥ prāṇamayo devatāmayaḥ sa etanmaya</i>) and Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 1.5.3: “that self is made with this: speech, mind, and breath” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>etanmayo vā ayam ātmā: vāṅmayo manomayaḥ prāṇamayaḥ</i>). Thus the term emphasizes the Buddha’s freedom from conditions. He exemplifies good qualities but is not “determined” by their kammic force.

## mn47:16.1  `cck12.553, dr12.511, ms9M_1689, msdiv490, ndp9.392, nya16, vri12.402`
- **PLI**: Yassa kassaci, bhikkhave, imehi ākārehi imehi padehi imehi byañjanehi tathāgate saddhā niviṭṭhā hoti mūlajātā patiṭṭhitā, ayaṁ vuccati, bhikkhave, ākāravatī saddhā dassanamūlikā;
- **EN**: When someone’s faith is settled, rooted, and planted in the Realized One in this manner, with these words and phrases, it’s said to be grounded faith that’s based on evidence.
- **NOTE**: In this sentence the word <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ākāra</i> is used in two distinct senses. In the stock phrase “in this manner, with these words and phrases” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>imehi ākārehi imehi padehi imehi byañjanehi</i>) it means “manner” of exposition (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn18/en/sujato#20.17'>MN 18:20.17</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn35.116/en/sujato#10.14'>SN 35.116:10.14</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an10.115/en/sujato#21.2'>AN 10.115:21.2</a>). In the phrase “grounded faith” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>ākāravatī saddhā</i>) it means a “ground” or “reason” on which faith is based.
