## mn105:9.12
- **PLI**: ‘āneñjasaṁyojanena hi kho visaṁyutto lokāmisādhimutto purisapuggalo’ti.
- **EN**: ‘That individual is intent on pleasures of the flesh, for they’re detached from things connected with the imperturbable.’
- **NOTE**: The reading <i lang='pi' translate='no'>āneñjasaṁyojanena hi kho visaṁyutto</i> is problematic, as it suggests they are free of the fetter of the imperturbable, which would mean they are an arahant. Several manuscripts omit this phrase, but it is glossed in the commentary. I think it harks back to the immediately prior <i lang='pi' translate='no'>āneñjapaṭisaṁyuttāya</i>; possibly the <i lang='pi' translate='no'>paṭi</i> dropped out in an old misreading. They’re not detached from the “fetter” of the imperturbable, but from “what is connected with” the imperturbable, i.e. they are disinterested in the talk about it.

## mn105:19.5
- **PLI**: Esaniyā sallaṁ esitvā sallaṁ abbuheyya, apaneyya visadosaṁ saupādisesaṁ.
- **EN**: extract it, and expel the poison, leaving some residue behind.
- **NOTE**: The term <i lang='pi' translate='no'>saupādisesa</i> is better known as a description of those who have attained Nibbāna “with residue”, namely the five aggregates that persist so long as life lasts. The prefix <i lang='pi' translate='no'>upādi</i> is from the same root as <i lang='pi' translate='no'>upādāna</i>, normally “grasping”, but here in its secondary sense of “material cause, fuel”. The “residue” of poison is the material cause of potential harm, just as the five aggregates still entail a residue of physical suffering for a perfected one.

## mn105:19.6
- **PLI**: Saupādisesoti jānamāno so evaṁ vadeyya:
- **EN**: Imagining that no residue remained, the surgeon would say:
- **NOTE**: For “imagining that no residue remained”, I follow PTS and Siamese editions, which have <i lang='pi' translate='no'>anupādiseso ti maññamāno</i>. Bodhi’s note 1003 to his <em>Middle Length Discourses</em> points out that earlier manuscripts support this reading, which seems to have been “corrected” to <i lang='pi' translate='no'>saupādisesoti jānamāno</i> in the Burmese Sixth Council edition on which SuttaCentral’s Mahāsaṅgīti edition is based. A Sanskrit fragment also supports the PTS/Siamese reading: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>(sā)va(ś)eṣaṁ nira(vaśe)ṣaṁ (iti manyamānaḥ)</i> (SHT IV 500 folio 3V3, cited in Analayo, <em>Comparative Study</em>, p. 611, note 136).

- **PLI**: Analañca te antarāyāya.
- **EN**: It is still capable of harming you.
