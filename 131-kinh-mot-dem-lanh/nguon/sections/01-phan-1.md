## mn131:1.1  `bj12.398, cck14.322, csp1ed11.226, csp2ed11.226, dr13.317, ms11M_764, msdiv272, ndp11.260, nya1, sya14.348, vri14.227`
- **PLI**: Evaṁ me sutaṁ—
- **EN**: So I have heard.
- **NOTE**: A favorite of meditators, this discourse takes a distinctive set of verses as a template for explaining the aspect of meditation called <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vipassanā</i>, commonly rendered “insight”; for why I use “discernment” see <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn14/en/sujato#1.37.5'>DN 14:1.37.5</a> and note. The next three discourses explain the same verses on different occasions, with <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn133/en/sujato'>MN 133</a> offering a complementary explanation.

## mn131:1.2
- **PLI**: ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṁ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme.
- **EN**: At one time the Buddha was staying near Sāvatthī in Jeta’s Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika’s monastery.

## mn131:1.3
- **PLI**: Tatra kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi:
- **EN**: There the Buddha addressed the mendicants,

## mn131:1.4

- **EN**: “Mendicants!”

## mn131:1.5

- **EN**: “Venerable sir,” they replied.

## mn131:1.6

- **EN**: The Buddha said this:

## mn131:3.1  `ms11M_765, nya3`
- **PLI**: “Atītaṁ nānvāgameyya,
- **EN**: “Don’t run back to the past,

## mn131:3.2
- **PLI**: nappaṭikaṅkhe anāgataṁ;
- **EN**: don’t anticipate the future.
- **NOTE**: To “anticipate” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>paṭikaṅkhe</i>) is not always bad. A mendicant ought to “anticipate” dangers (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>pāṭikaṅkhitabba</i>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an4.122/en/sujato#1.1'>AN 4.122:1.1</a> = <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn67/en/sujato#14.2'>MN 67:14.2</a>); Nālaka “anticipates” the arrival of the Buddha (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>patikkhaṁ</i>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/snp3.11/en/sujato#19.4'>Snp 3.11:19.4</a>); and an arahant “awaits their time” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>kālaṁ kaṅkhati</i>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn2.29/en/sujato#15.4'>SN 2.29:15.4</a>). The verse here has a specific application in meditation, which is explained below.

## mn131:3.3
- **PLI**: Yadatītaṁ pahīnaṁ taṁ,
- **EN**: What’s past is left behind,

## mn131:3.4
- **PLI**: appattañca anāgataṁ.
- **EN**: the future has not arrived;

## mn131:3.5  `ms11M_766`
- **PLI**: Paccuppannañca yo dhammaṁ,
- **EN**: and any present phenomenon
- **NOTE**: Here “phenomenon” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>dhamma</i>) means anything that is directly knowable (see <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn133/en/sujato#17.9'>MN 133:17.9</a>). | The two verses run on here, as indicated by the <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ca</i> linking this line with the previous.

## mn131:3.6
- **PLI**: Tattha tattha vipassati;
- **EN**: you clearly discern in every case.
- **NOTE**: For <i lang='pi' translate='no'>tattha tattha</i> as “in every case” see eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn30/en/sujato#2.1.3'>DN 30:2.1.3</a>, and compare <i lang='pi' translate='no'>tato tattha vipassati</i> at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/snp5.15/en/sujato#4.4'>Snp 5.15:4.4</a>.

## mn131:3.7
- **PLI**: Asaṁhīraṁ asaṅkuppaṁ,
- **EN**: The unfaltering, the unshakable:
- **NOTE**: At <a href='https://suttacentral.net/thag14.1/en/sujato#5.1'>Thag 14.1:5.1</a>, this phrase refers to the mind developed in meditation on love and compassion, while at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/snp5.19/en/sujato#19.1'>Snp 5.19:19.1</a> it refers to Nibbāna. In both cases, as here, it is the object of the verb, not an adverb.

## mn131:3.8
- **PLI**: Taṁ vidvā manubrūhaye.
- **EN**: having known that, foster it.

## mn131:3.9  `ms11M_767`

## mn131:4.1  `cck14.323, dr13.318, ms11M_769, msdiv273, ndp11.261, nya4, pts-vp-pli3.188, sya14.349, vri14.228`
- **PLI**: Kathañca, bhikkhave, atītaṁ anvāgameti?
- **EN**: And how do you run back to the past?

## mn131:4.2
- **PLI**: ‘Evaṁrūpo ahosiṁ atītamaddhānan’ti tattha nandiṁ samanvāneti, ‘evaṁvedano ahosiṁ atītamaddhānan’ti tattha nandiṁ samanvāneti, ‘evaṁsañño ahosiṁ atītamaddhānan’ti tattha nandiṁ samanvāneti, ‘evaṁsaṅkhāro ahosiṁ atītamaddhānan’ti tattha nandiṁ samanvāneti, ‘evaṁviññāṇo ahosiṁ atītamaddhānan’ti tattha nandiṁ samanvāneti—
- **EN**: You muster delight there, thinking: ‘I had such form in the past.’ … ‘I had such feeling … perception … choice … consciousness in the past.’
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Samanvāneti</i> is unique in early Pali, but the similar <i lang='pi' translate='no'>samānayi</i> is found at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn30/en/sujato#1.33.4'>DN 30:1.33.4</a> and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/thig12.1/en/sujato#10.2'>Thig 12.1:10.2</a> in the sense of “reunite, bring together”. “Muster” means both to bring together and to arouse. | <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn22.79/en/sujato'>SN 22.79</a> makes it clear that “in the past” means “in a past life”.

## mn131:4.3
- **PLI**: evaṁ kho, bhikkhave, atītaṁ anvāgameti.
- **EN**: That’s how you run back to the past.

## mn131:8.1  `cck14.324, dr13.319, ms11M_773, msdiv275, nya8, pts-vp-pli3.189`
- **PLI**: Kathañca, bhikkhave, paccuppannesu dhammesu saṁhīrati?
- **EN**: And how do you falter amid presently arisen phenomena?
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Dhammesu</i> (“phenomena”) is plural here, whereas in the verse it is singular. Sanskrit fragments, however, have plural in the verse as well.

## mn131:8.2

- **EN**: It’s when an unlearned ordinary person has not seen the noble ones, and is neither skilled nor trained in the teaching of the noble ones. They’ve not seen true persons, and are neither skilled nor trained in the teaching of the true persons.

## mn131:8.3

- **EN**: They regard form as self, self as having form, form in self, or self in form.

- **EN**: They regard feeling …

## mn131:8.5
- **PLI**: saññaṁ …
- **EN**: perception …

## mn131:8.6
- **PLI**: saṅkhāre …
- **EN**: choices …

## mn131:8.7
