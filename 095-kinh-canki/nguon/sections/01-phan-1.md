## mn95:1.2
- **PLI**: ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā kosalesu cārikaṁ caramāno mahatā bhikkhusaṅghena saddhiṁ yena opāsādaṁ nāma kosalānaṁ brāhmaṇagāmo tadavasari.
- **EN**: At one time the Buddha was wandering in the land of the Kosalans together with a large Saṅgha of mendicants when he arrived at a village of the Kosalan brahmins named Opāsāda.
- **NOTE**: Opāsāda does not appear anywhere else. We later hear of Caṅkī’s longhouse (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>pāsāda</i>), a luxurious residence to the north of the town on the way to the Godswood. Perhaps, then, Opāsāda means “below the mansion”.

## mn95:1.3
- **PLI**: Tatra sudaṁ bhagavā opāsāde viharati uttarena opāsādaṁ devavane sālavane.
- **EN**: He stayed in the Godswood of sal trees to the north of Opāsāda.
- **NOTE**: A grove in which offerings were made to the gods.

## mn95:2.1  `nya2`
- **PLI**: Tena kho pana samayena caṅkī brāhmaṇo opāsādaṁ ajjhāvasati sattussadaṁ satiṇakaṭṭhodakaṁ sadhaññaṁ rājabhoggaṁ raññā pasenadinā kosalena dinnaṁ rājadāyaṁ brahmadeyyaṁ.
- **EN**: Now at that time the brahmin Caṅkī was living in Opāsāda. It was a crown property given by King Pasenadi of Kosala, teeming with living creatures, full of hay, wood, water, and grain, a royal park endowed to a brahmin.
- **NOTE**: Caṅkī is regularly mentioned along with other senior brahmins Tārukkha, Pokkharasāti, Jānussoṇi, and Todeyya (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn13/en/sujato#2.2'>DN 13:2.2</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn98/en/sujato#2.2'>MN 98:2.2</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn99/en/sujato#13.5'>MN 99:13.5</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/snp3.9/en/sujato#1.4'>Snp 3.9:1.4</a>). His name is obscure; spelled <i lang='pi' translate='no'>caṅgī</i> in Sanskrit, it is perhaps related to the <i lang='pi' translate='no'>gatāgate caṅgimā</i> of Mahā-subhāṣita-Saṁgraha 7235-1, where it refers to the sensual walk of a young woman; dictionaries record the sense “beautiful” for Kannada <i lang='pi' translate='no'>caṁgi</i> and Sanskrit <i lang='pi' translate='no'>caṅgiman</i>. | “Royal park” is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>rājadāya</i> (cp. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>migadāya</i>, “deer park”). | A <i lang='pi' translate='no'>brahmadeyya</i> is a gift of land by a king to a brahmin, which was an outstanding feature of Indian feudalism. Similar grants are mentioned in the Dīgha Nikāya at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn3/en/sujato#1.2.1'>DN 3:1.2.1</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn5/en/sujato#1.4'>DN 5:1.4</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn12/en/sujato#1.3'>DN 12:1.3</a>, and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn23/en/sujato#1.4'>DN 23:1.4</a>.

## mn95:3.2
- **PLI**: “samaṇo khalu, bho, gotamo sakyaputto sakyakulā pabbajito kosalesu cārikaṁ caramāno mahatā bhikkhusaṅghena saddhiṁ opāsādaṁ anuppatto, opāsāde viharati uttarena opāsādaṁ devavane sālavane.
- **EN**: “It seems the ascetic Gotama—a Sakyan, gone forth from a Sakyan family—has arrived at Opāsāda together with a large Saṅgha of mendicants. He is staying in the Godswood to the north.

## mn95:3.3

## mn95:3.4

- **EN**: ‘That Blessed One is perfected, a fully awakened Buddha, accomplished in knowledge and conduct, holy, knower of the world, supreme guide for those fit for training, teacher of gods and humans, awakened, blessed.’

## mn95:3.5

- **EN**: He has realized with his own insight this world—with its gods, Māras, and divinities, this population with its ascetics and brahmins, gods and humans—and he makes it known to others.

## mn95:3.6

- **EN**: He teaches Dhamma that’s good in the beginning, good in the middle, and good in the end, meaningful and well-phrased. And he reveals a spiritual practice that’s entirely full and pure.

## mn95:3.7
- **PLI**: Sādhu kho pana tathārūpānaṁ arahataṁ dassanaṁ hotī”ti.
- **EN**: It’s good to see such perfected ones.”
