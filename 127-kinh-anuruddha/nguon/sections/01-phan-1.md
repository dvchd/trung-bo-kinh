## mn127:8.1  `cck14.268, csp1ed11.186, csp2ed11.186, dr13.262, ms11M_618, msdiv231, nya8, pts-vp-pli3.147, sya14.287, vri14.187`
- **PLI**: Katamā ca, gahapati, mahaggatā cetovimutti?
- **EN**: And what is the expansive release of the heart?
- **NOTE**: “Expansive” renders <i lang='pi' translate='no'>mahaggata</i>, literally “grown great”. This features as a state of mind that is developed in <i lang='pi' translate='no'>satipaṭṭhāna</i> (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn10/en/sujato#34.10'>MN 10:34.10</a>), and which is known with psychic powers (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn6/en/sujato#16.10'>MN 6:16.10</a>). It also appears just above next to “limitless” in the description of the divine meditations, a detail that perhaps prompted Pañcakaṅga’s question.

## mn127:8.2
- **PLI**: Idha, gahapati, bhikkhu yāvatā ekaṁ rukkhamūlaṁ mahaggatanti pharitvā adhimuccitvā viharati.
- **EN**: It’s when a mendicant meditates focused on pervading the extent of a single tree root as expansive.
- **NOTE**: This meditation practice is unique to this passage. It might be compared with the practice of emptiness at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn121/en/sujato#4.1'>MN 121:4.1</a>, which likewise moves from a perception of one’s limited surroundings toward something more universal. The meaning of “pervading” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>pharitvā</i>) might be understood in the same sense as it is found in the divine meditations, where I have translated it as “spread”. The sutta below, however, treats it as the spreading of light. In this interpretation, the “expansive” practice is a method of spreading love (etc.) by gradually expanding the perception of the field of love. The final extent is the land bordered by ocean, beyond which the mind become “limitless like the great ocean” (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn72/en/sujato#20.2'>MN 72:20.2</a>). In other words, the “expansive” liberation leads to the “limitless” liberation.
