- **EN**: Then several mendicants who had completed the rainy season residence in their native land went to the Buddha, bowed, and sat down to one side. The Buddha said to them:
- **NOTE**: The commentary says <i lang='pi' translate='no'>jātibhūmi</i> means the Buddha’s native land, but the word occurs at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an6.54/en/sujato'>AN 6.54</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-pvr14/en/sujato#2.1'>Pvr 14:2.1</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/ja537/en/sujato#26.1'>Ja 537:26.1</a>, and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/ja546/en/sujato#43.1'>Ja 546:43.1</a>, where it always means “one’s native land”. Given that Puṇṇa, the most famous teacher of their land, was unknown by Sāriputta, it seems likely that it was somewhat remote. One of the Chinese parallels (MA 9) describes him as of light skin and prominent nose, a description that might befit someone from the north-west of India.
