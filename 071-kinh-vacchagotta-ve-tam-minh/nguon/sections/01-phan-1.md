- **PLI**: Evaṁ me sutaṁ—
- **EN**: So I have heard.
- **NOTE**: This begins the “Vacchagotta trilogy”, a series of three suttas tracing Vacchagotta’s spiritual development. These are supplemented by an entire Saṁyutta of questions (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn33/en/sujato'>SN 33</a>). In this sutta he shows an attitude of curiosity and respect, and gains a degree of faith in the Buddha. | This is not the same person as the lay brahmin Vacchagotta of Venāgapura at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an3.63/en/sujato#2.1'>AN 3.63:2.1</a>.

## mn71:1.2
- **PLI**: ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā vesāliyaṁ viharati mahāvane kūṭāgārasālāyaṁ.
- **EN**: At one time the Buddha was staying near Vesālī, at the Great Wood, in the hall with the peaked roof.

## mn71:2.1  `nya2`
- **PLI**: Tena kho pana samayena vacchagotto paribbājako ekapuṇḍarīke paribbājakārāme paṭivasati.
- **EN**: Now at that time the wanderer Vacchagotta was residing in the Single Lotus monastery for the wanderers.
- **NOTE**: Vacchagotta is a clan name; his personal name is not recorded. The Vaccha clan stems from the Vedic seer Vatsa, author of Rig Veda 8.6–11. Several notable figures of this clan are known, including a lineage master in Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 4.5.3, but there is nothing to identify them with the Vacchagotta of the suttas. Given Vacchagotta’s evident interest in the ascetic traditions, it is noteworthy that a certain Śayyambha Vatsagotra, father of Manaka, was said to be a direct disciple of Mahāvīra (Kalpasūtra 10.3).

## mn71:5.2
- **PLI**: ‘samaṇo gotamo sabbaññū sabbadassāvī, aparisesaṁ ñāṇadassanaṁ paṭijānāti,
- **EN**: ‘The ascetic Gotama claims to be all-knowing and all-seeing, to know and see everything without exception, thus:
- **NOTE**: Such extraordinary claims were not unprecedented, as both the Jain leader Mahāvīra (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn14/en/sujato#17.2'>MN 14:17.2</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn79/en/sujato#6.7'>MN 79:6.7</a>) and Pūraṇa Kassapa (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/an9.38/en/sujato#2.1'>AN 9.38:2.1</a>) are said to claim omniscience in the Pali canon. Extant Jain texts have no shortage of such claims (eg. Sūyagaḍa1.6, Kalpasūtra 1, Bhagavatisūtra 4.10, etc.), and it seems likely that this was part of Mahāvīra’s teachings. Sources for Pūraṇa Kassapa are slim, but he was regarded as a great teacher of the Ājīvakas, whose founder the bamboo-staffed ascetic Gosāla was said to be omniscient (Basham, <em>History and Doctrines of the Ājīvikas</em>, 275–6).

## mn71:5.4

- **EN**: I trust those who say this repeat what the Buddha has said, and do not misrepresent him with an untruth? Is their explanation in line with the teaching? Are there any legitimate grounds for rebuttal and criticism?”

## mn71:5.5  `cck13.242, mc2.161, ms10M_519`
- **PLI**: “Ye te, vaccha, evamāhaṁsu: ‘samaṇo gotamo sabbaññū sabbadassāvī, aparisesaṁ ñāṇadassanaṁ paṭijānāti, carato ca me tiṭṭhato ca suttassa ca jāgarassa ca satataṁ samitaṁ ñāṇadassanaṁ paccupaṭṭhitan’ti, na me te vuttavādino, abbhācikkhanti ca pana maṁ asatā abhūtenā”ti.
- **EN**: “Vaccha, those who say this do not repeat what I have said. They misrepresent me with what is false and untrue.”
- **NOTE**: At <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an4.24/en/sujato#2.1'>AN 4.24:2.1</a> the Buddha claims to know whatever is “seen, heard, thought, known, attained, sought, and explored by the mind”, which is a complete knowledge of subjective experience. In the curious dialogue at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn90/en/sujato#8.5'>MN 90:8.5</a> the Buddha denies that anyone “will know” all things simultaneously. The Milindapañha reversed this to the positive statement that the Buddha could know all things, just not simultaneously (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mil5.1.2/en/sujato#1.2'>Mil 5.1.2:1.2</a>). The Buddha’s omniscience became the standard view of the later books of the Pali canon (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/cnd10/en/sujato#5.2'>Cnd 10:5.2</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/bv2/en/sujato#55.3'>Bv 2:55.3</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pp1.6/en/sujato#2.1'>Pp 1.6:2.1</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/kv3.1/en/sujato#5.3'>Kv 3.1:5.3</a>, etc.).

## mn71:11.2
- **PLI**: “atthi nu kho, bho gotama, koci gihī gihisaṁyojanaṁ appahāya kāyassa bhedā dukkhassantakaro”ti?
- **EN**: “Worthy Gotama, are there any laypeople who, without giving up the fetter of lay life, make an end of suffering when the body breaks up?”
- **NOTE**: Vacchagotta uses the phrase <i lang='pi' translate='no'>gihisaṁyojana</i> (“fetter of lay life”) for the inner attachment to lay life, which is not overcome by the mere act of joining a religious order. Compare the Buddha’s term <i lang='pi' translate='no'>gehasita</i>, the thoughts and memories “of the lay life” (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn21/en/sujato#6.2'>MN 21:6.2</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn119/en/sujato#4.7'>MN 119:4.7</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn125/en/sujato#23.5'>MN 125:23.5</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn137/en/sujato#9.3'>MN 137:9.3</a>). See also <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn54/en/sujato'>MN 54</a>, where the external “signs” of a householder are connected with the fetters.
