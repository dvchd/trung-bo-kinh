## mn149:2.1  `nya2`
- **PLI**: “mahāsaḷāyatanikaṁ vo, bhikkhave, desessāmi.
- **EN**: “Mendicants, I shall teach you the great discourse on what relates to the six sense fields.
- **NOTE**: For <i lang='pi' translate='no'>āyatanika</i> see <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn121/en/sujato#10.5'>MN 121:10.5</a>, where it refers to the life faculty of the body that is “related to the six sense fields”; there are also heavens and hells “related to the six fields of contact” (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn50/en/sujato#22.2'>MN 50:22.2</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sb35/en/sujato#35.135'>Sb 35:35.135</a>). In each case it refers to something that is connected to or related to the six sense fields, as indicated by the suffix <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ika</i>. Here this refers to the fact that this discourse treats multiple distinct teaching frameworks in relation to the six sense fields. These include the majority of the <i lang='pi' translate='no'>saṁyutta-mātikā</i>, the list of topics making up the Saṁyutta Nikāya. And this inclusive and integrative nature in turn explains why the teaching is “great”.

## mn149:3.2  `ms11M_1227, pts-vp-pli3.288, sya14.522`
- **PLI**: Tassa sārattassa saṁyuttassa sammūḷhassa assādānupassino viharato āyatiṁ pañcupādānakkhandhā upacayaṁ gacchanti.
- **EN**: Someone who lives aroused like this—fettered, confused, concentrating on gratification—accumulates the five grasping aggregates for themselves in the future.
- **NOTE**: In other words, one who becomes attached to sense experience creates kamma that generates a new life consisting of the five aggregates. This passage integrates the teachings on the six sense fields and the five aggregates under the first noble truth, as well as briefly implying the basic principle of dependent origination.
