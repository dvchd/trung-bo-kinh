## mn28:37.1  `nya37`
- **PLI**: mano aparibhinno hoti, bāhirā ca dhammā na āpāthaṁ āgacchanti no ca tajjo samannāhāro hoti, neva tāva tajjassa viññāṇabhāgassa pātubhāvo hoti.
- **EN**: mind is intact internally, so long as exterior ideas don’t come into range and there’s no corresponding engagement, there’s no manifestation of the corresponding type of consciousness.
- **NOTE**: The “mind” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>mano</i>, Sanskrit <i lang='pi' translate='no'>manas</i>) here is presumably what is elsewhere called the “mind element” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>manodhātu</i>, eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn155/en/sujato#4.8'>MN 155:4.8</a>), since both are connected with “ideas” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>dhammā</i>) and “mind consciousness” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>manoviññāṇa</i>). The exact nature of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>mano</i> here is not specified in early texts. Judging from this sutta, it parallels the physical basis for the other five kinds of consciousness (already in Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 6.1.8: “seeing through the eye, hearing through the ear, knowing through the mind”), in which case it might be the physical basis for consciousness. The suttas do not specify what this is; tradition calls it the <i lang='pi' translate='no'>hadayavatthu</i>, the “heart basis”, while today we would recognize it as the brain. Compare Aitareya Upaniṣad, which says that the mind (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>manas</i>) creates the heart, which in turn creates the moon (1.1.4); when man was formed, the moon, having become <i lang='pi' translate='no'>manas</i>, entered the heart (1.2.4); mind and heart are then said to be the same (3.2). As with the other senses, the mind remains inert until actuated by the Self. Leaving aside the metaphysics, the conception of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>manas</i> as the underlying potential for consciousness is similar, regardless of whether this was a physical organ, or a process of ongoing consciousness awaiting stimulation per the commentary (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>bhavaṅgacitta</i>). The Abhidhamma Vibhaṅga alludes to this passage when it says that the mind element arises either following sense stimulus, or else at the “initial engagement with all mental phenomena” (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/vb3/en/sujato#48.6'>Vb 3:48.6</a>).

- **EN**: Though the mind is intact internally and exterior ideas come into range, so long as there’s no corresponding engagement, there’s no manifestation of the corresponding type of consciousness.
