## mn93:1.1  `bj11.610, cck13.541, dr13.510, ms10M_1238, msdiv401, ndp10.403, nya1, pts-vp-pli2.147, sya13.558, vri13.362`
- **PLI**: Evaṁ me sutaṁ—
- **EN**: So I have heard.
- **NOTE**: This discourse records how the brahmin student Assalāyana took on the Buddha on the question of the four classes, an attempt that Assalāyana knew was doomed from the start. Like similar contests such as the Ambaṭṭhasutta (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn3/en/sujato'>DN 3</a>), it is rich in historical connections.

## mn93:1.2
- **PLI**: ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṁ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme.
- **EN**: At one time the Buddha was staying near Sāvatthī in Jeta’s Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika’s monastery.

## mn93:2.1  `nya2`
- **PLI**: Tena kho pana samayena nānāverajjakānaṁ brāhmaṇānaṁ pañcamattāni brāhmaṇasatāni sāvatthiyaṁ paṭivasanti kenacideva karaṇīyena.
- **EN**: Now at that time around five hundred brahmins from abroad were residing in Sāvatthī on some business.
- **NOTE**: Similar gatherings were noted at Campā (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn4/en/sujato#4.1'>DN 4:4.1</a>) and Opāsāda (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn95/en/sujato#7.1'>MN 95:7.1</a>).

- **EN**: Then those brahmins thought,

## mn93:2.3

## mn93:3.1  `nya3`
- **PLI**: Tena kho pana samayena assalāyano nāma māṇavo sāvatthiyaṁ paṭivasati daharo, vuttasiro, soḷasavassuddesiko jātiyā, tiṇṇaṁ vedānaṁ pāragū sanighaṇḍukeṭubhānaṁ sākkharappabhedānaṁ itihāsapañcamānaṁ, padako, veyyākaraṇo, lokāyatamahāpurisalakkhaṇesu anavayo.
- **EN**: Now at that time the student Assalāyana was residing in Sāvatthī. He was young, tonsured, and sixteen years old. He had mastered the three Vedas, together with their vocabularies and ritual performance, their phonology and word classification, and the testaments as fifth. He knew them word-by-word, and their grammar. He was well versed in cosmology and the marks of a great man.
- **NOTE**: While this description is similar to that of brahmin students such as Ambaṭṭha (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn3/en/sujato#1.3.1'>DN 3:1.3.1</a>), it is only here and at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn95/en/sujato#11.2'>MN 95:11.2</a> that we find the additional details that they are tonsured and sixteen years of age. | “Tonsured” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>vuttasira</i>) literally means “circlehead” and it refers to the practice of shaving off all but a topknot (cf. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nivṛttacūḍaka</i> at Manusmṛti 5.67). According to Manusmṛti 2.65, the ceremony of tonsure (there called <i lang='pi' translate='no'>keśānta</i>) is to be given to brahmins at sixteen, so this must be meant here. Contrast the usual brahmanical pejorative of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>muṇḍaka</i> (“shaveling”, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn81/en/sujato#6.8'>MN 81:6.8</a>).

## mn93:3.4
- **PLI**: So kho pahoti samaṇena gotamena saddhiṁ asmiṁ vacane paṭimantetun”ti.
- **EN**: “This Assalāyana is capable of debating with the ascetic Gotama about this.”
- **NOTE**: Assalāyana may be identified with Kauśalyaṣcāśvalāyana (“Assalāyana of Kosala”) who asked the sage Pippalāda about the nature of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>prāṇa</i> (breath/life energy) in Praśna Upaniṣad 3. He was probably a grandson or further descendant (as indicated by the <i lang='pi' translate='no'>naḍādi</i> formation of his name) of the Aśvala of Videha who debated Yājñavalkya (see note below).

## mn93:4.7
- **PLI**: Nāhaṁ sakkomi samaṇena gotamena saddhiṁ asmiṁ vacane paṭimantetun”ti.
- **EN**: I’m not capable of debating with the ascetic Gotama about this.”
- **NOTE**: This dialogue is modeled after the debate at Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 3.1 between Yājñavalkya and Aśvala. Foreign brahmins had come to Videha from Kuru and Pañcāla, the older-established and more prestigious Brahmanical lands (cf. the “western brahmins” of <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn42.6/en/sujato#2.1'>SN 42.6:2.1</a> and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an10.176/en/sujato#1.5'>AN 10.176:1.5</a>). King Janaka offered an extravagant prize of a thousand cows with gold-tipped horns for the greatest brahmin among them. But none dared step forward, so Yājñavalkya started to drive the cows home. Aśvala, the priest in charge of litany (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>hotṛ</i>) for Janaka, challenged him to a debate, but was ultimately silenced.
