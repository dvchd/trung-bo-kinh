## mn72:20.1  `bj11.264, ms10M_552, msdiv192, ndp10.182, nya20, pts-vp-pli1.488`
- **PLI**: “Evameva kho, vaccha, yena rūpena tathāgataṁ paññāpayamāno paññāpeyya taṁ rūpaṁ tathāgatassa pahīnaṁ ucchinnamūlaṁ tālāvatthukataṁ anabhāvaṅkataṁ āyatiṁ anuppādadhammaṁ.
- **EN**: “In the same way, Vaccha, any form by which a realized one might be described has been given up, cut off at the root, made like a palm stump, obliterated, and unable to arise in the future.
- **NOTE**: Here “realized one” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>tathāgata</i>) pertains to any arahant, as at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn54.12/en/sujato'>SN 54.12</a>. The Pali commentary and one of the Chinese parallels here (SA2 196 at T ii 445a18) agree in saying that a “living being” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>satta</i>) is meant. However, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>tathāgata</i> and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>satta</i> are exact verbal parallels: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>tathā</i> (“real”) + <i lang='pi' translate='no'>gata</i> (“come to the state of”) and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sa</i> (“real”) + <i lang='pi' translate='no'>tta</i> (“state of”). Thus it seems likely that this was originally intended as a mere verbal gloss to explain the compound.
