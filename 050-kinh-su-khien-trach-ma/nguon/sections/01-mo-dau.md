## mn50:2.2
- **PLI**: Tena kho pana samayena māro pāpimā āyasmato mahāmoggallānassa kucchigato hoti koṭṭhamanupaviṭṭho.
- **EN**: Now at that time Māra the Wicked had got inside Moggallāna’s belly.
- **NOTE**: Māra is sometimes called Namuci, and in fact seems to descend from the Vedic adversary of that name. Vedic Namuci “the wicked” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>pāpmā vai namuciḥ</i>, Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 12.7.3, Maitrāyaṇī Saṁhitā 4.4.4) was a wily trickster (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>māyin</i>, Rig Veda 1.53.7c) who was nonetheless outsmarted by Indra (Rig Veda 5.30.6c), and possessed ineffectual armies reliant on women (Rig Veda 5.30.9a). Buddhist Māra “the wicked” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>māro pāpimā</i>) was a wily trickster (<em>passim</em>) who was nonetheless outsmarted by the Buddha and his followers (<em>passim</em>); he possessed ineffectual armies (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn20/en/sujato#21.3'>DN 20:21.3</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an4.13/en/sujato#2.3'>AN 4.13:2.3</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/snp3.2/en/sujato'>Snp 3.2</a>), and sent his daughters to do his dirty work (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn4.25/en/sujato'>SN 4.25</a>). The decapitated head of Namuci introduced death (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>mṛtyu</i>) in the form of blood to the deathless (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>amṛta</i>) soma (Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 12.7.3.4, Maitrāyaṇī Saṁhitā 4.4.4), establishing his Pali names Maccurāja, “King of Death” and Māra, “Death”. He appears in a similar guise in Jainism too (Ācaraṅga 1.3.1.3).
