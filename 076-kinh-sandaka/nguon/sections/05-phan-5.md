## mn76:53.1  `bj11.330, ms10M_661, msdiv236, nya53, pts-vp-pli1.524, sya13.309`
- **PLI**: “Kīvabahukā pana, bho ānanda, imasmiṁ dhammavinaye niyyātāro”ti?
- **EN**: “But Reverend Ānanda, in this teaching and training, how many are emancipated?”
- **NOTE**: “(Those who are) emancipated” is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>niyyātāro</i>, a unique occurrence of an agent noun from <i lang='pi' translate='no'>niyyāti</i>, “to go out”. This was probably a technical term in Sandaka’s system.

## mn76:53.2
- **PLI**: “Na kho, sandaka, ekaṁyeva sataṁ na dve satāni na tīṇi satāni na cattāri satāni na pañca satāni, atha kho bhiyyova ye imasmiṁ dhammavinaye niyyātāro”ti.
- **EN**: “There are not just one hundred who are emancipated, Sandaka, or two or three or four or five hundred, but many more than that in this teaching and training.”

## mn76:53.3
- **PLI**: “Acchariyaṁ, bho ānanda, abbhutaṁ, bho ānanda.
- **EN**: “It’s incredible, worthy Ānanda, it’s amazing!

## mn76:53.4
- **PLI**: Na ca nāma sadhammokkaṁsanā bhavissati, na paradhammavambhanā, āyatane ca dhammadesanā tāva bahukā ca niyyātāro paññāyissanti.
- **EN**: Namely, that there’s no glorifying one’s own teaching and putting down the teaching of others. The Dhamma is taught in its own field, and so many emancipated are recognized.

## mn76:53.5
- **PLI**: Ime panājīvakā puttamatāya puttā attānañceva ukkaṁsenti, pare ca vambhenti tayo ceva niyyātāro paññapenti, seyyathidaṁ—
- **EN**: But these Ājīvakas, sons of she whose sons are dead, glorify themselves and put others down. And they only recognize three who have been emancipated:
- **NOTE**: The commentary takes <i lang='pi' translate='no'>mata</i> as “dead” and the compound <i lang='pi' translate='no'>puttamatāya</i> as genitive feminine, yielding the sense, “she whose sons are dead”, explaining that Sandaka thought, “Those Ājīvakas are surely dead; their mother is she whose sons are dead”. Perhaps, however, “she whose sons are dead” is an allusion to a child-killing <i lang='pi' translate='no'>yakkhinī</i> such as Hārītī of Madhurā or Kālī in the commentary to <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dhp5/en/sujato'>Dhp 5</a>, thus having the force, “sons of a she-devil”. Either way this is a curse or slur intruding into a passage whose intent is to highlight the Buddha’s fair-minded treatment of others. A near-identical phrase is used of brutal bandits at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/ja79/en/sujato#1.3'>Ja 79:1.3</a>. The compound is inverted at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn5.3/en/sujato#2.1'>SN 5.3:2.1</a>, but seems to have the same meaning.

- **EN**: Nanda Vaccha, Kisa Saṅkicca, and the bamboo-staffed ascetic Gosāla.”
