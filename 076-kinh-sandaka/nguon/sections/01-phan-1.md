## mn76:7.1  `dr13.268, mc2.199, ms10M_636, msdiv225, ndp10.213, nya7, sya13.291`
- **PLI**: “Idha, sandaka, ekacco satthā evaṁvādī hoti evaṁdiṭṭhi:
- **EN**: “Sandaka, take a certain teacher who has this doctrine and view:

## mn76:7.2
- **PLI**: ‘natthi dinnaṁ, natthi yiṭṭhaṁ, natthi hutaṁ, natthi sukatadukkaṭānaṁ kammānaṁ phalaṁ vipāko, natthi ayaṁ loko, natthi paroloko, natthi mātā, natthi pitā, natthi sattā opapātikā, natthi loke samaṇabrāhmaṇā sammaggatā sammāpaṭipannā ye imañca lokaṁ parañca lokaṁ sayaṁ abhiññā sacchikatvā pavedenti.
- **EN**: ‘There’s no meaning in giving, sacrifice, or offerings. There’s no fruit or result of good and bad deeds. There’s no afterlife. There’s no such thing as mother and father, or beings that are reborn spontaneously. And there’s no ascetic or brahmin who is rightly comported and rightly practiced, and who describes the afterlife after realizing it with their own insight.
- **NOTE**: Attributed to Ajita of the hair blanket at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn2/en/sujato#23.2'>DN 2:23.2</a>. | The denial of “mother and father” is usually interpreted as the denial of moral duty toward one’s parents. However, I think it is a doctrine of conception which denies that a child is created by the mother and father. Rather, the child is produced by the four elements, with parents as mere instigators and incubators.

## mn76:7.3
- **PLI**: Cātumahābhūtiko ayaṁ puriso yadā kālaṁ karoti, pathavī pathavīkāyaṁ anupeti anupagacchati, āpo āpokāyaṁ anupeti anupagacchati, tejo tejokāyaṁ anupeti anupagacchati, vāyo vāyokāyaṁ anupeti anupagacchati, ākāsaṁ indriyāni saṅkamanti.
- **EN**: This person is made up of the four principal states. When they die, the earth in their body progresses and proceeds to the substance of earth. The water in their body progresses and proceeds to the substance of water. The fire in their body progresses and proceeds to the substance of fire. The air in their body progresses and proceeds to the substance of air. The faculties are transferred to space.
- **NOTE**: This is a materialist analysis of the person. | The word <i lang='pi' translate='no'>kāya</i> (“substance”) is central to Jainism. Ācārāṅgasūtra 8.1.11 speaks of the “substances” of earth, water, fire, and air as being imbued with life so one should avoid damaging them.
