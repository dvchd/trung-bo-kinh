- **PLI**: “Seyyathāpi, bhaddāli, dakkho assadamako bhadraṁ assājānīyaṁ labhitvā paṭhameneva mukhādhāne kāraṇaṁ kāreti.

## mn65:33.2
- **PLI**: Tassa mukhādhāne kāraṇaṁ kāriyamānassa hontiyeva visūkāyitāni visevitāni vipphanditāni kānici kānici, yathā taṁ akāritapubbaṁ kāraṇaṁ kāriyamānassa.

## mn65:33.3
- **PLI**: So abhiṇhakāraṇā anupubbakāraṇā tasmiṁ ṭhāne parinibbāyati.
- **EN**: But with regular and gradual practice its bad behavior is extinguished.
- **NOTE**: For the phrase <i lang='pi' translate='no'>tasmiṁ ṭhāne parinibbāyati</i> (“its bad behavior is extinguished”, literally “it is extinguished regarding that state”) I follow the commentary, which specifies that <i lang='pi' translate='no'>tasmiṁ ṭhāne</i> refers to the twists and dodges that cease rather than the good habits that are developed.

- **EN**: When it has done this, the horse trainer next makes it get used to wearing the harness.

## mn65:33.5
- **PLI**: Tassa yugādhāne kāraṇaṁ kāriyamānassa hontiyeva visūkāyitāni visevitāni vipphanditāni kānici kānici, yathā taṁ akāritapubbaṁ kāraṇaṁ kāriyamānassa.
- **EN**: Because it has not done this before, it still resorts to some twists, ducks, and dodges.

## mn65:33.6

- **EN**: But with regular and gradual practice its bad behavior is extinguished.

## mn65:33.7

- **EN**: When it has done this, the horse trainer next makes it get used to walking in procession, circling, prancing, galloping, charging, the protocols and customs of court, and in the very best speed, fleetness, and camaraderie.
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Rājaguṇe</i> occurs in <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mil5.1.4/en/sujato'>Mil 5.1.4</a>, where it distinguishes royal officials from ordinary ones.
