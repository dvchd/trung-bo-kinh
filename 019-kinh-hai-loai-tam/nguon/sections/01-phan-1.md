# Phần 1  (50 đoạn)

## mn19:1.1  `bj10.288, cck12.217, dr12.208, ms9M_717, msdiv206, ndp9.153, nya1, sya12.232, vri12.163`
- **PLI**: Evaṁ me sutaṁ—
- **EN**: So I have heard.
- **NOTE**: This discourse shows that a meditator must abandon unwholesome thought then wholesome thought before entering absorption. It is one of several discourses that give detailed instructions on dealing with thought in meditation (eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn18/en/sujato'>MN 18</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn20/en/sujato'>MN 20</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an3.101/en/sujato'>AN 3.101</a>). While meditation existed before the Buddha, we do not find this kind of practical advice on how to go about it.

## mn19:2.1  `csp1ed9.162, csp2ed9.162, ms9M_718, nya2`
- **PLI**: “Pubbeva me, bhikkhave, sambodhā anabhisambuddhassa bodhisattasseva sato etadahosi:
- **EN**: “Mendicants, before my awakening—when I was still unawakened but intent on awakening—I thought:
- **NOTE**: This period of meditative development must have taken place after giving up self-mortification practices. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn36/en/sujato#34.1'>MN 36:34.1</a> says that at this point, after restoring his strength by eating solid food, he developed the absorptions, giving the impression that this happened immediately. However, the current sutta, supported by passages such as <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn128/en/sujato#16.2'>MN 128:16.2</a>, shows that this took some time, although it is not clear how long.

## mn19:2.2
- **PLI**: ‘yannūnāhaṁ dvidhā katvā dvidhā katvā vitakke vihareyyan’ti.
- **EN**: ‘Why don’t I meditate by continually dividing my thoughts into two classes?’

## mn19:2.3
- **PLI**: So kho ahaṁ, bhikkhave, yo cāyaṁ kāmavitakko yo ca byāpādavitakko yo ca vihiṁsāvitakko—
- **EN**: So I assigned sensual, malicious, and cruel thoughts
- **NOTE**: By analyzing thoughts (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>vitakka</i>), he is consciously developing the second factor of the noble eightfold path, right thought (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>sammāsaṅkappa</i>). In this context, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vitakka</i> and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>saṅkappa</i> are synonyms.

## mn19:2.4
- **PLI**: imaṁ ekaṁ bhāgamakāsiṁ;
- **EN**: to one class.

## mn19:2.5
- **PLI**: yo cāyaṁ nekkhammavitakko yo ca abyāpādavitakko yo ca avihiṁsāvitakko—
- **EN**: And I assigned thoughts of renunciation, good will, and harmlessness

## mn19:2.6
- **PLI**: imaṁ dutiyaṁ bhāgamakāsiṁ.
- **EN**: to the second class.

## mn19:3.1  `cck12.218, dr12.209, ms9M_719, msdiv207, nya3, pts-vp-pli1.115, sya12.233`

- **EN**: Then, as I meditated—diligent, keen, and resolute—a sensual thought arose.

## mn19:3.2
- **PLI**: So evaṁ pajānāmi:
- **EN**: I understood:

## mn19:3.3
- **PLI**: ‘uppanno kho me ayaṁ kāmavitakko.
- **EN**: ‘This sensual thought has arisen in me.
- **NOTE**: First one knows the thought, understanding it in terms of the framework.

## mn19:3.4
- **PLI**: So ca kho attabyābādhāyapi saṁvattati, parabyābādhāyapi saṁvattati, ubhayabyābādhāyapi saṁvattati, paññānirodhiko vighātapakkhiko anibbānasaṁvattaniko’.
- **EN**: It leads to hurting myself, hurting others, and hurting both. It blocks wisdom, it’s on the side of distress, and it doesn’t lead to extinguishment.’
- **NOTE**: Then one reflects on the causal outcome of the thought.

## mn19:3.5
- **PLI**: ‘Attabyābādhāya saṁvattatī’tipi me, bhikkhave, paṭisañcikkhato abbhatthaṁ gacchati;
- **EN**: When I reflected that it leads to hurting myself, it went away.
- **NOTE**: Thoughts are eradicated not by force or judgment, but by reflective wisdom.

## mn19:3.6
- **PLI**: ‘parabyābādhāya saṁvattatī’tipi me, bhikkhave, paṭisañcikkhato abbhatthaṁ gacchati;

## mn19:3.7
- **PLI**: ‘ubhayabyābādhāya saṁvattatī’tipi me, bhikkhave, paṭisañcikkhato abbhatthaṁ gacchati;
- **EN**: When I reflected that it leads to hurting both, it went away.

## mn19:3.8
- **PLI**: ‘paññānirodhiko vighātapakkhiko anibbānasaṁvattaniko’tipi me, bhikkhave, paṭisañcikkhato abbhatthaṁ gacchati.
- **EN**: When I reflected that it blocks wisdom, it’s on the side of distress, and it doesn’t lead to extinguishment, it went away.

## mn19:3.9

- **EN**: So I gave up, dispelled, and eliminated any sensual thoughts that arose.

## mn19:4-5.1  `ms9M_720, msdiv208, ndp9.154, nya4-5, vri12.164`
- **PLI**: Tassa mayhaṁ, bhikkhave, evaṁ appamattassa ātāpino pahitattassa viharato uppajjati byāpādavitakko …pe…
- **EN**: Then, as I meditated—diligent, keen, and resolute—a malicious thought arose …
- **NOTE**: The difference between “malice” (or “ill will”, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>byāpāda</i>) and “cruelty” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>vihiṁsā</i>) is subtle; they are the respective opposites of “love” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>mettā</i>) and “compassion” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>karuṇā</i>). <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Mettā</i> wishes well simply and without qualification, just as “malice” wishes ill. But <i lang='pi' translate='no'>karuṇa</i> takes pleasure in the alleviation of pain, while <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vihiṁsā</i> takes pleasure in inflicting pain.

## mn19:6.1  `bj10.290, cck12.219, csp1ed9.163, csp2ed9.163, dr12.210, ms9M_721, nya6, sya12.234`
- **PLI**: Yaññadeva, bhikkhave, bhikkhu bahulamanuvitakketi anuvicāreti, tathā tathā nati hoti cetaso.
- **EN**: Whatever a mendicant frequently thinks about and considers becomes their heart’s inclination.
- **NOTE**: This is a key principle underlying Buddhist meditation. Compare Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 10.5.2.20, where the form in which one worships the divinity determines the divine manifestation.

## mn19:6.2
- **PLI**: Kāmavitakkañce, bhikkhave, bhikkhu bahulamanuvitakketi anuvicāreti, pahāsi nekkhammavitakkaṁ, kāmavitakkaṁ bahulamakāsi, tassa taṁ kāmavitakkāya cittaṁ namati.
- **EN**: If they often think about and consider sensual thoughts, they’ve given up the thought of renunciation to cultivate sensual thought. Their mind inclines to sensual thoughts.

## mn19:6.3
- **PLI**: Byāpādavitakkañce, bhikkhave …pe…
- **EN**: If they often think about and consider malicious thoughts … their mind inclines to malicious thoughts.

## mn19:6.4
- **PLI**: vihiṁsāvitakkañce, bhikkhave, bhikkhu bahulamanuvitakketi anuvicāreti, pahāsi avihiṁsāvitakkaṁ, vihiṁsāvitakkaṁ bahulamakāsi, tassa taṁ vihiṁsāvitakkāya cittaṁ namati.
- **EN**: If they often think about and consider cruel thoughts … their mind inclines to cruel thoughts.

## mn19:7.1  `nya7`
- **PLI**: Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, vassānaṁ pacchime māse saradasamaye kiṭṭhasambādhe gopālako gāvo rakkheyya.
- **EN**: Suppose it’s the last month of the rainy season, in autumn, when the crops grow closely together, and a cowherd must take care of the cattle.
- **NOTE**: Compare <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an3.101/en/sujato'>AN 3.101</a>, where the Buddha illustrates the same point with a simile of smelting gold.

## mn19:7.2
- **PLI**: So tā gāvo tato tato daṇḍena ākoṭeyya paṭikoṭeyya sannirundheyya sannivāreyya.
- **EN**: He’d tap and poke them with his staff on this side and that to keep them in check.

## mn19:7.3
- **PLI**: Taṁ kissa hetu?
- **EN**: Why is that?

## mn19:7.4
- **PLI**: Passati hi so, bhikkhave, gopālako tatonidānaṁ vadhaṁ vā bandhanaṁ vā jāniṁ vā garahaṁ vā.
- **EN**: For he sees that if they wander into the crops he could be executed, imprisoned, fined, or condemned.
- **NOTE**: At this point, a meditator guards against unwholesome thoughts, gently and persistently.

## mn19:7.5
- **PLI**: Evameva kho ahaṁ, bhikkhave, addasaṁ akusalānaṁ dhammānaṁ ādīnavaṁ okāraṁ saṅkilesaṁ, kusalānaṁ dhammānaṁ nekkhamme ānisaṁsaṁ vodānapakkhaṁ.
- **EN**: In the same way, I saw that unskillful qualities have the drawbacks of sordidness and corruption, and that skillful qualities have the benefit and cleansing power of renunciation.
