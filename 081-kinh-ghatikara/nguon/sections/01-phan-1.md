- **NOTE**: This lively story is one of the most developed of the early canonical Jātakas. The popularity of the story is attested by the many parallels, which include a retelling of the story by the deity who had been Ghaṭīkāra (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn1.50/en/sujato'>SN 1.50</a>). - **NOTE**: Each of the occasions when the Buddha smiled in this way served as an introduction to an uplifting story of the past. In <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn83/en/sujato'>MN 83</a>, the Buddha tells of the fabled king Maghadeva of Mithilā, who went forth and practice the divine meditations. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an5.180/en/sujato'>AN 5.180</a>, like the current sutta, tells the story of a virtuous layperson in the time of Kassapa Buddha. - **NOTE**: Various texts of the northern Sarvāstivāda movement, including the Madhyama-āgama and the Saṅghabhedavastu, speak instead of the potter Nandīpāla and the Brahmin youth Uttara. | Ghaṭīkāra (“potter”) Nandīpāla is also referred to by his clan name Bhaggava. | Jotipāla means “guardian of the sacred flame”, i.e. someone who maintains the Vedic fire ritual, as attested by another Jotipāla of the past (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn19/en/sujato#47.26'>DN 19:47.26</a>). ## mn81:6.4
- **PLI**: ‘āyāma, samma jotipāla, kassapaṁ bhagavantaṁ arahantaṁ sammāsambuddhaṁ dassanāya upasaṅkamissāma.
- **EN**: ‘Come, dear Jotipāla, let’s go to see the Blessed One Kassapa, the perfected one, the fully awakened Buddha.
- **NOTE**: Kassapa (Sanskrit Kaśyapa) means “tortoise”. It is a common Brahmanical clan name, stemming from an ancient figure reckoned as the eldest of the “seven sages”, to whom some Vedic verses are attributed. Further details of the Buddha Kassapa’s time are found at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn14/en/sujato#1.4.6'>DN 14:1.4.6</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn15.20/en/sujato#4.1'>SN 15.20:4.1</a>, and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn48.57/en/sujato#3.1'>SN 48.57:3.1</a>.

## mn81:6.5
- **PLI**: Sādhusammatañhi me tassa bhagavato dassanaṁ arahato sammāsambuddhassā’ti.
- **EN**: For I deem it holy to see that Blessed One.’
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Sādhusammata</i> is normally an epithet of (real or supposed) saints. Here it is the “sight” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>dassana</i>) of the saint that is deemed holy. This is an early example of the belief known in Hinduism as <i lang='pi' translate='no'>darshana</i>, that the sight of a holy person, place, or object was auspicious and conveyed blessings on the seer. This topic is also treated at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/snp4.4/en/sujato'>Snp 4.4</a> and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an6.30/en/sujato'>AN 6.30</a>.

## mn81:6.16
- **PLI**: ‘Tena hi, samma jotipāla, sottisināniṁ ādāya nadiṁ gamissāma sināyitun’ti.
- **EN**: ‘Well then, dear Jotipāla, let’s take some bathing cleanser and go to the river to bathe.’
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Sotti</i> is uncertain. I take it as from the root <i lang='pi' translate='no'>suc</i> in the sense “cleanser”. However, it may be related to Sanskrit <i lang='pi' translate='no'>śukti</i> (from the same root), which is mentioned by Varāhamihira as an ingredient in perfumed bath powders fit for a king (Bṛhat Saṁhitā 77). There, it is probably either powdered or ashen oyster shell, which is included as an exfoliant in some modern soaps, or else perfume from a plant whose leaves or flowers resemble oysters. The commentary, identifying it with the <i lang='pi' translate='no'>kuruvindakasutti</i> that was banned for mendicants at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd15/en/sujato#1.3.18'>Kd 15:1.3.18</a>, evidently derives it from <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sutta</i> (“string”) and explains it as a string of resin-balls compounded with <i lang='pi' translate='no'>kuruvindaka</i> (itself uncertain, possibly a herb). However, these explanations all seem dubious, as Pali texts treat <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sotti</i> as a normal means of washing, and should not require specialized ingredients or methods (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/an3.70/en/sujato#8.2'>AN 3.70:8.2</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn93/en/sujato#10.5'>MN 93:10.5</a>).
