## mn58:1.2
- **PLI**: ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā rājagahe viharati veḷuvane kalandakanivāpe.
- **EN**: At one time the Buddha was staying near Rājagaha in the Bamboo Grove, the squirrels’ feeding ground.
- **NOTE**: This sutta is framed as a response to a challenge by Mahāvīra, the Jain leader. It shows the Buddha’s characteristic dry humor to undermine religious pretensions (see eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn76/en/sujato#21.4'>MN 76:21.4</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn11/en/sujato#82.7'>DN 11:82.7</a>). The dialogue slyly shows the Buddha defeating Mahāvīra by using one of Mahāvīra’s key methods against him.

## mn58:2.1  `nya2`
- **PLI**: Atha kho abhayo rājakumāro yena nigaṇṭho nāṭaputto tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā nigaṇṭhaṁ nāṭaputtaṁ abhivādetvā ekamantaṁ nisīdi. Ekamantaṁ nisinnaṁ kho abhayaṁ rājakumāraṁ nigaṇṭho nāṭaputto etadavoca:
- **EN**: Then Prince Abhaya went up to the Jain ascetic of the Ñātika clan, bowed, and sat down to one side. The Jain Ñātika said to him,
- **NOTE**: Prince Abhaya (“fearless”) raised Jīvaka after discovering him abandoned <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd8/en/sujato#1.4.5'>Kd 8:1.4.5</a>. In this sutta he appears as an acolyte of Mahāvīra, while in <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn46.56/en/sujato'>SN 46.56</a> he asks the Buddha about Pūraṇa Kassapa. Apart from these details he is unknown in the early texts. Commentaries say he was the son of King Bimbisāra by one of his queens, Padumavatī of Ujjenī, making him half-brother to Ajātasattu. They also say that following Bimbisāra’s death, Abhaya went forth and spoke the verse at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/thag1.26/en/sujato'>Thag 1.26</a>. | Not to be confused with Abhaya the Licchavī of <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an3.74/en/sujato'>AN 3.74</a> and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an4.196/en/sujato'>AN 4.196</a>; nor with the author of <a href='https://suttacentral.net/thag1.98/en/sujato'>Thag 1.98</a>; nor is <a href='https://suttacentral.net/thig2.8/en/sujato'>Thig 2.8</a> addressed to his mother, but that of the nun Abhayā (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/thig2.9/en/sujato'>Thig 2.9</a>).

## mn58:3.6
- **PLI**: ‘bhāseyya nu kho, bhante, tathāgato taṁ vācaṁ yā sā vācā paresaṁ appiyā amanāpā’ti?
- **EN**: ‘Sir, may the Realized One utter speech that is disliked by others?’
- **NOTE**: The Jain doctrine <i lang='pi' translate='no'>anekantavāda</i> (“doctrine of many-sidedness”, “non-categorical doctrine”) posits that in metaphysical propositions, one must conditionally affirm multiple possibilities so as to illuminate the complex, multifacted nature of reality. An aspect of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>anekantavāda</i> is the <i lang='pi' translate='no'>syadvada</i>, which frames all statements with <i lang='pi' translate='no'>syā</i>, “it may be”. This uses the optative tense to conditionally affirm or deny without making absolute statements. The same tense is employed here by Mahāvīra in order to trap the Buddha on the horns of a dilemma (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>ubhatokoṭika</i>). Needless to say, this is not how he is presented in Jain texts.

- **NOTE**: Mahāvīra is quoting from the Buddha at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an8.7/en/sujato#1.7'>AN 8.7:1.7</a> = <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd17/en/sujato#4.7.1'>Kd 17:4.7.1</a>. This dates this sutta to the last years of the Buddha’s life, after the rebellion of Devadatta and the ascension of Ajātasattu, and shortly before Mahāvīra’s passing.
