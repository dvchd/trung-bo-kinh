# Phần 1  (50 đoạn)

## mn52:1.1  `bj11.20, cck13.19, mc2.13, ms10M_27, msdiv17, ndp10.15, nya1, sya13.18, vri13.12`
- **PLI**: Evaṁ me sutaṁ—
- **EN**: So I have heard.
- **NOTE**: This discourse is repeated at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an11.16/en/sujato'>AN 11.16</a>.

## mn52:1.2
- **PLI**: ekaṁ samayaṁ āyasmā ānando vesāliyaṁ viharati beluvagāmake.
- **EN**: At one time Venerable Ānanda was staying near Vesālī in the little village of Beluva.
- **NOTE**: The Buddha spent his last rains retreat here (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn47.9/en/sujato#1.5'>SN 47.9:1.5</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn16/en/sujato#2.22.3'>DN 16:2.22.3</a>). The fact that the Buddha is not mentioned and the setting at Pāṭaliputta suggest that he had already passed away.

## mn52:1.3
- **PLI**: Tena kho pana samayena dasamo gahapati aṭṭhakanāgaro pāṭaliputtaṁ anuppatto hoti kenacideva karaṇīyena.
- **EN**: Now at that time the householder Dasama, a wealthy citizen, had arrived at Pāṭaliputta on some business.
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Dasama</i> means “tenth”, explained by the commentary as meaning he was tenth in line in his family. | <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Aṭṭhakanāgara</i> is usually taken to be the name of a city, but neither the city nor the possessive form <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nāgara</i> appear elsewhere. Since all we know of him is that he was very rich; since the Pali is in the habit of naming people after their prominent quality; and since we find elsewhere a man named after his wealth (Dhaniya at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/snp1.2/en/sujato'>Snp 1.2</a>), perhaps we should take <i lang='pi' translate='no'>aṭṭhaka</i> from <i lang='pi' translate='no'>artha</i> in the sense of “wealthy”, which agrees with the later Sanskrit sense of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nāgara</i> as “prominent citizen”.

## mn52:2.1  `ms10M_28, nya2`
- **PLI**: Atha kho dasamo gahapati aṭṭhakanāgaro yena kukkuṭārāmo yena aññataro bhikkhu tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā taṁ bhikkhuṁ abhivādetvā ekamantaṁ nisīdi. Ekamantaṁ nisinno kho dasamo gahapati aṭṭhakanāgaro taṁ bhikkhuṁ etadavoca:
- **EN**: He went to the Chicken Monastery, approached a certain mendicant, bowed, sat down to one side, and said to him,
- **NOTE**: With the rise of Pāṭaliputta, the “Chicken Monastery” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>kukkuṭārāma</i>) became an important center after the Buddha’s passing. Malalasekera (<em>Dictionary of Pali Proper Names</em>) suggests it was the same monastery later renowned as Aśokārāma. Ānanda stayed there often (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn45.18/en/sujato'>SN 45.18</a>–20, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn47.21/en/sujato'>SN 47.21</a>–3), which explains why Dasama expected to find him there.

## mn52:3.1  `cck13.20, csp1ed10.13, csp2ed10.13, dr13.17, ms10M_29, ms10M_30, msdiv18, nya3, sya13.19`
- **PLI**: Atha kho dasamo gahapati aṭṭhakanāgaro pāṭaliputte taṁ karaṇīyaṁ tīretvā yena vesālī yena beluvagāmako yenāyasmā ānando tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā āyasmantaṁ ānandaṁ abhivādetvā ekamantaṁ nisīdi. Ekamantaṁ nisinno kho dasamo gahapati aṭṭhakanāgaro āyasmantaṁ ānandaṁ etadavoca:

- **NOTE**: Beluva was immediately north of Vesālī, so about a day’s walk from Pāṭaliputta. But Dasama could doubtless afford a carriage.

## mn52:3.2
- **PLI**: “atthi nu kho, bhante ānanda, tena bhagavatā jānatā passatā arahatā sammāsambuddhena ekadhammo akkhāto yattha bhikkhuno appamattassa ātāpino pahitattassa viharato avimuttañceva cittaṁ vimuccati, aparikkhīṇā ca āsavā parikkhayaṁ gacchanti, ananuppattañca anuttaraṁ yogakkhemaṁ anupāpuṇātī”ti?
- **EN**: “Honorable Ānanda, is there one thing that has been rightly explained by the Blessed One—who knows and sees, the perfected one, the fully awakened Buddha—practicing which a diligent, keen, and resolute mendicant’s mind is freed, their defilements are ended, and they arrive at the supreme sanctuary from the yoke?”
- **NOTE**: A number of “one things” are taught throughout the suttas. It seems Dasama wanted a simple summary, but Ānanda wished to challenge him.

## mn52:4.1  `bj11.22, cck13.21, dr13.18, mc2.14, ms10M_33, msdiv19, nya4, sya13.20`
- **PLI**: “Idha, gahapati, bhikkhu vivicceva kāmehi vivicca akusalehi dhammehi savitakkaṁ savicāraṁ vivekajaṁ pītisukhaṁ paṭhamaṁ jhānaṁ upasampajja viharati.
- **EN**: “Householder, it’s when a mendicant, quite secluded from sensual pleasures, secluded from unskillful qualities, enters and remains in the first absorption, which has the rapture and bliss born of seclusion, while placing the mind and keeping it connected.

## mn52:4.2
- **PLI**: So iti paṭisañcikkhati:
- **EN**: Then they reflect:

## mn52:4.3
- **PLI**: ‘idampi kho paṭhamaṁ jhānaṁ abhisaṅkhataṁ abhisañcetayitaṁ.
- **EN**: ‘Even this first absorption is produced by choices and intentions.’
- **NOTE**: This illustrates the development of insight based on absorption. After emerging from the attainment, the meditator reflects on the nature of that experience, realizing that even the most blissful and peaceful of states is still “chosen and intended” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>abhisaṅkhataṁ abhisañcetayitaṁ</i>), i.e. constructed by volition.

## mn52:4.4
- **PLI**: Yaṁ kho pana kiñci abhisaṅkhataṁ abhisañcetayitaṁ tadaniccaṁ nirodhadhamman’ti pajānāti.
- **EN**: They understand: ‘But whatever is produced by choices and intentions is impermanent and liable to cessation.’

## mn52:4.5
- **PLI**: So tattha ṭhito āsavānaṁ khayaṁ pāpuṇāti.
- **EN**: Abiding in that they attain the ending of defilements.
- **NOTE**: Insight must persist to bear fruit.

## mn52:4.6
- **PLI**: No ce āsavānaṁ khayaṁ pāpuṇāti, teneva dhammarāgena tāya dhammanandiyā pañcannaṁ orambhāgiyānaṁ saṁyojanānaṁ parikkhayā opapātiko hoti tattha parinibbāyī anāvattidhammo tasmā lokā.
- **EN**: If they don’t attain the ending of defilements, with the ending of the five lower fetters they’re reborn spontaneously, because of their passion and love for that meditation. They are extinguished there, and are not liable to return from that world.
- **NOTE**: “Passion and love for that meditation” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>teneva dhammarāgena tāya dhammanandiyā</i>); here <i lang='pi' translate='no'>dhamma</i> means the state of meditation, i.e. the jhāna. This is the “higher fetter” of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>rūparāga</i>, the desire for birth in the Brahmā realms of luminous form.

## mn52:4.7
- **PLI**: Ayampi kho, gahapati, tena bhagavatā jānatā passatā arahatā sammāsambuddhena ekadhammo akkhāto, yattha bhikkhuno appamattassa ātāpino pahitattassa viharato avimuttañceva cittaṁ vimuccati, aparikkhīṇā ca āsavā parikkhayaṁ gacchanti, ananuppattañca anuttaraṁ yogakkhemaṁ anupāpuṇāti.
- **EN**: This is one thing that has been rightly explained by the Blessed One—who knows and sees, the perfected one, the fully awakened Buddha—practicing which a diligent, keen, and resolute mendicant’s mind is freed, their defilements are ended, and they arrive at the supreme sanctuary from the yoke.
