## mn49:10.1  `bj10.770, cck12.566, csp1ed9.405, csp2ed9.405, dr12.524, ms9M_1722, msdiv504, ndp9.403, nya10, sya12.595`

- **NOTE**: MS reads <i lang='pi' translate='no'>añño kāyo</i> (“another realm”), but the PTS and BJT reading <i lang='pi' translate='no'>aññe tayo kāyā</i> is supported by the Chinese parallel (MA 78 at T i 548a28). Possibly this change was necessitated by the addition of a fourth realm below.

## mn49:10.2
- **PLI**: tamahaṁ jānāmi passāmi.
- **EN**: but which I know and see.
- **NOTE**: PTS and BJT have <i lang='pi' translate='no'>tyāhaṁ</i> (= <i lang='pi' translate='no'>te ahaṁ</i>), consistent with their plural reading, as opposed to MS’s singular <i lang='pi' translate='no'>tamahaṁ</i>. This shows that this variation, which is not decided by the commentary, was a deliberate editorial choice.

## mn49:10.3
- **PLI**: Atthi kho, brahme, ābhassarā nāma kāyo yato tvaṁ cuto idhūpapanno.
- **EN**: There is the realm named after the gods of sublime luminosity. You passed away from there and were reborn here.
- **NOTE**: Here <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ābhassarā</i> is masculine plural, hence not an adjective of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>kāyo</i> but a reference to the “gods” of that realm. MS is inconsistent in this point, for below we find <i lang='pi' translate='no'>subhakiṇho</i> and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vehapphalo</i> as singular adjectives, where PTS and BJT consistently use the plural form.

## mn49:11.1  `nya11`
- **PLI**: Pathaviṁ kho ahaṁ, brahme, pathavito abhiññāya yāvatā pathaviyā pathavattena ananubhūtaṁ tadabhiññāya pathaviṁ nāpahosiṁ, pathaviyā nāpahosiṁ, pathavito nāpahosiṁ, pathaviṁ meti nāpahosiṁ, pathaviṁ nābhivadiṁ.
- **EN**: Since directly knowing earth as earth, and since directly knowing that which does not fall within the scope of experience characterized by earth, I have not become earth, I have not become in earth, I have not become as earth, I have not become one who thinks ‘earth is mine’, I have not affirmed earth.
- **NOTE**: This difficult passage directly echoes <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn1/en/sujato#3.3'>MN 1:3.3</a>. | <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Anubhūta</i> has its normal sense of “experiences”, “undergoes” (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/thig10.1/en/sujato#8.2'>Thig 10.1:8.2</a>, more commonly <i lang='pi' translate='no'>paccanubhūta</i>, eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn79/en/sujato#8.1'>MN 79:8.1</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn15.1/en/sujato#1.14'>SN 15.1:1.14</a>). Commentary has <i lang='pi' translate='no'>appattaṁ</i>, “not attained” (i.e. not within the scope of absorption). | That which “falls within the scope of experience characterized by earth” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>yāvatā pathaviyā pathavattena ananubhūtaṁ</i>) is sense experience and the four absorptions; that which does not fall in such a scope are the formless attainments and especially Nibbāna. | For <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nāhosiṁ</i> (prefer over MS <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nāpahosiṁ</i>) the commentary glosses “grasps”. The sense is that he does not identify. | For <i lang='pi' translate='no'>abhivadati</i>, compare Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 2.4.14, which says that so long as there is the appearance of duality, one sees, hears, smells, “speaks about” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>abhivadati</i>), “conceives” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>manute</i>), and “cognizes” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>vijānāti</i>) particulars. Notice that this employs <i lang='pi' translate='no'>abhivadati</i> alongside <i lang='pi' translate='no'>manute</i>, just as <i lang='pi' translate='no'>abhivadati</i> in the Pali appears alongside where <i lang='pi' translate='no'>maññati</i> appears in <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn1/en/sujato#3.3'>MN 1:3.3</a>.
