## mn122:20.1  `ms11M_522, msdiv192, ndp11.180, nya20, sya14.242, vri14.158`
- **PLI**: “Na kho, ānanda, arahati sāvako satthāraṁ anubandhituṁ, yadidaṁ suttaṁ geyyaṁ veyyākaraṇaṁ tassa hetu.
- **EN**: “A disciple would not deem it worthwhile to follow the Teacher for the sake of this, namely statements, mixed prose & verse, or discussions.
- **NOTE**: Here we find mentioned only the first three of the normal nine categories (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>aṅga</i>) of the teaching (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn22/en/sujato#10.2'>MN 22:10.2</a>). This may be an earlier list or perhaps simply an abbreviation. Note that the PTS edition reads <i lang='pi' translate='no'>yadidaṁ suttaṁ geyyaṁ veyyākaraṇassa hetu</i>, which would imply only two categories: “for the sake of explanations, namely statements and mixed prose & verse”. A passage at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an5.194/en/sujato#3.3'>AN 5.194:3.3</a> mentions four categories, with the addition of “amazing stories”, but this too might be simply an artifact of abbreviation.

## mn122:20.2
- **PLI**: Taṁ kissa hetu?
- **EN**: Why is that?

## mn122:20.3
- **PLI**: Dīgharattassa hi te, ānanda, dhammā sutā dhātā vacasā paricitā manasānupekkhitā diṭṭhiyā suppaṭividdhā.
- **EN**: Because for a long time you have learned the teachings, remembering them, rehearsing them, mentally scrutinizing them, and penetrating them theoretically.
- **NOTE**: The Buddha shifts to address Ānanda directly. This is in reference to the fact that Ānanda was renowned as the greatest master of scriptural memorization.

## mn122:20.4
- **PLI**: Yā ca kho ayaṁ, ānanda, kathā abhisallekhikā cetovinīvaraṇasappāyā ekantanibbidāya virāgāya nirodhāya upasamāya abhiññāya sambodhāya nibbānāya saṁvattati, seyyathidaṁ—appicchakathā santuṭṭhikathā pavivekakathā asaṁsaggakathā vīriyārambhakathā sīlakathā samādhikathā paññākathā vimuttikathā vimuttiñāṇadassanakathā—evarūpiyā kho, ānanda, kathāya hetu arahati sāvako satthāraṁ anubandhituṁ api paṇujjamāno.
- **EN**: But a disciple would deem it worthwhile to follow the Teacher, even if driven away, for the sake of talk about self-effacement that helps open the heart and leads solely to disillusionment, dispassion, cessation, peace, insight, awakening, and extinguishment. That is, talk about fewness of wishes, contentment, seclusion, aloofness, arousing energy, ethics, immersion, wisdom, freedom, and the knowledge and vision of freedom.
- **NOTE**: That is, rather than simply memorizing more texts, one such as Ānanda should seek encouragement that supports practice.

## mn122:22.1  `cck14.227, dr13.221, ms11M_524, msdiv193, nya22, pts-vp-pli3.116`
- **PLI**: Kathañcānanda, ācariyūpaddavo hoti?
- **EN**: And how is there a peril for the tutor?
- **NOTE**: As is made clear by the example of the spiritual practitioner below, who is said to follow the Buddha, the “tutor” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>ācariya</i>, or <i lang='pi' translate='no'>satthā</i> “teacher” in the next line) and the “pupil” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>antevāsī</i>) here are outside of Buddhism.

## mn122:22.2
- **PLI**: Idhānanda, ekacco satthā vivittaṁ senāsanaṁ bhajati araññaṁ rukkhamūlaṁ pabbataṁ kandaraṁ giriguhaṁ susānaṁ vanapatthaṁ abbhokāsaṁ palālapuñjaṁ.
- **EN**: It’s when some teacher frequents a secluded lodging—a wilderness, the root of a tree, a hill, a ravine, a mountain cave, a charnel ground, a forest, the open air, a heap of straw.

## mn122:22.3
- **PLI**: Tassa tathāvūpakaṭṭhassa viharato anvāvattanti brāhmaṇagahapatikā negamā ceva jānapadā ca.
- **EN**: While meditating withdrawn, they’re visited by a stream of brahmins and householders, and people of town and country.
- **NOTE**: The more a monastic has a reputation for seclusion, the more people seek them out.

## mn122:22.4
- **PLI**: So anvāvattantesu brāhmaṇagahapatikesu negamesu ceva jānapadesu ca mucchaṁ nikāmayati, gedhaṁ āpajjati, āvattati bāhullāya.
- **EN**: When this happens, they enjoy infatuation, fall into greed, and return to indulgence.

## mn122:22.5
- **PLI**: Ayaṁ vuccatānanda, upaddavo ācariyo.
- **EN**: This is said to be the tutor’s peril.
- **NOTE**: Compare the similar phrase at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn16.8/en/sujato#7.7'>SN 16.8:7.7</a>.

## mn122:22.6
- **PLI**: Ācariyūpaddavena avadhiṁsu naṁ pāpakā akusalā dhammā saṅkilesikā ponobbhavikā sadarā dukkhavipākā āyatiṁ jātijarāmaraṇiyā.
- **EN**: They’re ruined by bad, unskillful qualities that are corrupting, leading to future lives, hurtful, resulting in suffering and future rebirth, old age, and death.

## mn122:22.7
- **PLI**: Evaṁ kho, ānanda, ācariyūpaddavo hoti.
- **EN**: That’s how there is a peril for the tutor.
