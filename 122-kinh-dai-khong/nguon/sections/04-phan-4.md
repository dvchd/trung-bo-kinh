## mn122:27.1  `nya27`
- **PLI**: Na vo ahaṁ, ānanda, tathā parakkamissāmi yathā kumbhakāro āmake āmakamatte.
- **EN**: I shall not mollycoddle you like a potter with their damp, unfired pots.
- **NOTE**: This passage seems to mean, “I shall not work you …”, which is the exact opposite of the sense required by the next sentence. A Sanskrit parallel yields the required sense with the verb <i lang='pi' translate='no'>dhanayiṣye</i>, “to treasure, to mollycoddle” (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/san-mu-kd17/san/gbm?lang=en&reference=main&highlight=false#sc493'>Mūlasarvāstivāda Vinaya, Kd 17:493</a>). Perhaps an initial negatory <i lang='pi' translate='no'>a-</i> has been elided in the Pali <i lang='pi' translate='no'>parakkamissāmi</i>, which would yield a similar sense, “I shall not stop working you …”. | The same Sanskrit passage has <i lang='pi' translate='no'>bhājanānā</i> (“pots”) where the Pali has <i lang='pi' translate='no'>āmake āmakamatte</i>, literally “uncooked, a bit damp”.
