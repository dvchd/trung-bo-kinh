- **EN**: Then the Buddha robed up in the morning and, taking his bowl and robe, entered Kapilavatthu for alms.

## mn122:2.2
- **PLI**: Kapilavatthusmiṁ piṇḍāya caritvā pacchābhattaṁ piṇḍapātapaṭikkanto yena kāḷakhemakassa sakkassa vihāro tenupasaṅkami divāvihārāya.
- **EN**: He wandered for alms in Kapilavatthu. After the meal, on his return from almsround, he went to the lodge of the Sakyan Khemaka the Dark for the day’s meditation.
- **NOTE**: The Sakyans Khemaka, known for his dark skin, and Ghaṭā are otherwise unknown. Their “lodges” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>vihāra</i>) were, according to the commentary, buildings offered by them for the use of the Saṅgha inside the Banyan Tree Monastery, rather than being their personal homes.

## mn122:2.3
- **PLI**: Tena kho pana samayena kāḷakhemakassa sakkassa vihāre sambahulāni senāsanāni paññattāni honti.
- **EN**: Now at that time many resting places had been spread out at the lodge of Khemaka the Dark.
- **NOTE**: The fact that they were readily noticeable suggests that the beds were in the open, a detail confirmed by the Tibetan parallel. Bedding and furniture were typically property of the Saṅgha, rather than individuals, so it is an offense to leave them exposed to the elements (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-bu-vb-pc14/en/sujato'>Bu Pc 14</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-bu-vb-pc15/en/sujato'>Bu Pc 15</a>).

## mn122:2.8  `cck14.220, ms11M_510, msdiv186, sya14.235`
- **PLI**: Tena kho pana samayena āyasmā ānando sambahulehi bhikkhūhi saddhiṁ ghaṭāya sakkassa vihāre cīvarakammaṁ karoti.
- **EN**: Now at that time Venerable Ānanda, together with many other mendicants, was making robes at the lodge of the Sakyan Ghaṭā.
- **NOTE**: Some translators (Chalmers, Horner, Siam Rath), as well as the Dictionary of Pali Proper Names, take this name as the masculine form <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ghaṭāya</i>, assuming it is compounded with <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sakkassa</i>; but names are not usually compounded in this way (cf. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>kāḷakhemakassa sakkassa</i> above), and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ghaṭāya</i> does not seem to be attested elsewhere. In his hand-written manuscript, Ñāṇamoḷī had <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ghāṭā</i>, which was followed by Bodhi and Thanissaro. This reads the masculine agent noun “slayer”; but the genitive form would be <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ghātassa</i>, while PTS, BJT, MS, and commentary all have <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ghaṭāya</i> with no variants. Rather, read per text as the feminine genitive of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ghaṭā</i>, suggesting that it was a woman who donated the building. She was perhaps born under the astrological sign known in India as <i lang='pi' translate='no'>kumbha</i> or <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ghaṭa</i> (“water-pot”), and in the west as Aquarius (“water-bearer”).

## mn122:6.2
- **PLI**: sabbanimittānaṁ amanasikārā ajjhattaṁ suññataṁ upasampajja viharituṁ.
- **EN**: to enter and remain in emptiness internally by not focusing on any signs.
- **NOTE**: Normally the meditations on emptiness and on signlessness are presented as separate meditation practices (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn41.7/en/sujato#4.1'>SN 41.7:4.1</a>). The outcome of the signless meditation is a heart that is “empty” of greed, hate, and delusion (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn43/en/sujato#37.1'>MN 43:37.1</a>).

- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Tittha</i>, literally “ford”, is a path to salvation, used as a term for a non-Buddhist “religion”. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Titthakara</i> is a “religious founder” (literally “ford-maker”); <i lang='pi' translate='no'>titthiya</i> is a “monastic of (another) religion” (for example at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-bu-vb-np22/en/sujato#1.2.5'>Bu NP 22:1.2.5</a>); <i lang='pi' translate='no'>titthiyasāvaka</i> is a “disciple of a monastic of (another) religion”.
