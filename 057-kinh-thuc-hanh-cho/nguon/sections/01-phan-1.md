## mn57:1.2
- **PLI**: ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā koliyesu viharati haliddavasanaṁ nāma koliyānaṁ nigamo.
- **EN**: At one time the Buddha was staying in the land of the Koliyans, where they have a town named Haliddavasana.
- **NOTE**: The Koliyans were south-eastern neighbors of the Sakyans, with whom they had close ties in marriage and customs. | Haliddavasana (“yellow-clothes”) is also the scene of <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn46.54/en/sujato'>SN 46.54</a>, which discusses similarities and differences between Buddhism and wanderers when it comes to meditation.

## mn57:2.1  `nya2`
- **PLI**: Atha kho puṇṇo ca koliyaputto govatiko acelo ca seniyo kukkuravatiko yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkamiṁsu; upasaṅkamitvā puṇṇo koliyaputto govatiko bhagavantaṁ abhivādetvā ekamantaṁ nisīdi. Acelo pana seniyo kukkuravatiko bhagavatā saddhiṁ sammodi. Sammodanīyaṁ kathaṁ sāraṇīyaṁ vītisāretvā kukkurova palikujjitvā ekamantaṁ nisīdi.
- **EN**: Then Puṇṇa the Koliyan, a cow votary, and the naked ascetic Seniya, a dog votary, went to the Buddha. Puṇṇa bowed to the Buddha and sat down to one side, while Seniya exchanged greetings and polite conversation with him before sitting down to one side curled up like a dog.
- **NOTE**: The cow votary (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>govatika</i>) is attested in Sanskrit (eg. Mahābhārata 5.97.13). Elsewhere, non-Buddhist texts mention cat votaries (Manu 4.30), snake votaries, fish votaries, those who live like beasts (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>mṛgacaryā</i>), and others, but not, so far as I am aware, dog votaries. The cow and dog observances are found in later Buddhist texts (Yogācārabhūmi 157.10, Mahākarmavibhaṅga 44.19, Śikṣāsamuccaya 332.3, etc.) evidently drawing from this sutta. Thus, while no parallel of this sutta has survived, it was known in northern Buddhist traditions and must have been lost due to a failure of transmission.

## mn57:4.8
- **PLI**: Tassa taṁ govataṁ dīgharattaṁ samattaṁ samādinnaṁ.
- **EN**: For a long time he has undertaken that observance to behave like a cow.
- **NOTE**: From a text of a later date, the cow observance is described thus: “One should bathe in the urine of a cow, maintain himself with milk, and move with the cows, eating after they had eaten. It is known as <i lang='pi' translate='no'>govrata</i>. One would become free from the sins in a month. He would attain the world of cows and reach heaven” (Agni Purāna 292:12–13a, Gangadharan’s translation). This is not a penance for burning off sin, but rather a way of elevating oneself through the sacred nature of the cow. It is not clear, however, if this is the same as the observance in the Buddha’s day.
