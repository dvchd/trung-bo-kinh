- **PLI**: Evaṁ me sutaṁ—
- **EN**: So I have heard.
- **NOTE**: Mindfulness of breathing is taught partially in the Satipaṭṭhānasutta (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn10/en/sujato#4.3'>MN 10:4.3</a> = <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn22/en/sujato#2.3'>DN 22:2.3</a>) and the Kāyagatāsatisutta (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn119/en/sujato#4.2'>MN 119:4.2</a>), and fully in the Rāhulovādasutta (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn62/en/sujato#24.3'>MN 62:24.3</a>), the Girimānandasutta (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/an10.60/en/sujato'>AN 10.60</a>), and throughout the relevant Saṁyutta (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn54.1/en/sujato'>SN 54.1</a>ff.); it is also in the Vinaya (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-bu-vb-pj3/en/sujato#1.3.6'>Bu Pj 3:1.3.6</a>) and in the quasi-Abhidhamma text Paṭisambhidāmagga (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/ps1.3/en/sujato#60.3'>Ps 1.3:60.3</a>). It is regarded in the tradition as the method practiced by the Buddha himself, and has remained the most popular and universal meditation to this day, both in Buddhism and generally.

## mn118:3.1  `cck14.180, csp1ed11.123, csp2ed11.123, dr13.174, ms11M_409, msdiv145, ndp11.143, nya3, pts-vp-pli3.80, sya14.191, vri14.125`
- **PLI**: Tena kho pana samayena bhagavā tadahuposathe pannarase pavāraṇāya puṇṇāya puṇṇamāya rattiyā bhikkhusaṅghaparivuto abbhokāse nisinno hoti.
- **EN**: Now, at that time it was the sabbath—the full moon on the fifteenth day—and the Buddha was sitting surrounded by the Saṅgha of monks for the invitation to admonish.
- **NOTE**: This was the end of the rains retreat, during which mendicants stayed in one place. To conclude the retreat, the mendicants gather to invite admonition from each other, clearing the air of any conflicts or tensions that might have arisen during the retreat. This is done instead of the regular recitation of the Pātimokkha rules. Discussed in detail at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd4/en/sujato'>Kd 4</a>.

## mn118:4.4
- **PLI**: Idhevāhaṁ sāvatthiyaṁ komudiṁ cātumāsiniṁ āgamessāmī”ti.
- **EN**: I will wait here in Sāvatthī for the Komudī full moon of the fourth month.”
- **NOTE**: The “fourth month” of the rainy season (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>vassa</i>), of which the rains retreat (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>vassāvāsa</i>) is normally the first three. When the retreat is finished, mendicants usually stay in the monastery for a while to complete the <i lang='pi' translate='no'>kaṭhina</i> (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd7/en/sujato'>Kd 7</a>), repair their robes, and otherwise make preparations for wandering. During this time, it was customary for mendicants who had spent the rains elsewhere to visit Sāvatthī to see the Buddha (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd4/en/sujato#1.8.1'>Kd 4:1.8.1</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-bu-vb-pj4/en/sujato#1.2.1'>Bu Pj 4:1.2.1</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-bu-vb-pc8/en/sujato#1.2.1'>Bu Pc 8:1.2.1</a>). This month, which falls between the full moons of October and November, is called Kattikā after the constellation Pleiades. The final day of Kattikā is the full moon of Komudī, named after the white lotuses said to bloom at that time, and after the brilliance of the moon in the clear skies following the rains (see eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn79/en/sujato#17.2'>MN 79:17.2</a>).
