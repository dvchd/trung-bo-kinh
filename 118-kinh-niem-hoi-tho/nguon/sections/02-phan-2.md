- **EN**: And how is mindfulness of breathing developed and cultivated to be very fruitful and beneficial?

## mn118:17.1  `nya17`
- **PLI**: Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu araññagato vā rukkhamūlagato vā suññāgāragato vā nisīdati pallaṅkaṁ ābhujitvā ujuṁ kāyaṁ paṇidhāya parimukhaṁ satiṁ upaṭṭhapetvā.
- **EN**: It’s when a mendicant—gone to a wilderness, or to the root of a tree, or to an empty hut—sits down cross-legged, sets their body straight, and brings mindfulness to the present.
- **NOTE**: The situation here—a mendicant gone to the forest—establishes that this practice takes place in the wider context of the Gradual Training. Indeed, this whole sutta can be understood as an expansion of this phrase, mentioned briefly at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn2/en/sujato#67.3'>DN 2:67.3</a>.

## mn118:17.2
- **PLI**: So satova assasati satova passasati.
- **EN**: Just mindful, they breathe in. Mindful, they breathe out.
- **NOTE**: The most fundamental meditation instruction. Notice how the Buddha phrases it: not “concentrate on the breath” as an object, but rather “breathing” as an activity to which one brings mindfulness. The stages of breath meditation are not meant to be done deliberately, but to be observed and understood as the natural process of deepening meditation.

## mn118:18.1  `cck14.183, dr13.177, ms11M_415, nya18`
- **PLI**: Dīghaṁ vā assasanto ‘dīghaṁ assasāmī’ti pajānāti, dīghaṁ vā passasanto ‘dīghaṁ passasāmī’ti pajānāti;
- **EN**: Breathing in heavily they know: ‘I’m breathing in heavily.’ Breathing out heavily they know: ‘I’m breathing out heavily.’
- **NOTE**: In the beginning, the breath is somewhat rough and coarse. The Pali idiom is “long” and “short” breath, but in English we usually say one breathes “heavily” or “lightly”.

## mn118:18.2
- **PLI**: rassaṁ vā assasanto ‘rassaṁ assasāmī’ti pajānāti, rassaṁ vā passasanto ‘rassaṁ passasāmī’ti pajānāti;
- **EN**: Breathing in lightly they know: ‘I’m breathing in lightly.’ Breathing out lightly they know: ‘I’m breathing out lightly.’
- **NOTE**: Over time, the breath becomes more subtle and soft.

## mn118:18.3
- **PLI**: ‘sabbakāyapaṭisaṁvedī assasissāmī’ti sikkhati, ‘sabbakāyapaṭisaṁvedī passasissāmī’ti sikkhati;
- **EN**: They practice like this: ‘I’ll breathe in experiencing the whole body.’ They practice like this: ‘I’ll breathe out experiencing the whole body.’
- **NOTE**: The “whole body” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>sabbakāya</i>) here refers to the breath, marking the fuller and more continuous awareness that arises with tranquility. Some, however, interpret it as the “whole physical body”, broadening awareness to encompass the movement and settling of energies throughout the body.

## mn118:18.4
- **PLI**: ‘passambhayaṁ kāyasaṅkhāraṁ assasissāmī’ti sikkhati, ‘passambhayaṁ kāyasaṅkhāraṁ passasissāmī’ti sikkhati.
- **EN**: They practice like this: ‘I’ll breathe in stilling the physical process.’ They practice like this: ‘I’ll breathe out stilling the physical process.’
- **NOTE**: The “physical process” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>kāyasaṅkhāra</i>) is the breath itself (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn41.6/en/sujato#1.8'>SN 41.6:1.8</a>). This can become so soft as to be imperceptible.

## mn118:19.1  `ms11M_416, nya19, pts-vp-pli3.83, vri14.127`
- **PLI**: ‘Pītipaṭisaṁvedī assasissāmī’ti sikkhati, ‘pītipaṭisaṁvedī passasissāmī’ti sikkhati;
- **EN**: They practice like this: ‘I’ll breathe in experiencing rapture.’ They practice like this: ‘I’ll breathe out experiencing rapture.’
- **NOTE**: “Rapture” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>pīti</i>) is a joyous emotional response to pleasure, usually a spiritual sense of elevation or uplift in meditation. Normally rapture is said to arise before tranquility as part of the process leading toward absorption (see below, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn118/en/sujato#34.1'>MN 118:34.1</a>). In mindfulness of breathing, however, the body typically becomes quite tranquil before rapture arises.

## mn118:19.2
- **PLI**: ‘sukhapaṭisaṁvedī assasissāmī’ti sikkhati, ‘sukhapaṭisaṁvedī passasissāmī’ti sikkhati;
- **EN**: They practice like this: ‘I’ll breathe in experiencing bliss.’ They practice like this: ‘I’ll breathe out experiencing bliss.’
- **NOTE**: The uplifting quality of rapture becomes sublimated to a more subtle, pervasive pleasure or bliss. The meditator refines and stabilizes these positive emotions in their journey toward absorption, which occurs when all necessary factors are fully developed and perfectly balanced in a subtle union.

## mn118:19.3
- **PLI**: ‘cittasaṅkhārapaṭisaṁvedī assasissāmī’ti sikkhati, ‘cittasaṅkhārapaṭisaṁvedī passasissāmī’ti sikkhati;
- **EN**: They practice like this: ‘I’ll breathe in experiencing the mental process.’ They practice like this: ‘I’ll breathe out experiencing the mental process.’
- **NOTE**: “Mental processes” are said to be “perception and feeling” (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn44/en/sujato#14.1'>MN 44:14.1</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn41.6/en/sujato#1.7'>SN 41.6:1.7</a>). The “feelings” include rapture and bliss. “Perception” in meditation includes the “light” (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn128/en/sujato#28.2'>MN 128:28.2</a>) or “forms” (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn77/en/sujato#23.14'>MN 77:23.14</a>) often experienced by meditators at this point. Later texts call this a <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nimitta</i>, a “sign” that the mind is becoming subtle and the hindrances quiet as the meditator nears absorption.

## mn118:19.4
- **PLI**: ‘passambhayaṁ cittasaṅkhāraṁ assasissāmī’ti sikkhati, ‘passambhayaṁ cittasaṅkhāraṁ passasissāmī’ti sikkhati.
- **EN**: They practice like this: ‘I’ll breathe in stilling the mental process.’ They practice like this: ‘I’ll breathe out stilling the mental process.’
- **NOTE**: A paradox of meditation is that the stronger things are, the more subtle they become. In the initial stages of practice, it is common to have overwhelming experiences of rapture, with tears of joy, or dramatic and striking “visions”. Inexperienced meditators often take this to signify a profound realization. With patient mindfulness, however, the feelings and perceptions settle down and become unified, so they are no longer experienced as separate things. Just as the physical breath virtually disappeared, here too the feelings and perceptions can become so subtle as to feel like the simple, normal state of the mind.

## mn118:20.1  `ms11M_417, nya20, sya14.195`
- **PLI**: ‘Cittapaṭisaṁvedī assasissāmī’ti sikkhati, ‘cittapaṭisaṁvedī passasissāmī’ti sikkhati;

## mn118:20.2
- **PLI**: ‘abhippamodayaṁ cittaṁ assasissāmī’ti sikkhati, ‘abhippamodayaṁ cittaṁ passasissāmī’ti sikkhati;
- **EN**: They practice like this: ‘I’ll breathe in gladdening the mind.’ They practice like this: ‘I’ll breathe out gladdening the mind.’
- **NOTE**: This applies especially to the first two absorptions.

## mn118:20.3
- **PLI**: ‘samādahaṁ cittaṁ assasissāmī’ti sikkhati, ‘samādahaṁ cittaṁ passasissāmī’ti sikkhati;
- **EN**: They practice like this: ‘I’ll breathe in immersing the mind in samādhi.’ They practice like this: ‘I’ll breathe out immersing the mind in samādhi.’
- **NOTE**: “Immersion” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>samādhi</i>) is deep meditative stillness. The word conveys the sense of “gathered”, “collected”, with a secondary sense of “ignited”, “illuminated”. It emphasizes both the stability of the mind as well as the unification of all the mental factors present (see <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn44/en/sujato#12.2'>MN 44:12.2</a> and note there).

## mn118:20.4
- **PLI**: ‘vimocayaṁ cittaṁ assasissāmī’ti sikkhati, ‘vimocayaṁ cittaṁ passasissāmī’ti sikkhati.
- **EN**: They practice like this: ‘I’ll breathe in freeing the mind.’ They practice like this: ‘I’ll breathe out freeing the mind.’
- **NOTE**: The mind is liberated from hindrances in absorption. This is a state so very refined it is called the “bliss of awakening” (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn139/en/sujato#9.18'>MN 139:9.18</a>), albeit temporarily (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/an5.149/en/sujato'>AN 5.149</a>).

## mn118:21.1  `bj12.230, ms11M_418, ndp11.146, nya21`
- **PLI**: ‘Aniccānupassī assasissāmī’ti sikkhati, ‘aniccānupassī passasissāmī’ti sikkhati;
- **EN**: They practice like this: ‘I’ll breathe in observing impermanence.’ They practice like this: ‘I’ll breathe out observing impermanence.’
- **NOTE**: The first twelve steps focused on serenity (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>samatha</i>), and now the final four steps shift focus to discernment (or “insight”, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vipassanā</i>). This exemplifies the most common course of practice, where serenity precedes discernment (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/an4.170/en/sujato#3.2'>AN 4.170:3.2</a>). Here, impermanence is contemplated in the context of mindfulness of breathing itself. The entire course of practice has seen the physical energies of the breath become subtle and fade, then the mental processes, then the mind itself is transformed. The meditator reflects on these changes and sees not simply that change happens, but how changes happen due to causes.

## mn118:21.2
- **PLI**: ‘virāgānupassī assasissāmī’ti sikkhati, ‘virāgānupassī passasissāmī’ti sikkhati;
- **EN**: They practice like this: ‘I’ll breathe in observing fading away.’ They practice like this: ‘I’ll breathe out observing fading away.’
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Virāga</i> means both “dispassion” in the sense of letting go desire, and “fading away” in the sense of its gradual disappearance.

## mn118:21.3
- **PLI**: ‘nirodhānupassī assasissāmī’ti sikkhati, ‘nirodhānupassī passasissāmī’ti sikkhati;
- **EN**: They practice like this: ‘I’ll breathe in observing cessation.’ They practice like this: ‘I’ll breathe out observing cessation.’
- **NOTE**: This refers to the permanent ending of the fetters or underlying tendencies.

## mn118:21.4
- **PLI**: ‘paṭinissaggānupassī assasissāmī’ti sikkhati, ‘paṭinissaggānupassī passasissāmī’ti sikkhati.
- **EN**: They practice like this: ‘I’ll breathe in observing letting go.’ They practice like this: ‘I’ll breathe out observing letting go.’
- **NOTE**: This is the full renunciation of all worldly desires that occurs with arahantship (“perfection”). These final four stages illustrating the progress of insight can even be mapped on to the four stages of awakening: the stream-enterer sees that “whatever has a beginning also has an end”; for the once-returner, greed and hate are fading away; for the non-returner, greed and hate have ceased; and for the arahant, all defilements are relinquished.

- **NOTE**: Likewise, the sense here is that the pleasant feelings of breath meditation are part of the full spectrum of feelings contemplated in <i lang='pi' translate='no'>satipaṭṭhāna</i>, which include painful feelings (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn10/en/sujato#32.1'>MN 10:32.1</a>). The material property of “air” is its most obvious dimension, but we experience breath as a complex that includes emotional and cognitive dimensions. As the physical process becomes subtle, feeling becomes apparent as an aspect of the breath that has always been there, but not clearly perceived.
