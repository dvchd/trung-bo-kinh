- **EN**: We will meditate spreading a heart of love to that individual. And with them as a basis, we will meditate spreading a heart like a catskin bag to everyone in the world—abundant, expansive, limitless, free of enmity and ill will.’

## mn21:21.1  `ms9M_769, msdiv233, nya21`
- **PLI**: Imañca tumhe, bhikkhave, kakacūpamaṁ ovādaṁ abhikkhaṇaṁ manasi kareyyātha.
- **EN**: If you frequently reflect on this advice on the simile of the saw,
- **NOTE**: This dramatic passage is quoted at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn28/en/sujato#24.3'>MN 28:24.3</a>, which adopts the name “The Advice on the Simile of the Saw” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>kakacūpamovāda</i>). It became one of the most famous similes in Buddhism. In addition to these two Majjhima suttas and their Chinese parallels, it is cited in canonical texts at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/thag6.12/en/sujato#5.2'>Thag 6.12:5.2</a> and SA 497, and in commentarial texts such as the Sanskrit Abhidharma text Mahāvibhāṣā (T 1545 at T xxvii 190a28), the Pali commentary to <a href='https://suttacentral.net/ud3.3/en/sujato'>Ud 3.3</a>, and the Visuddhimagga chapter on loving-kindness meditation (Vsm 2.9.15). A similar sentiment, moreover, is expressed in the Jain Isibhāsiyāiṁ chapter 34.
