## mn21:2.1  `nya2`
- **PLI**: Tena kho pana samayena āyasmā moḷiyaphagguno bhikkhunīhi saddhiṁ ativelaṁ saṁsaṭṭho viharati.
- **EN**: Now at that time, Venerable Phagguna of the Top-Knot was spending too long mixing closely with some nuns.
- **NOTE**: This monk appears in three discourses, in none of which he distinguishes himself. At <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn12.12/en/sujato'>SN 12.12</a> he persistently attempts to rephrase questions on dependent origination in terms of a self. And at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn12.32/en/sujato'>SN 12.32</a> Venerable Sāriputta gets word that Phagguna had disrobed. According to the commentary, he was called Phagguna “of the Top-Knot” because of the style he wore his hair as a layperson. The name stuck, presumably to distinguish him from the Phagguna of <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an6.56/en/sujato'>AN 6.56</a> (and probably <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn35.83/en/sujato'>SN 35.83</a>) who died in robes as a non-returner after hearing a Dhamma talk.

## mn21:2.2

- **EN**: So much so that

## mn21:2.3
- **PLI**: sace koci bhikkhu āyasmato moḷiyaphaggunassa sammukhā tāsaṁ bhikkhunīnaṁ avaṇṇaṁ bhāsati, tenāyasmā moḷiyaphagguno kupito anattamano adhikaraṇampi karoti.
- **EN**: if any mendicant criticized those nuns in his presence, Phagguna of the Top-Knot got angry and upset, and even instigated disciplinary proceedings.
- **NOTE**: A “disciplinary proceeding” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>adhikaraṇa</i>) is a legal case in Vinaya. A standard set of four is listed at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn104/en/sujato#12.3'>MN 104:12.3</a>. The kind of case is not specified in the text, but the commentary says they accused other mendicants of various offenses and got Vinaya experts to litigate them, making it a “disciplinary issue arising from accusations” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>anuvādādhikaraṇa</i>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd14/en/sujato#14.2.7'>Kd 14:14.2.7</a>).

- **NOTE**: The grammar here is a little tricky. The genitive plays two roles in these passages: specifying the context (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>tava sammukhā</i>, “in your presence”), and as the object of criticism or blows (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>pahāraṁ dadeyya</i> “if they strike” operates above on “those nuns” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>tāsaṁ bhikkhunīnaṁ</i>) and below on “you” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>tava</i>)). Here we would expect both senses to be used, but the text only has <i lang='pi' translate='no'>tava</i> once. I assume that either one instance of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>tava</i> has been lost or it is meant to be distributed.

## mn21:7.4
- **PLI**: ahaṁ kho, bhikkhave, ekāsanabhojanaṁ bhuñjāmi.
- **EN**: ‘I eat my food in one sitting per day.
- **NOTE**: Eating once a day is also encouraged at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn65/en/sujato'>MN 65</a>, while <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn70/en/sujato'>MN 70</a> encourages not eating at night. In both cases, monks objected when asked to follow this practice. Thus all three instances depict the growing influence of recalcitrant monks in the Saṅgha, culminating in the Buddha laying down a formal Vinaya rule against eating at the wrong time (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-bu-vb-pc37/en/sujato'>Bu Pc 37</a>). Per the allowance at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn65/en/sujato#4.1'>MN 65:4.1</a>, the mendicants are not required to eat in one sitting, which remained as an optional ascetic practice.
