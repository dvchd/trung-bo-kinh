## mn21:7.9
- **PLI**: satuppādakaraṇīyameva me, bhikkhave, tesu bhikkhūsu ahosi.
- **EN**: I just had to prompt their mindfulness.
- **NOTE**: This is referring to the early years of the dispensation, before the formal Vinaya code was laid down. The Buddha was reluctant to create a legal code and did so only when it became necessary (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-bu-vb-pj1/en/sujato#3.4.6'>Bu Pj 1:3.4.6</a>). He preferred to lead without judgment and punishment, but by reason, positive encouragement, and inspiring with his own example.

## mn21:9.1  `cck12.237, ms9M_757, msdiv226, nya9, sya12.253`
- **PLI**: Bhūtapubbaṁ, bhikkhave, imissāyeva sāvatthiyā vedehikā nāma gahapatānī ahosi.
- **EN**: Once upon a time, mendicants, right here in Sāvatthī there was a housewife named Vedehikā.
- **NOTE**: The lady Vedehikā is known only here. According to the commentary, her name means either “daughter of the lady from Videha”, or (implausibly) “wise one”. However, the much earlier Arthaśāstra treats <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vaidehika</i> either as a “merchant” (2.21.7) or as a caste name (3.7.32). This story paints a rare portrait of domestic life.

## mn21:9.2

- **EN**: She had this good reputation:

## mn21:9.3
- **PLI**: ‘soratā vedehikā gahapatānī, nivātā vedehikā gahapatānī, upasantā vedehikā gahapatānī’ti.
- **EN**: ‘The housewife Vedehikā is gentle, placid, and calm.’
- **NOTE**: The same set of three in a similar context occur at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an6.60/en/sujato'>AN 6.60</a>.

## mn21:9.4
- **PLI**: Vedehikāya kho pana, bhikkhave, gahapatāniyā kāḷī nāma dāsī ahosi dakkhā analasā susaṁvihitakammantā.
- **EN**: Now, Vedehikā had a bonded maid named Kāḷī who was deft, tireless, and well-organized in her work.
- **NOTE**: According to ancient Indian law (Arthaśāstra 3.13), slaves may be born such, be captured in war, or a person in a time of trouble may bind themselves in service for a fee. Non-Aryans (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>mleccha</i>) may indenture their children, but this is forbidden for Aryans. Such bondservants were protected against cruelty, sexual abuse, and unfair work. After earning back the fee of their indenture they were freed, retaining their original inheritance and status. | The maid’s name means “black” (compare the Kāḷī at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/thag2.16/en/sujato#1.1'>Thag 2.16:1.1</a> who “looks like a crow”). This is a persistent pattern in early Pali. The only other named slaves are Disā (“foe”, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn3/en/sujato#1.16.1'>DN 3:1.16.1</a>), her son Kaṇha (“black”, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn3/en/sujato#1.16.1'>DN 3:1.16.1</a>), and Kāka (“crow”, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd8/en/sujato#1.26.6'>Kd 8:1.26.6</a>). These people were evidently non-Aryans descended from the native peoples of India, perhaps Tamils or other tribal groups.
