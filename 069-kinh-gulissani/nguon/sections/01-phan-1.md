## mn69:2.1  `nya2`
- **PLI**: Tena kho pana samayena goliyāni nāma bhikkhu āraññiko padasamācāro saṅghamajjhe osaṭo hoti kenacideva karaṇīyena.
- **EN**: Now at that time a wilderness mendicant of boorish behavior named Gulissāni had come down to the midst of the Saṅgha on some business.
- **NOTE**: Gulissāni (variant Goliyāni) is only known here. | The term <i lang='pi' translate='no'>padasamācaro</i> (Mahāsaṅgīti editions) or <i lang='pi' translate='no'>padarasamācāro</i> (Buddha Jayanthi, PTS, and Thai editions) is unique and uncertain. The commentary does not gloss it linguistically, but explains, “of weak conduct, of coarse conduct”. The reading <i lang='pi' translate='no'>padara</i> is preferred in Cone’s <em>Dictionary of Pali</em> in the sense “cleft”. But I propose we read with Sanskrit <i lang='pi' translate='no'>padra</i>, “village”. This explains the confusion in Pali readings, as the Sanskrit is midway between <i lang='pi' translate='no'>pada</i> and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>padara</i>. The point is that he was supposed to be a forest monk, yet he behaved “boorishly” as if he were from the village.

- **EN**: There Venerable Sāriputta spoke to the mendicants about Gulissāni:

## mn69:6.1  `bj11.230, dr13.197, ms10M_471, ndp10.158, nya6, pts-vp-pli1.470, sya13.216`

- **EN**: A wilderness monk who has come to stay in the Saṅgha shouldn’t have relations with families before or after the meal.
